Passport validity and the 6-month rule

Many airlines and some border checks expect a passport to stay valid for six months beyond your arrival date, and the airline check is the one that bites first: it can happen at the gate, before immigration ever sees you. The six-month figure is a widely used norm, a floor some destinations sit above or below, so verify with your airline and the destination's official sources before you book travel.

The organizer move is the same either way. Open the passport, read the expiry date, write it into row 1 and into the renewal calendar below. If the remaining runway looks short, start the renewal before anything else on the list, because a passport renewal sets the pace for every step that needs the passport. How long a renewal takes depends on where you are - check the official issuer, not a forum.

Apostille vs legalization, in 3 rows

Both are ways a foreign official can trust your document. Which route applies depends on the countries involved, so check the receiving country's official guidance and the issuing authority's own list before you pay any intermediary.

ApostilleLegalization
What it isOne certificate that certifies the signature on a documentA chain of certifications, often ending at a consulate
Where it is the routeBetween countries that signed the Hague Apostille ConventionWherever the receiving country's rules point outside that route
Where it happensA designated authority in the document's home countryThe same chain, ending at the destination country's consulate or embassy

Those three rows are the whole difference. Everything beyond them - which documents qualify, what it costs, where to file - is specific to the document and the countries on your route, so treat any timeline or price you read online as a question to verify, not an answer.

The copies strategy: 4 rules of thumb

Four rules of thumb from moves done the hard way. They are opinions and habits, not requirements.

  1. Scan every document into one cloud folder, then test the login from a different device before departure day. A backup you cannot reach is a wish, not a backup.
  2. Carry paper copies in two places: one set with you, one set in a different bag. Originals ride in carry-on, never in checked luggage or shipping boxes.
  3. Leave one set with a trusted person at home, and write down what they hold. Next year you will not remember whether the birth certificate is with your brother or in the safe.
  4. Rescan anything that changes: renewed passport, new stamp, new permit. Delete the stale scan. An outdated copy handed to a clerk is worse than no copy, because you are the one who has to untangle it.
