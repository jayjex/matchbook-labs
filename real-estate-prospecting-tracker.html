How the columns work

The follow-up cadence: day 0, 3, 10, 21

The last column in the log points at this schedule. Count days from the first contact, and write the date for the next touch in the row before you move on to the next lead.

WhenWhat
Day 0: callThe first conversation, live or as a voicemail. Keep it short: who you are, why you're calling, one question that starts a real conversation. Log what you said while it's fresh, because the day-3 text has to match it.
Day 3: textTwo sentences. Reference something from the call, then one soft question or offer to help. No new pitch, no attachment, nothing that needs more than a one-word reply.
Day 10: value-dropSend something useful with no ask attached: a note on recent sales near their address, a prep checklist, an answer to a question they raised. This is the touch that takes the most effort, which is why it's easy to skip, and it's also the one that gives the person a reason to write back.
Day 21: close-outOne last short message: door stays open, here's how to reach me. Then mark the row Finished in pencil and stop spending attention on it. If they come back later, start a fresh row so the history stays readable.

Four touches per lead, written down before you start. The log column does the tracking; you only have to do what the date says. Job hunting runs the same cadence: the job application tracker puts stages and follow-up dates on one page.

What the source letters mean

  1. F — FSBO. "For sale by owner." The homeowner is selling without an agent. They're already in the market, which makes them easy to find, and they're handling every part of the sale themselves, which is exactly where an honest offer of help lands.
  2. E — Expired. A listing that came off the market unsold after its agreement ended. The seller was ready to move recently and may still be. Approach with what went wrong and what you'd do differently, not with a repeat of the last pitch they heard.
  3. R — Referral. Someone you know sent the lead to you by name. Warmest channel there is, and the most fragile: whoever referred you is lending you their reputation, so report back to them after the first conversation.
  4. O — Online. The lead came from a portal form, your website, or a social message. They filled in a box and moved on, so expect to earn the conversation: respond fast, drop anything that sounds scripted, and let the cadence above do its work.
  5. S — Sphere. People who already know you: past clients, neighbors, friends, family, the regulars at your coffee spot. The easiest conversations you'll ever have and the ones most often forgotten. A row in this log counts for sphere leads too, same as any other source.

Circling the source every time costs two seconds and buys you a pattern: after a few weeks you can see which letters fill the page and which ones you keep avoiding. That's the point of the column.

Weekly goals: target vs actual

Fill the target column on Monday morning, in ink. Fill the actual column on Friday, also in ink, even when the week went badly: the gap between the two columns is the useful part, and you can only see it if both numbers are honest.
