The order is deliberate. Courses sit first because every other section references them: grid cells hold course names, grade rows repeat them. The dates box sits above the weekly grid because a term deadline outlives any single week. The grade table comes last because it fills slowest, one row at a time when results post. Six rows is a cap, not a quota; a lighter term should leave rows empty.

Four tips for running the sheet

Opinions from use, not science - your term, your call.

  1. Exam dates go in the box the day syllabi land. Every course hands out its exam dates in week one. Copying them in before you leave the room takes thirty seconds; the classic alternative is discovering in week nine that two exams share a morning. The box has six lines for a reason: courses overlap, and so do their exams.
  2. Fill the professor column completely, office hours included. The room number is on the roster, but office hours live on the syllabus, and midterm season is the worst time to go hunting for them. Write them in the row while the syllabus is already open.
  3. Write a target grade now; fill the grade column only when the school posts it. A target is a decision about where your study hours go, made early enough to matter. It is a write-in, not a prediction. The grade column staying empty for weeks is the sheet working, not failing.
  4. Give the sheet five minutes a week. Sunday night works for most people. Scan the dates box against the calendar, check nothing new landed, move on. A term sheet that only gets looked at during panic week is a poster, not a planner.
