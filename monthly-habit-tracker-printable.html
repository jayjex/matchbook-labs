4 tips for a month that stays readable

Our opinions from running these sheets, no research behind them, take or leave:

  1. Two habits beat eight. The sheet has six rows, and filling all six is the fastest way to a messy grid. Two rows fully ticked tell you more than eight rows half ticked, so we would rather see a short list finished than a long list abandoned. Add a third row only after a full month at two.
  2. Read "missed most" before "best streak". The best-streak line feels good, but the missed-most line is the one that changes next month. Whatever you missed most either matters less than you thought (drop it) or is set at the wrong bar (shrink it). The recap box puts both on one page on purpose.
  3. One sheet per month, always fresh. Reusing a half-finished sheet across two months turns the circles into mush. Print a new one, write the new month, carry over only the keep/drop answers from the recap. Ten seconds of setup, and every sheet stays a clean record.
  4. Give weekend habits their own rule. If a habit runs Monday to Friday, write that on the line ("gym, Mon to Fri") and dash through the weekend circles in advance. Pre-marked rest circles never read as a miss when you scan the row later.

Common questions

How do I fill the circles?

Shade them solid, tick them, or write the count in them (minutes, glasses, pages). The one convention worth keeping: a filled circle means the habit happened by the rule written on its line, and an empty circle means it did not. Partial effort goes in the notes lines, not in the circle.

What about months with fewer than 31 days?

Cross out the unused circles at the end of the grid on day one, so an empty circle at day 30 in February never reads as a miss. The month line at the top is write-in, so the same sheet works for every month of the year.

Six rows are not enough for my list.

Print a second sheet rather than cramming two habits on one line; separate rows keep the circles honest. Or track longer runs differently: the habit streak tracker covers one habit across 30 days with streak counters, and the 66-day habit tracker runs three habits for about two months per page.

Does it print well in black and white?

Yes. The sheet is grayscale by design: gray examples, gray day numbers, thin rules. It reads the same on a cheap laser printer as on screen.
