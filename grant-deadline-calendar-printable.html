"Who has it" is the column to watch: a reference letter, a budget sign-off, a team bio living in someone else's inbox. A blank there is the row to chase first.

Reminder notes

One habit, opinion: work backwards from the due date. If the portal closes on the 28th, the finished draft belongs around the 21st and the last material around the 14th. Write the backwards dates here, not the official one alone.

4 opinion tips for a grant deadline calendar

Opinions, labeled as such. These held up for us; take or leave:

  1. Set the reminder two weeks before the due date. (Opinion.) Two weeks absorbs the portal account you still have to create, the letter someone owes you, and one failed upload. One week absorbs one of the three.
  2. Write the time and time zone next to every date. (Opinion.) "March 3" and "March 3, 17:00 CET" are different deadlines, and the funder's page decides which one is real.
  3. Enter the deadline the day you hear about the program. (Opinion.) Thirty seconds, while the funder name and program page are still open in a tab. "I'll add it later" is how a row stays empty until the date has passed.
  4. Review the sheet on a fixed day each week. (Opinion.) Move the circles, chase the blank "who has it" cells, cross out programs that closed. A calendar read once a week does its job; one read the night before a deadline is decoration.

What this page is

A tracking sheet. It records dates, materials and stages; it doesn't evaluate your chances, doesn't promise funding, and doesn't pick programs for you. Deadlines and requirements come from each funder's own page, and programs change them often enough that the sheet needs re-checking against the source before you plan around a date.
