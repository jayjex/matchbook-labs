Free Monthly Bill Review Checklist (Two Pages)

A bill review is a ten-minute appointment with your own statements: once a month, you list what you pay, mark each bill keep or cancel, and check whether last month's list still matches what you used. Most people run that review in their head, where it turns into a vague intention. These two printable pages put it on paper. Page 1 is a ten-row table - each row gets a bill name written in, an amount written in, and two circles: one for cancel (Y/N), one for keep (Y/N). Page 2 is a usage-check box and an annual-renewals box and nothing else. Print it, write on it, reprint it next month. Below the sheet sits a $16 checklist for a different kind of audit entirely - smart-contract code, not your bills.

How to use this page: print at 100% scale - both pages come out on two sheets. Wait until this month's statements exist, then do page 1 in one sitting, about ten minutes: write each bill on its own row with its amount, and circle cancel or keep for each. If a row takes more than a few seconds, that hesitation is the finding - park it and move on. Page 2 takes another five minutes and works best right after page 1, while the cancel circles are still fresh.

4 opinions on running this monthly review

  1. Cancel one bill every month. (Opinion.) A review that ends with every circle on keep took inventory. One deliberate cancel per month keeps the ritual honest, and it forces the question that matters: is there anything here I'm paying for out of habit? Some months the honest answer is no - fine, cancel nothing and write that down. The asking every month is the habit; the cancels follow when the evidence lines up.
  2. Judge usage, not intentions. (Opinion.) The keep circle wants to be circled for the version of you who will definitely start using the language app again. Ask instead: did I open this since last month's review? Last month's behavior is the only evidence on the table. The usage-check box exists to hold that evidence, and a bill that shows up there twice in a row has made its own case.
  3. Do it the day the last statement lands. (Opinion.) A review without a fixed date slides to never, because no month announces itself as review month. Pick the day your latest statement cycle closes and keep it - the sheet on page 1 has a month line for exactly this. Same day every month also means the amounts you compare across sheets are the same billing cycle length, which keeps the comparison clean.
  4. Reprint fresh each month and keep the old sheets. (Opinion.) Editing one sheet forever turns the review into a scribble. A fresh print each month, with the previous sheet filed next to it, shows the drift you'd never notice in the moment: which keep circles survived three months running, whether last month's cancel circle ever got acted on, how the annual-renewals box filled in as the year went around.
