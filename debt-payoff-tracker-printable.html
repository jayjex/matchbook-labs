4 opinions on keeping this sheet alive

  1. Pick one payoff order and stay with it. (Opinion, and not a vote for either order.) Smallest balance first, highest rate first - both are just ways of numbering the same eight rows, and the sheet records either one. What kills trackers is switching orders halfway, because the bar stops meaning anything. Choose once, write the order in the margins if it helps, and let it ride until a row is gone.
  2. Take the balances from today's statements, not from memory. (Opinion.) Memory rounds balances down - the number you remember is always a few weeks old and always a little too optimistic. Eight rows copied straight off the current statements take ten minutes, and they're the only version of the sheet that means anything.
  3. Fill a bar segment the day a payment clears. (Opinion.) The bar is the part of the sheet that survives week three, but only if it gets filled while the payment is fresh. Batch-filling the bar at month end turns it into bookkeeping; filling it the day the payment lands is the whole reward, and it costs fifteen seconds.
  4. When the log fills, print a fresh page instead of squeezing. (Opinion.) Six log rows and eight bar segments run out eventually, and that's the point of a printable. Don't write in the margins or cross out old rows - print a clean copy, carry the balances over from the last sheet, and keep the filled one as the record.
