Five record-keeping tips

  1. Write it the day it happens. A note written on Tuesday tells you what happened Tuesday. The same note written on Sunday is what you remember on Sunday, and memory keeps the interesting parts and drops the rest.
  2. One page, one place. Chicken notes, seed receipts, the fence repair, the feed total, all on the same sheet in the same spot. A system split across three places trains you to check zero of them.
  3. Date everything, even the seeds. The day a packet went in the ground and the day the compost got turned are the two numbers you'll want next spring. Dates turn loose notes into a timeline.
  4. Track only what you'll act on. If a number will never change what you do, leave the column out. Most weeks, a dozen chores and one line of notes is a complete record.
  5. Keep the finished sheets. What went into bed 2, when the beans were done, which month the feed bill spiked, it's all on old sheets. Tossing them resets your memory to zero every January; a drawer of them is next year's plan already written.

Print tips

  • One sheet per week. Print a stack at the start of the season and keep them clipped together.
  • Print at 100% scale on A4 or US Letter. Grayscale is fine; the layout is lines and boxes, no ink-heavy fills.
  • Laminate one copy and use a wet-erase marker for the chore column if you'd rather not print weekly. Keep a paper copy for the notes, which are worth keeping.
  • Prefer typing? Copy the tables into a spreadsheet; the columns transfer as-is.
