Etsy Tags and Titles Guide

A listing's title and its 13 tags are two fields that should say the same thing. This page gives you a 4-row structure for the title, a write-in tag bank with 6 slots for multi-word phrases, and four opinions, labeled as opinions. Print the tag bank, fill it in for one listing, and you have both fields drafted in ten minutes.

How to use it: fill the tag bank first, then build the title from the table using your best bank phrase as the lead. The tips at the end are opinions and marked that way; the table and the bank are craft you can check against your own listing.

Title structure (4 slots)

One workable order for a product title. Fill the slots with words true of the item in front of you.

SlotWhat goes thereKeep it honest by
1. Lead wordsThe exact phrase a buyer would type, placed first. No prefixes like "custom" or "sale" in front of it.Reading it aloud: does it start with the thing, or with a word about the seller?
2. SpecificsMaterial, size, color, audience, or occasion. The checkable nouns a buyer uses to narrow a search.Only writing details the photos could prove.
3. Plain nameWhat the item is, in ordinary words. This is the fallback phrase buyers use when they do not know the fancy term.Writing the word you would say to a neighbor, not the word from a mood board.
4. The cutEverything left over: filler adjectives, repeated words, decoration. This slot's job is to leave.Cutting until every remaining word earns a search or a buyer.

Titles on marketplaces have length limits, and limits change. Check the current limit in the official help docs before you pack a title to its edge; the structure above works at any length.

Tag bank (write-in, 6 slots)

Write one multi-word phrase per line, two words minimum, one word wasted. Each line has a different job so the six phrases end up covering six intents instead of one intent six ways.

Tags for one listing: ______________________

  1. _________________________________________ what the item is, named plainly
  2. _________________________________________ who it is for
  3. _________________________________________ when it gets bought or given
  4. _________________________________________ what it is made of
  5. _________________________________________ its style, in words buyers use
  6. _________________________________________ a phrase one specific buyer would type

Line 6 is the one most listings skip. Think of the last message a real customer sent you and write the phrase they used.

4 opinion tips

  1. A tag is a search query, not a decoration. (Opinion.) I fill every tag slot as if I were typing the words into a search box myself, because that is what the field is for. Phrases that would never get typed do not get slots.
  2. The first words of a title do the heaviest lifting. (Opinion.) Buyers scan openings, and the opening is the part I am most careful with. Whatever slot 2 through 4 lose to trimming, the lead phrase keeps.
  3. Specific beats clever. (Opinion.) A pun can be fun on the product and dead in search. When a listing has room for one, the search phrase wins the field and the joke goes in the description.
  4. Borrow words from buyers, not from a thesaurus. (Opinion.) Reviews, questions, and messages hand you the exact vocabulary your market already types. I mine those before I invent anything.
