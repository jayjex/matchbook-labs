4 opinions on running this walk

  1. Walk it before the boxes arrive. (Opinion.) The kit's own instruction is to do this before anything moves in, and it holds up: an empty room shows every wall, floor and window in one look, while a room stacked with boxes makes each condition line a guess. Twenty minutes on day one buys every line on page 1 for free.
  2. Date-stamp the photos. (Opinion.) Turn the timestamp on before the walk, shoot each room wide first and then close up, and keep the shots in a folder named for the move. The kit pairs every inspection line with a photo-taken box for this reason: a condition note plus a dated photo of the same spot is a much stronger pair than either one alone, and the date is what separates move-in pictures from everything your phone has taken since.
  3. Photograph the meter displays, don't just copy the digits. (Opinion.) Transcribing a meter by hand is how one wrong digit turns into a bill that needs untangling later. The kit's move-in page tells you to photograph the meter readings too, and that's the habit worth keeping: the written number on the sheet is for quick checking, the photo is the record.
  4. Leave page 2 signed by everyone who walked the place. (Opinion.) The kit's inspection pages carry an inspection date and a who-was-present line, and the reason is plain: two people looking at the same sheet on day one agree on what it says. Put agreements in writing, as the kit's own about page puts it, and a signed copy each side is the simplest version of that.
