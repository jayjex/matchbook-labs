Fantasy Football Draft Strategy Guide

A draft day guide you can write on: a table of what each round stretch decides, how auction and snake drafts differ, and four opinions we hold after printing and using our own draft kits. Every tip here is about organizing decisions, and every opinion is labeled as ours. No player names, no ranking numbers, no predictions of how any pick will turn out.

How to use this guide: read the round table before draft night, then write your own plan into the same stretches (print the page or copy the table onto paper). The table gives you craft habits, not answers; which position you target in each stretch is your call and your league's settings decide the slots. What each round stretch decides

Drafts are long, and a plan for all of them fits in five rows. The stretches below follow a 12-team, 15-round snake format; adjust the boundaries to your league size. The tips are generic craft habits, so they hold for any scoring system and any site you draft on.

StretchWhat it decidesCraft tips
Early rounds (roughly 1-4)The anchors of your starting lineup. These picks feed the slots that stay in your lineup every week.Decide before pick one which positions your first four picks must fill, and in what order. Check the bye column on every pick; two starters sharing a bye week is a problem you can only fix before the pick.
Middle rounds (roughly 5-10)Depth. This stretch fills the rest of the starters and sets the floor of your roster.Count the open starter slots on your roster grid before each pick and take the position with the fewest planned names. If a run starts on one position, decide in advance whether you'd join it or let it pass.
Late rounds (roughly 11-13)Backup plans and the last bench jobs.Spend these on the slots still blank on the roster grid, not on more depth at a position you've already covered. One backup per starter is plenty; the rest of the picks can chase upside.
Kickers and team defensesTwo positions with their own rows on every cheat sheet, and their own timing.Give them real lines on the cheat sheet like any other position, but hold the picks until the final rounds. In our kits we pencil both into the last stretch, where the room starts grabbing them anyway.
BenchEvery bench slot is a weekly job: someone to check on, start on a bye, or drop.Size the bench before the draft; six bench slots on our 17-slot roster grid means six jobs to manage during the season. Fill them with backups you could start without a headache, not with every name you like.

Snake format? Your slot flips each round (last in round 1 drafts first in round 2), so plan by round number, not overall pick slot. The free draft board template prints all 180 pick cells with the round numbers down the side.

Auction vs snake, in three rows

Both formats draft the same roster; the difference is how each name leaves the board. The first two rows are mechanics, the third is ours.

Snake draftAuction draft
How picks workPick order flips each round, and the wall board tracks every cell. No money, no bids.Every manager gets the same budget (most leagues use $200) and bids on each player. The tracker's running total is your budget check.

Four opinions, labeled as ours

1. Draft your fun team last. (Opinion.) Everyone wants a player or two from the team they root for, the college favorite, the name the whole league teases them about. Take those picks in the final rounds, after the slots that carry your lineup are filled. The fun team assembles itself from leftovers; the competitive team needs the early picks.

2. Write the structure before the rankings. (Opinion.) Team count, round count, roster slots, budget: those numbers live in your league settings and never change mid-draft, while rankings change every week. Put the structure on the sheet first and the names second. Every blank box in our kit is that principle printed on paper.

3. Keep the paper next to the laptop all season. (Opinion.) The trade evaluator and waiver tracker earn their keep after draft day, and a sheet in a drawer gets used zero times. One stack of pages by the laptop covers the whole season: the grid for your roster, the trade card for offers, the waiver rows for adds.

4. Color-code the board if the room moves faster than your handwriting. (Opinion.) One cell per pick, first name only, and a marker color per team when you need to scan fast. Our kit's color guide page lists every team by name with a swatch and hex value, so the markers on your desk can match the sheet.

One honest note: this is strategy framing, not prediction. Everything above is a way to organize decisions you'll make under time pressure. It can't tell you what any manager in your room will do, which name falls to which slot, or how any player's season goes. A draft plan that promises outcomes is selling something. What a plan can do is turn each pick into a three-second read from a sheet you filled out the night before, which is the whole job of the table and the kit below.
