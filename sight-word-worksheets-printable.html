How to run a practice session (5 steps):

1. A grown-up writes one word in the first blank word box of the practice table. Write it small and neat, it stays the model for the row.

2. The kid copies the word into the trace box, once, then writes it again on the second line if there is room.

3. Say the word out loud, then use it in a sentence. The kid writes one sentence on the line. Invented spelling is fine at this stage.

4. Fill in the next row with the next word, or stop and finish the rest of the sheet another day.

5. After the table, turn to page 2 for the word hunt: find all 5 words in the grid, left to right, and tick them off the list.

4 tips for kindergarten word practice

Opinions from watching early readers work through sheets like these, not a teaching study:

  1. (Opinion.) Keep it to 5 words per week. A kindergartner who meets 5 words gets enough repeats to own them by Friday. Twenty words on Monday is a pile of stickers for the grown-up and a blur for the kid.
  2. (Opinion.) Trace before sentences, not after. The hand needs a warm-up the same as anything else. Copying the word twice in the trace box takes twenty seconds and saves the sentence line from turning into a stall.
  3. (Opinion.) Read the word list out loud before the hunt. Kids who scan the grid cold look for letters at random. Kids who heard the 5 words first listen for a beginning letter, which is a plan, not a guess.
  4. (Opinion.) Keep finished sheets in one folder. Flip back to last week's rows at the start of the next session. The kid sees old words read at a glance, which is the whole point of sight words, and the folder quietly becomes a record of what to retire.
