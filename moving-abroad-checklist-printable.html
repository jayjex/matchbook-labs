Moving Abroad Checklist (Free Printable): Two Pages for the Last 90 Days and the First Week

The last ninety days before a move abroad run on paperwork that moves at other people's speed: forms that wait on signatures, documents that wait on appointments, letters that wait on the mail. This moving abroad checklist is a free printable two-page set for that window. Page one is a 90-days-out table with 8 write-in task rows, each with a done circle and a notes column. Page two covers the landing side: a first-week box with 5 lines and a paperwork folder box with 3. It is a relocation checklist built for printing, not an app to log into. If you want the document records themselves, the expat document checklist printable tracks names, expiry dates, and copies; the 12-month moving abroad checklist starts the timeline earlier, and the cost checklist handles the money.

How to use this page: print both pages at 100% scale. On page one, one row per task: write what needs doing, circle Done when it is finished, and use the notes column for whatever the row waits on, like the office you mailed the form to or the date you expect an answer. Nothing on the sheet names the tasks, because the ones that matter depend on your route; write your own from whatever your research turns up. On page two, use the first-week lines for the handful of things arrival-day-you will not remember on its own, and the folder box to decide where the paper lives before it piles up. Requirements vary by country - check official government sources, and treat these pages as a place to organize what you find, not a source itself.

4 habits, all opinions

  1. Scan every document twice. (Opinion.) Once when the paper arrives, once the week you pack. The second pass catches the scan that saved blank, the file named scan_final_final, and the document that changed since the first pass.
  2. Put the rows that wait on other people first. (Opinion.) Packing waits for nobody but you; paperwork queues do not care about your flight date. Start with whatever needs an office, a signature, or the mail.
  3. Stick the printed page where you pass it daily. (Opinion.) A checklist in a drawer gets read twice: the day it is printed and never. The fridge door checks more rows than a folder does.
  4. Leave the last week to the first-week box. (Opinion.) Do not start a new task in the final days of a move; spend them on boxes and on filling the 5 lines, so the plan exists before the jet lag does.

Check your destination. What any task on this list involves (forms, offices, translations, or nothing at all) depends on the countries on your route, and those requirements change. The answer lives with the destination's official government sources, not on a printable. This sheet records what you find; it does not decide it.
