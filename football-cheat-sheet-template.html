How to Fill Out a Cheat Sheet the Night Before

Build your own tiers

Ranking 200 players is a chore and goes stale the moment one injury report drops. What actually wins drafts is tiering: group players so that when pick 7 and pick 9 are on the clock, you can see at a glance who falls into your current tier. Write tiers as horizontal bands, roughly 5 to 8 players each. When a tier empties, that is your signal to reach one spot early for a positional need.

Only write names you can read fast

The cheat sheet gets 3 to 5 seconds of attention per pick. Keep the top of the sheet clean: 60 to 80 names total, biggest role first, and a bye-week box next to each line so you can dodge drafting three receivers who all sit in week 9. Cross names off as they get picked. A messy sheet you can scan beats a complete one you have to hunt through.

Mark your reaches and fades

Circle two or three players you would take a round early, and strike players you keep ending up with but never want. On draft day, fatigue pushes everyone toward the same mid-round names. Your pre-draft notes are the only defense.

Print two boards

One board tracks the picks; one lives next to your laptop for your own roster plan. Trying to do both jobs on a single sheet ends with missed picks. If you are in an auction league, the second board becomes a bid tracker: write each player's winning bid so you can read the market as the auction goes on.
