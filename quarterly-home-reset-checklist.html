The quarterly reset checklist

20 items in six zones, ordered so you can start anywhere and still finish. Kitchens and bedrooms carry the most steps because that is where clutter and postponed decisions pile up fastest. Every item works the same in an apartment or a house; nothing here assumes a yard, a garage, or any particular climate.

Kitchen (4)

  • ☐ Counters cleared and wiped; daily-use appliances stay out, everything else goes back in a cabinet
  • ☐ Fridge and freezer swept: expired food out, shelves wiped, a use-up-first list on the door
  • ☐ One appliance deep-cleaned end to end: oven, microwave, or coffee maker
  • ☐ Sink scrubbed, drains flushed with hot water, trash bin washed and dried

Entry (3)

  • ☐ Everything that does not belong returned to its real home: shoes, mail, packages, purchases waiting to go back
  • ☐ Hooks and drop zone reset: one hook per person, the rest empty
  • ☐ Floor and surfaces clear enough that nothing blocks the door

Living areas (3)

  • ☐ Flat surfaces cleared and wiped; each item kept, filed, donated, or tossed
  • ☐ Cables, remotes, and orphan chargers sorted; dead ones gone
  • ☐ Rug and upholstery vacuumed, under the cushions included

Bedrooms (4)

  • ☐ The clothes chair emptied: laundry run, everything hung or folded, nothing re-deposited
  • ☐ Closet fast pass: anything unworn since last quarter goes in a donate bag
  • ☐ Under-bed checked for dust, lost items, and boxes pushed straight
  • ☐ Bedding stripped and washed; mattress rotated

Bath (3)

  • ☐ Expired medicine, makeup, and sunscreen bagged for disposal per your local rules
  • ☐ Towels and bathmats washed; extras thinned to two per person
  • ☐ Sink, tub, and toilet scrubbed; grout and caulk lines spot-checked

Utility (3)

  • ☐ Air filter checked and replaced if gray; dryer lint path brushed
  • ☐ Smoke and CO alarms tested; dead batteries replaced today
  • ☐ Water heater and laundry zone cleared; nothing stored against vents or the electrical panel

Monthly mini-schedule: October, November, December

Five items per month, one sitting each. These carry what a quarterly pass cannot: recurring checks and the year-end items that have a deadline. All generic, so the order holds wherever you live; swap a line if your home does not have the thing it names.

October (5)

  • ☐ Swap the air filter and vacuum the fridge coils
  • ☐ Descale the kettle, coffee maker, and showerhead
  • ☐ Audit subscriptions against the table below; cancel anything you have not used in 30 days
  • ☐ Set the holiday budget ceiling: gifts, food, travel, per person
  • ☐ Clear one drawer or cabinet completely; put back only what earns its place

November (5)

  • ☐ Deep-clean the oven and range hood filter before holiday cooking
  • ☐ Flip and rotate mattresses; wash duvets and pillow protectors
  • ☐ Check the fire extinguisher gauge and the first-aid kit contents
  • ☐ Run the washer on a cleaning cycle; clear the dryer lint path end to end
  • ☐ Confirm holiday hosting and travel dates; book whatever needs booking

December (5)

  • ☐ Reset the entry and coat zone for guest traffic
  • ☐ Wipe high-touch points: handles, switch plates, remotes, phone
  • ☐ Update emergency contacts and the household document locator
  • ☐ File year-end receipts, shred expired documents, back up photos
  • ☐ Restock batteries, flashlights, and first-aid basics; note January purchases now

Four reset tips

Four opinions from resets that stuck, not survey data. Take what fits your household.

  1. Audit money before you clean. Cleaning can wait a week; renewals cannot. The subscription table above takes twenty minutes from your bank statement, and every cancellation repeats monthly. Do it first, before the timer starts on the kitchen.
  2. One zone per sitting, timer on. Ninety minutes, then stop wherever you are. A reset spread across the whole house at once ends with five half-finished rooms. Five finished sittings end with a reset that actually happened.
  3. The donate bag leaves the house the same day. Put it in the car trunk, not by the door. A bag that lives by the door for a week becomes furniture; a bag in the trunk gets dropped off the next time you are driving anyway.
  4. Reset the entry last. It is the zone you walk through most, so it is the first thing you see every time you come home. Finishing there buys the most visible progress per hour of anything on the sheet.
