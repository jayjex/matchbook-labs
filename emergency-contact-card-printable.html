4 tips for a contact card that works when it matters

Four habits from households whose sheets are still accurate a year later. They are opinions, not rules.

  1. Give the sitter the fridge sheet, not the whole binder. A pet sitter or house sitter needs one page: who to call, the doctor or vet line, the meeting point, and where the gas and water sit. The full binder stays where it lives; the sheet does the errand.
  2. Copy it onto the fridge and every phone lock screen. That line comes straight from the binder's contacts page, and it earns its place: paper survives a dead battery, and a lock screen survives a lost wallet. Do both the same sitting so the copies never disagree.
  3. Tape over the finished sheet. A strip of clear tape keeps cooking splatter and kid hands off the handwriting. Rewrite the sheet when the ink fades rather than squinting at it in a bad moment.
  4. Re-read the sheet on two dates a year. Attach the check to the daylight clock change or a birthday. Two minutes per check: does each number still connect, and is the meeting point still the right porch?

About the shutoff box

The box records locations, and that is all it does. Where the gas valve, water main, and breaker panel sit is a fact about your home, best learned at move-in from your utility's guide, which walks you through each one. Write the spot, then label it with tape if it hides behind something. Note which shutoffs you can turn yourself and which need the utility crew: the crew decides when it is safe, and never re-enter or reconnect anything on your own. Those mechanics live in your utility's guide, not on this page.
