4 opinions on keeping this schedule alive

  1. Set a 15-minute timer for the daily jobs. (Opinion.) A job without an end time grows to fill the whole evening, and a schedule that needs the whole evening dies in week two. Fifteen minutes of counters and dishes is a real routine; "clean the kitchen later" is not. When the timer ends, tick the box and stop.
  2. Quick daily jobs go in the first rows, weekly jobs lower down. (Opinion, borrowed straight from the pack's own schedule page.) The grid reads top to bottom, so the jobs you want done every day should be the first ones your eye lands on. Dishes and counters in row 1, sheets and floors in row 3.
  3. Laminate the sheet or slip it in a sheet protector and use a dry-erase pen. (Opinion from the pack's print tips.) One print then lasts weeks instead of one week, and the weekly tick routine still works - wipe the sheet at the end of the week. Print a fresh paper copy whenever you want a dated record instead.
  4. If a job keeps getting skipped, move it earlier in the day or split it in two. (Opinion, again from the pack's how-to.) A job skipped three days running is usually a job scheduled at the wrong time, not a personal failure. "Mop the floor" becomes "sweep after dinner" plus "mop on Saturday morning", and both of those actually happen.
