3 tips for solving word scrambles

What works for us when a row refuses to crack:

  1. Pull the vowels out first. List the vowels in a row and the consonants underneath. Most of these words carry two or three vowels, and the consonant skeleton left over is usually enough to name the word. EEPPRP stops fighting back the moment you see two P's and three E's.
  2. Count before you guess. The answer is exactly as long as the scramble, so a five-letter row can't be SKILLET. Cross off every kitchen word of the wrong length, write down candidates of the right one, and tick off letters as you place them.
  3. Test the endings kitchen words share. The -ER and -LE endings alone cover a third of this list (SIMMER, TOASTER, LADLE, NOODLE). When the front of a word won't come, strip the likely suffix off the back and see what's left to rearrange.
