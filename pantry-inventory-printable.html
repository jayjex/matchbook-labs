Pantry Inventory, Free to Print

A shelf list you can keep current in pen. This pantry inventory printable is two pages: page 1 logs 12 items with quantity, the day you opened each one, the date the label prints, and a yes/no circle for whether the next shopping trip replaces it. Page 2 holds the two shelves the main log never fits (six freezer rows, six spice rows) and 8 shopping lines that start from the circles you marked. Every date on the sheet is transcribed from a label you own; the sheet invents none.

How to use this page: print both pages at 100% scale (the site chrome drops out on paper). Fill page 1 one row at a time, when you notice the item, not from memory in one sitting. Write what you have, when you opened it, and the use-by date exactly as printed on the label, then fill the circle next to yes or no in the reorder column. Page 2 covers the freezer and the spice shelf, then the shopping lines copy from the circles on shopping day. Reprint page 1 when 12 rows run out.

Four opinions on running a pantry inventory

  1. (Opinion.) Put the newest stock behind the oldest. The hand reaches the front row first, so the front row should hold the item that needs using first, and the new purchase goes behind it. A shelf ordered any other way hides its own history.
  2. (Opinion.) Write the opened date the day you open it. The purchase date lives on a receipt you will throw away; the opened date lives nowhere else. It is also the row detail nobody remembers a week later, which is exactly why it earns its column.
  3. (Opinion.) Pick one date format and keep it for every row. A column written in mixed formats cannot be scanned, and a column you cannot scan at a glance is a column you stop reading. One format, all rows, and the whole sheet stays readable from arm's length.
  4. (Opinion.) Let the reorder circles draft the shopping list. Fill the circle in the moment you notice the stock is low, because that notice is free and the memory of it is not. On shopping day the 8 lines on page 2 copy from the circles instead of from a guess in the aisle.

What this page is

A shelf list, and nothing past that. It records what you own, how much, since when, and what the labels say. No temperature appears anywhere on it, no storage rule, and no guidance on what anyone should eat or discard; those are decisions the sheet never touches. The use-by column transcribes your own labels, so if a label says nothing, the column stays blank and the row still works. What the sheet does do is turn "do we already have one of these" into a glance instead of a search, and it makes the shopping list start from evidence you wrote down instead of memory in the store.
