The per-job checklist, residential standard

28 steps in six blocks. Kitchen and bathrooms carry the most steps because that is where callbacks come from. Floors go last in every room: dust first, vacuum second, mop third.

Entry and setup (4)

  • ☐ Walk-through against the client's notes: special requests, locked rooms, pets secured
  • ☐ Caddy restocked: labeled bottles, cloths, pads, trash liners
  • ☐ Fragile items noted; furniture moved only where the client agreed
  • ☐ Windows opened where weather allows, for airflow while you work

Kitchen (6)

  • ☐ Counters, backsplash, and table cleared, wiped, dried
  • ☐ Sink scrubbed, faucet and fixtures polished, drain rinsed
  • ☐ Stovetop degreased; knobs, hood, and appliance fronts wiped
  • ☐ Microwave cleaned inside and out; turntable washed
  • ☐ Cabinet fronts and handles spot-cleaned
  • ☐ Floor swept and mopped, corners included

Bathrooms (6)

  • ☐ Toilet cleaned: bowl, seat, outside, base, behind
  • ☐ Shower and tub scrubbed; glass squeegeed streak-free
  • ☐ Vanity, sink, and faucet cleaned; mirror polished
  • ☐ Tile and grout spot-treated where the agreement covers it
  • ☐ Towels straightened; paper and soap refilled per agreement
  • ☐ Floor swept and mopped, behind the toilet included

Bedrooms (5)

  • ☐ Beds made or linens changed per the client's agreement
  • ☐ Surfaces dusted: nightstands, dressers, sills, headboard
  • ☐ Mirrors and glass wiped
  • ☐ Trash emptied, liners replaced
  • ☐ Floor vacuumed, edges and under-bed line where reachable

Living areas (4)

  • ☐ Upholstery lint-rolled, cushions straightened
  • ☐ Hard surfaces and screens dusted; remotes wiped
  • ☐ Sills, ledges, and baseboards spot-wiped
  • ☐ Rugs vacuumed; hard floors swept and mopped

Exit walk (3)

  • ☐ Supplies packed, nothing left behind
  • ☐ Checklist ticked to the end; notes written for the next visit
  • ☐ Lights off, doors locked, key returned per your key log

Five opinions on pricing cleaning jobs

These are opinions from working the loop, not survey data. Take the ones that fit your market and your agreements.

  1. Charge by the job, not the hour, for standing residential work. Clients buy a clean house on a schedule, and hourly billing punishes you for getting faster at it. Hourly still earns its place on first deep cleans or jobs where the scope is unknown; there it protects you.
  2. Price move-outs above a standard clean of the same size. Empty homes take longer per room: insides of cabinets and appliances, marks on walls, floors under where furniture sat. Clients comparing quotes rarely expect that, so say it in the quote.
  3. Write what is not included as plainly as what is. Inside the fridge, interior windows, laundry: if it is out of scope, list it as out of scope. Most pricing disputes come from scope, not from the number.
  4. Re-quote after two visits if the first clean ran long. A heavy catch-up clean should not become the permanent rate. Once the home is on maintenance level, your price and your time should match again.
  5. Keep a written rate per client and touch it yearly. Rate drift is quiet: you agreed on a price three years ago, the job grew two rooms, nobody said anything. The client tracker above is where that review starts.
