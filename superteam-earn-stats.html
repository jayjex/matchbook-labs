Superteam Earn Statistics: Reward Distribution From 28 Live Listings

Superteam Earn (superteam.fun) is a bounty board where Solana ecosystem sponsors post content, design, and development work with advertised rewards. We pulled the live board on Sep 4, 2026 and froze it. This page reports the reward distribution from that snapshot: which buckets sponsors price into, how the deadlines cluster, and how much of the board accepts agent submissions. Reward distribution from the snapshot

The 28-listing file holds 26 listings with a single listed reward and 2 that quote a range instead. Buckets below cover the 26 single-reward listings, in USD-denominated tokens (USDC or USDG) at the listed amount.

The middle of the board is thick: 22 of 26 listings (85%) paid somewhere between $500 and $4,999. Median listed reward: $1,000. Smallest: $205. Largest: $10,000. The 26 fixed rewards sum to $56,705. The two range listings sat at $50 to $250 and $1,000 to $2,000, and they are excluded from the buckets rather than folded into a midpoint.

Tokens and deadline urgency

Sponsors denominate rewards in stablecoins: 15 listings in USDG, 13 in USDC. We report listed amounts, so a $1,000 bounty counts as $1,000 regardless of token.

Deadlines ran from Sep 4 to Oct 15, and they cluster near the harvest date: 4 listings were due within 48 hours of the pull, 11 more within 3 to 7 days, 6 within 8 to 14 days, and 7 had 15 days or more. That curve is the practical read on the board: listings turn over fast, so any Superteam Earn statistics you see without a snapshot date are noise.

agentAccess: 45 HUMAN_ONLY vs 6 AGENT_ALLOWED

The raw cards dump ships 51 records across 3 paginated pulls of the live board. The agentAccess field reads verbatim: 45 HUMAN_ONLY, 6 AGENT_ALLOWED. Deduplicating those records to the 17 unique live listings behind them gives 15 HUMAN_ONLY and 2 AGENT_ALLOWED, and both agent-allowed listings were $500 bounties.

The practical signal: most sponsors price listings above the agent-allowed tier and keep them human-only, so automated agents competing on the open board mostly see the bottom of the reward table. If you build agent tooling, that split is the constraint your tool operates inside.

What the busiest listings looked like

Competition counts at pull time (the 17 unique listings): median submissions per listing was 54, median comments 11. The outliers: a $1,500 Flint content post drew 214 submissions and 42 comments; the $8,000 Breakpoint 2026 content bounty had 76 submissions; a $10,000 Solana Summit Serbia bounty had 51. Reward size did not track competition in any obvious way, which is itself the useful finding for anyone pricing a submission effort.

Method and snapshot honesty

Every number on this page is computed from files harvested from the live superteam.fun API on Sep 4, 2026: a 28-listing file with full description HTML, and the raw paginated cards dump. Superteam Earn rotates listings constantly, rewards move as sponsors edit or close listings, and competition counts changed by the hour while we pulled. Treat this page as a dated snapshot of one day on the board, not a live feed, and the downloadable dataset as the same snapshot in raw form so you can recompute every figure.

This is market research material. It describes how sponsors advertised contest prizes on one day, and nothing here predicts what the board will pay, whether any listing gets won, or what any submission is worth. Rewards are contest prizes controlled by sponsors. This page is not investment advice, and none of these numbers are returns.
