Moving Abroad Checklist (Free): the 12-Month Countdown, Documents, Budget, and Packing List

A move abroad fails in the boring middle: the document that takes six weeks to arrive, the deposit you paid twice, the box you shipped without writing down what was in it. This expat relocation checklist is the whole move on one page. Work the countdown backward from your date, gather documents in parallel, and split your money into two pots so one bad month does not end the plan. Every table is write-in, and the numbers shown are worked examples, not quotes for your route.

Passport validity and the 6-month rule

Many countries want a passport valid for six months beyond your arrival or stay, and some airlines check this at boarding even when immigration does not. Where your destination sits on that range, and what renewals cost and take, varies by country - check official government sources. The practical move is the same either way: if your passport has less than a year on it, renew early, because the renewal time becomes your schedule's floor.

Apostilles, in two sentences

An apostille is a certificate from a designated authority in the document's home country that certifies the signature on it, so a foreign official can accept the document without further checks. Which papers need one, and who issues them, differs by document and country, so confirm against the official list of the authority that would issue yours before paying any intermediary.

Tax residency, one note

Moving out does not automatically end your tax obligations at home, and the date your old residence ends for tax purposes is not always the date you fly. Rules differ by country and by treaty - check with a tax official or a professional in both countries before you assume anything about the year you move.

The budget split: one-time costs vs a first-3-months runway

The mistake that strands people is budgeting the move and not the arrival. Split the money into two pots. Pot one is everything you pay once to get there. Pot two is the runway that carries you until income starts, and it is the pot that decides whether the move survives contact with month two. Note the currency you actually budget in, because exchange fees come out of the runway, not out of thin air.

Why the runway is 3x the one-time pot: in the example, three months of ordinary living costs more than the entire physical move. If income does not start when planned, the runway is what buys you time to fix it without a panic flight home. Underestimate income, overestimate costs, and keep the two pots in separate accounts if your bank allows it.

Housing viewing log

Viewings blur together by the third one, and the address on the listing means nothing a week later. One row per viewing, written the same day:

DateAddress (write it in full)Rent / monthNotes

Write the address fully; future you will not remember which "nice 2nd floor place" this was. Questions worth asking every time: notice period on both sides, who repairs what, how the deposit comes back, what the real monthly costs were for the last tenant.

Arrival day: the 10-item list

Long flight, new country, zero sleep. Do not trust memory today; trust the list.

  1. Documents still in carry-on, never in checked luggage.
  2. Phone charged, offline map downloaded before landing.
  3. Local SIM or eSIM working.
  4. Small amount of local cash in hand.
  5. New address written on paper, not just in your phone.
  6. First-night accommodation confirmed in writing.
  7. Pickup or transit plan to the accommodation known before you leave the airport.
  8. Note where your passport and documents are before leaving the airport.
  9. Water and something eaten; essentials bought (toiletries, charger adapter).
  10. Message home: landed, safe, address shared.
