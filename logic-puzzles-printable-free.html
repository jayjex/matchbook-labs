Easy Logic Grid Puzzles (Free Printable)

Two small logic grids live on this page, both solvable with a pencil. Three friends, three favorite colors, three hobbies per grid, six clues each. Before publishing, a small solver script checked every grid against all 36 possible arrangements, and the six clues allow exactly one answer apiece. Print with Ctrl+P (Cmd+P on Mac): the grids and clues stay on the sheet, the answers drop off it. No sign-up.

How to use this page: hit Ctrl+P (Cmd+P on Mac) for a clean worksheet — the answer tables stay hidden in print mode. Solving on screen instead? Each grid has an answer section in a collapsed panel under its clues; open it when you are done or stuck. Mark an X in a grid box when a clue rules that combination out, and when only one box in a row survives, that combination is true.

3 tips for solving logic grids

What works for us, from solving a lot of these:

  1. Read all six clues before you mark anything. The last clue often tells you what to do with the first five, and marking too early means erasing. In both grids above, the color clues land first and the linking clue at the end is what turns them into a full answer.
  2. Mark every box a clue rules out, even the small ones. A single X looks useless until a second X lands in the same row, because three options minus two leaves exactly one. The negative clues at the top of each list exist for this.
  3. When a row has one open box, fill it in pen, then re-read every clue. Placing Cal on painting instantly splits the chess-and-baking pair in Puzzle 1, and the blue-chess clue finishes the job. Every placed fact can retire another clue.

A word on difficulty, since "easy" means different things to different people: both grids here are a gentle intro, sized for a first attempt or for kids who like deduction. They will not prepare anyone for a championship.
