What size should advent activity cards be?

This sheet prints six cards across, so on US Letter or A4 each card lands around 1.2 inches (3 cm) wide. That slips straight into a muffin tin cup, a small envelope, or a numbered box. If your calendar slots run smaller, fold each card in half with the number on the outside; the fold also gives the card some stiffness, which helps kids pull it out of tight pockets. For bigger slots, leave the card flat or tape it to the inside of the door. The exact size matters less than legibility: a number a five-year-old can read from across the room beats a card that fits to the millimeter.

4 tips for using advent activity cards

Opinions from running a card-a-day December, not survey findings:

  1. Prep the materials Sunday night. A card that says bake cookies dies at 5pm on a Tuesday when there is no butter. Before the week starts, check all 7 upcoming cards against the kitchen and the craft drawer, and buy or pull what's missing. Ten minutes on Sunday saves seven separate scavenger hunts.
  2. Mix effort levels across the week. If three baking cards land back to back, the calendar becomes homework. Deal the tags out like a hand of cards: one heavy bake or fort day per weekend, 10-minute cards (snowflakes, story, cocoa) on school nights. The tags on each card exist so you can do this in one glance.
  3. Number the blanks before you cut. Write a small number on the back corner of every card, printed and blank alike. When a day melts down and an activity gets skipped, you can reshuffle the deck without reading all 18 cards to find the gap.
  4. Cut all of them in one sitting. Cutting one card per night sounds manageable and never happens. One pass with scissors on December 1st takes about five minutes for the whole sheet; a paper trimmer makes it faster but is not required.

Swap cards and repeat next year

When the calendar empties on December 24th, rubber-band the deck and put it in the box with the decorations instead of the recycling. Next December, pull it out: most cards survive a year fine, and kids will ask for the cocoa bar and the fort night by name. Swap out the two or three that flopped for new blanks, shuffle the order so the month feels fresh, and you have next year's calendar built in an afternoon. A set that improves like this beats a store calendar you rebuy every year.

Common questions

How do you attach activity cards to an advent calendar?

Depends on the container: tuck them into numbered envelopes clothespinned to a string, roll and insert into muffin tin cups, tape them inside box lids, or fold and slot into small drawers. Tape on the outside works too, but pulling a card out is half the fun for kids, so containers that open beat flat attachments.

Can I write my own activities on the blank cards?

Yes, that's what the 6-card blank template is for. Ballpoint and fine markers work on the printed lines; if you use gel pens, let them dry a minute before stacking the cards. The blank cards are also the fix when one of the printed 12 doesn't fit your family: write a replacement and drop the printed one.

Do these cards print okay in black and white?

Yes. The print stylesheet switches to white background and dark text, and the tag labels stay readable as bordered text without color. Card stock holds up better across multiple years, but plain printer paper works for a first December.

Are the activity cards religious?
