Daily Habit Scorecard Printable: the Free One-Page Template (Print to PDF)

This daily habit scorecard prints on one sheet and grades a week of habits in two ways at once: a circle for every habit you kept each day, and one number from 1 to 5 for the day as a whole. Ten write-in rows, seventy circles, a day-grade line, and a three-question evening check. Pen and paper, a couple of minutes a night. If you want the same idea at week level with goals instead of habits, the weekly goals template covers that angle.

How to use this page: print at 100% and write your habits in the rows on Sunday evening or Monday morning, before the week starts. Name them the way you would explain them to someone else ("lights out by 11", "no phone at breakfast"). Each evening, fill the day's circle for every row you kept, then write one number from 1 to 5 in the day-grade line for how the whole day went. Last, answer the three evening-check questions. One sheet covers one week; print a fresh copy for the next.

The layout makes one evening pass possible. Circles sit in the same row as the habit they belong to, so a fill is one glance and one pen stroke. The day-grade line is separate from the circles on purpose: the circles say which rows held, the number says how the whole day went, and the two disagree often enough that both are worth writing down. A day can fill six circles and still earn a 2, and the grade is where that shows.

Four tips for running the scorecard

Opinions from printing and filling sheets like this one, not findings from anywhere - your rows and your weeks will differ.

  1. (Opinion.) Score the night before, not the next morning. Filling circles and writing the grade that evening records the day while it is still fresh; a morning pass records whatever you can reconstruct over coffee. If a row is ambiguous at scoring time, the blank circle is the honest answer, and question 3 of the evening check is where you fix tomorrow's version of that row.
  2. (Opinion.) Write habits a stranger could grade. The rule sheet in the 30-Day Discipline Starter puts it as "a number, a duration, a yes or a no", and the same test works here: "be more organized" can't fill a circle, while "10 minutes" can fail loudly and get rewritten. Vague rows turn every evening into an argument with yourself.
  3. (Opinion.) Start with three rows, earn up to ten. The grid holds ten so a full week still fits on one page; it is a ceiling. Three rows that fill their circles every day say more than ten rows that fill two, and a new habit earns its row only when the first three fill without thinking.
  4. (Opinion.) Grade bad days too. A 2 written down is a record; a blank is a guess. The grade line exists so a bad Tuesday still leaves a mark on the sheet, which is also what makes restarts cheap: the miss costs one cell, and the week keeps its shape. Miss a whole evening, fill what you remember the next day and mark the rest as best you can.
