Grocery Budget Tracker, Free to Print

Food money, four weeks at a time, on two pages. This grocery budget tracker is a food budget template free to print: page 1 holds 4 weekly rows, each with a budget cell, a spent cell and a left cell, all write-in. Page 2 splits the same month into 6 food categories (produce, protein, dairy, pantry, frozen, other) and adds a 3-line price book for the items you price-check. The sheet records what you planned and what the receipts say; the numbers stay yours.

How to use this page: print both pages at 100% scale (the site chrome drops out on paper). On page 1, write each week and its budget before you shop; copy spent from the receipts at week's end; subtract by hand for left. On page 2, keep the 6 categories as they print or cross one out and write your own, and use the price book for items you buy often. Reprint the whole set each month.

Four opinions on keeping a grocery tracker

  1. (Opinion.) Shop with a list, not with your appetite. The list gets written at home, from the category table on page 2, before the store doors open. A list written in aisle three follows the endcaps; a list written at dinner follows the table you already filled in.
  2. (Opinion.) Write price-book lines while standing at the shelf. The tag is in front of you and the pen is in your pocket. A price remembered at home gets rounded, and a rounded price makes the next comparison wrong in a direction you will not see.
  3. (Opinion.) Fill the spent column the same day the receipts come home. Receipts from one trip read in one sitting take two minutes; four trips remembered on Sunday take a guess. The column exists to hold what the register printed, not what you recall.
  4. (Opinion.) Write the budget cell before the trip, never after. A budget copied after shopping records the spending twice. One written before the trip gives the week a number to be copied against, which is the whole point of a column called budget.

What this page is

A grocery tracker and a price book. It records weeks, categories, receipts, and shelf prices; it predicts nothing, prices nothing, and recommends nothing. Nothing on this page is financial advice. What the sheet does is put the whole month of food spending on two pages you can hold, so the next trip starts from numbers you wrote down instead of ones you are recalling.
