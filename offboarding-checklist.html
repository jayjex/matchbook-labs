The offboarding checklist, one page

Employee: ______________   Role: ______________   Manager: ______________   Last day: ______________

IT and access revocation (6 items)

  • Email forwarded or auto-reply set, account suspended at end of last day
  • Shared drives and file ownership moved to the successor
  • Internal tools and admin rights removed
  • Customer and vendor account access removed
  • Password manager entries and MFA revoked
  • SaaS seats cancelled and the license list updated

Knowledge transfer (5 items)

  • Handover document written and shared with the successor
  • Open projects and deadlines listed with current status
  • Credentials moved to the team password manager
  • Introductions made to key customers and vendors
  • Recurring tasks walked through live at least once

Final pay and benefits (write it down now)

  • Final pay date and amount: ______________________________
  • Benefits that continue, and until when: ______________________
  • Who answers pay and benefits questions after the last day: ____________

Equipment return (6 items)

  • Laptop, monitor, and charger
  • Phone, SIM, and tablet
  • Keys, badge, and building access
  • Company card and expense accounts closed
  • Tools, uniforms, and specialty equipment
  • Return deadline set and every item checked back in

Exit interview, 4 questions

  1. What made you start looking for a new job?
  2. What would have made you stay?
  3. What should we stop, start, or keep doing?
  4. What should the next person in this role know?

The last-day timeline, 5 steps

Offboarding fails when everything lands on the last day. Work back from the exit date instead.

WhenWhat happens
Two weeks outLast day confirmed in writing, handover doc started, exit conversation on the calendar
One week outAccess list sent to IT, equipment return deadline set, customers and vendors told who takes over
Day beforeRecurring tasks walked through live, key contacts introduced, stray files collected
Last dayEquipment returned, accounts shut down at end of day, goodbye note to the team
Week afterFinal pay out on your normal payroll schedule, benefits questions routed to the named contact, exit notes filed

Four offboarding mistakes

In my view, these four cause most of the damage. All four are fixable with the sheet above.

1. Orphaned accounts

The leaver's logins keep working because nobody owns turning them off. Three months later a former contractor still has the shared drive. One person owns access revocation, and access dies at end of day on the last day. That single rule removes the whole category of problem.

2. Tribal knowledge walks out the door

The tasks nobody ever documented leave with the person who did them. The invoice run that only works on Tuesdays, the vendor who answers one email address, the spreadsheet with the macro. The handover table forces a task, an owner, and a link, and anything without a link gets a live walk-through. Five rows of that beats an hour of "wait, how did you do X" messages a month later.

3. Improvising final pay and benefits

Questions about final pay and benefits show up weeks after the last day, when everyone has moved on and nobody remembers the details. Write the three lines on the sheet while the person is still in the room: the pay date, what continues and until when, and who fields the questions. Then check your payroll schedule and local rules against what you wrote, before you commit to it.

4. Skipping the exit conversation

The exit interview is the only unfiltered review of working there that you will get. My take: ask the same 4 questions every time, write the answers down, and read them together once a quarter. One leaver is an anecdote. Three leavers with the same answer is a scheduling problem, a pay problem, or a manager problem, and you want to know which.
