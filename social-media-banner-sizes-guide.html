The safe-zone rule

Every platform in the table crops banner edges on some device, and several of them also drop profile avatars and interface buttons over the corners, usually bottom-left. That leaves one region you can trust: roughly the middle 60 percent of the image, held slightly above dead center. Anything with meaning goes there. Words, logos, and faces placed in the outer 15 to 20 percent will be cut or covered for a chunk of your audience, which is why banners that look perfect in a desktop draft cut their own words in half on a phone. Design the full canvas, then check the crop on an actual phone before you publish.

Four opinions

  1. Put the wordmark in the center band, not the left. The usual logo-left habit comes from letterheads, and banners crop from the sides first. Center placement survives every platform in the table.
  2. Design at double size, export down. Platforms recompress uploads hard. A 2x canvas scaled to final dimensions keeps type edges clean where a 1x export turns to mush.
  3. Dark background, light text. Profile interfaces pile white UI on top of banners, and photo backgrounds fight it. A dark gradient behind your text is the setup that still looks intentional two years later.
  4. No type under ~28px at final size. After platform compression, small text becomes noise. If a line needs to be small to fit, the banner carries too many words.
