Why your first month often costs double

Move-in math is brutal because three charges land on one day. You pay the first month's rent, the security deposit (commonly equal to one month), and sometimes the last month upfront. Meanwhile the old lease usually runs past move-out day, so for some stretch you are paying two rents. On the local example that overlap row is $100 because the leases overlap by days; on the cross-country one it is $600 because a full month doubles up.

The deposit side doubles the sting: your old deposit does not come back on move-out day. The outgoing walk-through, any deductions, and the refund check or transfer take weeks, and it lands after you have already fronted the new deposit. Budget for the gap instead of assuming the refund covers the new deposit. Two habits that help: overlap the two leases as little as the calendar allows, and ask about refund timing in writing at the old walk-through, so you know which month that money actually returns.

Five ways to cut the packing supplies row

  1. Get boxes free from liquor and grocery stores. Their boxes are small and built to carry weight, which is exactly what you want for books and dishes. Go early on a weekday and ask; most hand them over by the bundle.
  2. Wrap dishes in towels and clothes. Bubble wrap is one of the priciest supplies per box, and you already own its replacement. Towels, sheets, and t-shirts pad plates just as well, and socks fit glasses. Your laundry travels padded instead of boxed separately.
  3. Use suitcases, laundry baskets, and drawers as containers. Anything with a handle is a box you do not have to buy or tape. Dresser drawers can be moved whole if the piece is light enough.
  4. Label with masking tape and one marker. Pre-printed label sheets and color-coded sticker kits are the same product at four times the price. Room name on two sides of the box does the whole job.
  5. Fill gaps with shredded scrap paper. Junk mail, magazines, and newspaper from the recycling bin replace packing paper for anything that is not fragile-fragile. Save the paid padding for the dishes.

Moving insurance or self-insure?

My opinion: self-insure a local move where you drive the van yourself, and think hard before paying extra on a full-service move. Self-insure works because the realistic worst case on a local self-drive move is a dropped box, and your renters or home policy may already cover belongings in transit; one phone call to your insurer before move day tells you. On a full-service move, the standard released-value coverage that comes free is compensation by weight at a token rate, which pays a fraction of what a damaged item is worth, so buying it and calling it insurance is misleading. Either buy the mover's full-value protection deliberately, or photograph your valuables, pack them in your own car, and accept the risk on the rest. What I would not do is sign a mover's waiver without reading what per-item limits apply, because that is the part that decides whether the coverage means anything.
