The two-page reading log

Nothing is pre-filled and there is no example shelf to copy. The rows stay blank so a 900-page hardcover and a 90-page novella take the same line, and so the stars come from your read, not from anyone's scale. Ten rows per page keeps the sheet to one side of paper; when it fills, print another and date the corner. The start and end date columns are there because the gap between them is the honest record of how a book actually went, and the star circles fill in with a pen: five circles, shade one per star.

Four opinions on keeping a reading log

Opinions from keeping sheets like this, take or leave:

  1. One line per book is enough. (Opinion.) Title, author, dates, stars. A log that asks for a book report gets abandoned by row four; a log that asks for a line fills up.
  2. Log it when you finish, not later. (Opinion.) The end date and the stars take thirty seconds at the kitchen table that night. Batch-filling six books in March from memory is how months go blank.
  3. Shade the stars honestly, including the twos. (Opinion.) A shelf of all fives tells you nothing in year two. The two-star rows are the ones that save you from buying the same dud twice, and they make the fives mean something.
  4. Keep the to-read list fed. (Opinion.) An empty list is where reading streaks go to die, because the decision happens at the bookshelf with nothing picked. One line, plus who recommended it, and the next book is already chosen before you finish the current one.
