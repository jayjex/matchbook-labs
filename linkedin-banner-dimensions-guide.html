LinkedIn Banner Dimensions (2026 Reference)

One strip, 1584 by 396 pixels, stretched across the top of the profile. This page collects the upload spec, what mobile apps crop out of it, which file format survives upload best, and four design opinions from making banners. The last section covers a template pack for the posts that run under the banner.

The spec

Spec as commonly documented as of early 2026. Platforms change display behavior without announcements. Check LinkedIn's official help pages before you export.

What the mobile slice means in practice: a logo pushed hard against the left edge renders fully on a desktop monitor and falls out of frame on the app. The parts that carry meaning (wordmark, name, title) belong in the middle band. Design the full 1584 × 396 canvas, then check the crop on an actual phone before you publish.

Four opinions

  1. (Opinion.) Put the logo just left of center, not against the left edge. The letterhead habit parks logos in the corner, and corners are what phones crop. Slightly left of center reads as placed rather than floaty, and it survives the mobile slice.
  2. (Opinion.) Design at double size, export down. The platform recompresses uploads. A 2x canvas scaled to 1584 × 396 keeps type edges clean where a 1x export turns soft.
  3. (Opinion.) Dark background, light text. Profile pages stack white cards and interface elements under the banner, and photo backgrounds fight them. Dark with light text is the setup that still reads a year from now.
  4. (Opinion.) One message per banner. Name and role, at most. A strip 396 pixels tall has no room for contact lines, taglines, and a mission statement; banners that try all three end up as unreadable noise.
