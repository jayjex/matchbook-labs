Security rules - printed on every copy

  1. Never write a full address on this sheet. The last 4 characters are enough to match a row to a screen.
  2. Use the wallet name or alias, not keys: no seed phrase, recovery phrase, password or private key on paper.
  3. Verify the full address on your device before sending - the sheet is a label, never a source to copy from.

4 opinions on keeping the inventory current

  1. Record the last 4 characters, never the full address. (Opinion.) A full address on paper turns one lost sheet into a permanent exposure, and a printed address invites copy-paste from paper, where one misread character sends funds nowhere. Four characters at the end identify a wallet for matching purposes and give a thief nothing. If a row needs more than the last 4, that detail belongs on the device, not the sheet.
  2. Name wallets by what they do. (Opinion.) "main cold wallet", "airdrop burner", "client payments" - a row named for its job tells you which wallet to reach for without opening anything. Names like "wallet 1" and "new wallet" age badly: by next quarter neither says what it holds or why it exists.
  3. Walk the list against the apps, not from memory. (Opinion.) Open the phone's app list and each exchange's withdrawal whitelist, and fill rows from what you see. Memory produces the three wallets you use; the app list surfaces the one you forgot, which is usually the row the quarter check exists for.
  4. Reprint fresh each quarter and keep the old sheets. (Opinion.) Editing one sheet forever turns the inventory into a diary of crossed-out names. A fresh print beside the old one shows what moved: which wallet got retired, which alias finally settled, whether the same unnamed wallet shows up twice in a row.
