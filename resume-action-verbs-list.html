Resume Action Verbs List (Free): 20 Strong Verbs, 5 Categories, Fill-In Examples

A verb at the front of a bullet says what you did; "responsible for" says what existed near you. This resume action verbs list is free: 20 strong verbs in 5 categories, one fill-in example bullet per category, 3 before-and-after rewrites, a placement note, and 4 opinions, each labeled as one. Every example is generic on purpose, so the blanks take your facts, not mine.

How to use this page: find the row that matches the thing you did, take a verb, and write one bullet that starts with it. Keep the fact you already have, swap the opener for the verb, and fill the example blanks with your own numbers and dates. Read the 3 rewrites before you start, and the 4 opinions after, so you know where the list ends and taste begins.

The verbs table: 5 categories, 20 verbs

Each row holds one kind of thing people do at work. The example bullet is a shape, not a suggestion about your job: copy its structure, fill every blank with something true, and delete whatever does not apply.

3 rewrites, before and after

The rewrite rule is small: the opener goes, the fact stays. Fill the blanks with what you know.

BeforeAfter
"Was responsible for ordering supplies""Ordered ___ in supplies weekly"
(the duty becomes the act, and the number lands where it counts)
"Helped with the newsletter""Wrote the ___ section of the newsletter"
("helped" hides your part; name the part you owned)
"In charge of the register""Counted the register daily and trained ___ new cashiers"
(two plain verbs, both checkable, no adjectives)

What changed each time: the opener went, the fact stayed, and the blanks are yours.

Where the verb goes

Put the verb first in every bullet, and keep the tense honest: past tense for roles you have left, present for the one you are in. The verb replaces the opener and nothing else - "Was responsible for the schedule" becomes "Scheduled the week", and everything after it stays the fact you already had. If a bullet already starts with a number or a tool, leave it alone; it is doing fine.

4 opinions, labeled as opinions

  1. One verb per bullet. (Opinion.) Two openers in one bullet ("Led ... built ...") split the reader's attention. Pick the verb that names the thing you would point at first.
  2. Plain beats rare. (Opinion.) "Led" survives every reader. "Spearheaded" makes some of them stop, and not the way you want. Pick the word you would say out loud in a meeting.
  3. Rewrite 3 bullets, not the whole resume. (Opinion.) The top three carry the page. Rewording all 30 is how typos get in.
  4. Numbers beat stronger verbs. (Opinion.) "Answered 40 calls a day" says more than "managed a high-volume line". If you know the number, verb first, number last.

This page is a writing list. Swapping verbs shapes sentences; it does not add experience, and no list of words is a job guarantee.
