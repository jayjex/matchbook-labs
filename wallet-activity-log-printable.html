A paper record of the transactions you actually made: when, from which wallet, on which chain, and what the gas cost. This free printable wallet activity log gives you 12 transaction rows per page, a monthly recap for the 5-line summary, and a labels box where your wallet aliases live. The hash column takes the last 4 characters only, so the sheet is safe to leave on a desk. It organizes your own history. It predicts nothing about any airdrop and holds no price claims. How to use this page: print both pages at 100% scale (the site chrome drops out on paper). One row per transaction, filled in the moment it confirms: date, the alias you call that wallet, the chain, the last 4 characters of the tx hash, and the gas paid. At month end, complete the recap table, then print a fresh page 1. Keep the filled sheets private; they are your activity history.

The sheet: 2 pages

Security reminders — read before you transact

1. Never share a seed phrase or private key. Support staff, admins, "verification" flows: no legitimate role ever asks for them. A message that asks is a scam, whatever name or logo it carries.

2. Verify URLs yourself. Type the address or open your own bookmark. Links in DMs and comments stay unclicked until you have checked where they lead.

3. Test with a small transaction first. A new address, bridge, or wallet gets a small test send before anything larger moves. The test costs minutes; the alternative can cost the balance.

Four opinions on keeping a wallet activity log

  1. Name wallets by job, not by brand. (Opinion.) Burner-2 tells you what the wallet is for; a nickname that made sense in week one tells you nothing in month three. Give each wallet a job-based alias and write the scheme down once in the labels box, so every later row stays readable.
  2. The last 4 of the hash is enough on paper. (Opinion.) A full hash is a long string that invites copy errors and serves no purpose on a desk sheet. Date plus chain plus last 4 pins the row well enough to find it again in an explorer when it matters.
  3. Write the gas the moment it confirms. (Opinion.) Fees are the part memory rounds down, and rounded-down fees make the recap quietly wrong. The same-day row takes seconds; a reconstructed month takes an evening.
  4. Recap before you archive the sheet. (Opinion.) The 5-row table works only while page 1 is still in front of you. Fill it at month end, then file both pages together; a recap promised "later" competes with a fresh month and loses.

What this page is

This is personal record-keeping, not financial advice. Nothing on it predicts any airdrop, token price, or project plan, and nothing here tells you what to buy, sell, or hold; the sheet exists so your own past actions are checkable when a project publishes its criteria. No reward is promised by writing rows: most activity across most seasons earns nothing, and a log that proves history is still worth more than a memory that argues with itself.
