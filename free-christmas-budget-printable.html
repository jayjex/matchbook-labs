The 10-week savings grid: $600 at $60 a week

Whatever your total is, divide by 10. A $600 target costs $60 a week, starting ten weeks out, which lands in early October most years. Miss a week? Spread the gap over the weeks left instead of doubling the next deposit. The grid exists so the shortfall shows up in week 2, not week 9.

5 rules that stop gift overbuying

  1. The list is the contract. If a gift has no row on the table, it does not get bought. Store trips without the list are where the budget leaks: you always come home with one thing more than the list.
  2. Budget by person, not by mood. Write the dollar number next to the name before you shop and treat it as a price cap. A number written in the store aisle is a rationalization, not a budget.
  3. Make the table fit the dashboard. Add the budget column and compare it to the gifts line. If the table is bigger, cut a gift. Never cut the rent money to protect a gift count.
  4. Update the actual column the day money moves. A budget filled in once at the end of December is a diary. Same-day updates catch an over-run while the receipt is still returnable.
  5. Give stocking stuffers a fixed list and a cap. Pick 24 small items under $10 each, write them down, buy them in one pass. The impulse bin at checkout is how a $340 gift line quietly becomes $430.

Common questions

How much should a Christmas budget be?

Work backwards from your list, not forwards from a percentage. Price the gifts you actually intend to buy, add food, travel, cards, decorations, and one charity amount. That sum is your budget. The example here lands at $600 for a household of four; the same sheets work at $250 or $1,500 without changing anything but the numbers.

When should I start saving for Christmas?

Ten weeks before your spending starts, which usually means early October. Starting later works with the same grid: divide the goal by the weeks you have left and accept the bigger deposit. The grid's job is showing the gap early, and it does that from any start date.

My gift list has more than 12 people. What do I do?

Print the table twice, or move to the full planner, which carries 50 gift rows across two pages plus a separate plan page for the six people who take the most thought. Keep the dashboard on one page either way; it is the sheet that keeps the others honest.
