Cash Envelope Template Printable

Six envelopes and a ledger, both free to print. This cash envelope template printable works as a blank cash envelope system: page 1 holds 6 write-in envelope boxes, each with a category line, a monthly-amount line, and a spent-tally of 4 circles. Page 2 is the envelope log: 10 write-in rows with a date, the envelope the money left, the amount out, and what is left in that envelope. The sheets record; the amounts stay yours.

How to use this page: print both pages at 100% scale (the site chrome drops out on paper). Write your own category on each envelope box and the amount you plan to spend there for the month; those two lines are the whole setup. During the month, fill one circle on an envelope each time you spend from it, and add each withdrawal as a row on the page 2 log: date, envelope, out, left. A full envelope of circles and an empty log row tell you the same thing at a glance. Reprint both pages each month.

Four opinions on running a paper envelope month

  1. (Opinion.) Round every monthly amount to a whole number. Envelopes get counted by hand and split mid-month, and 43.70 is a number nobody counts twice. A whole number is faster to write, faster to count, and the rounding difference is smaller than the error a fiddly figure invites. Pick the figures you will actually keep up with.
  2. (Opinion.) Fill the log row the same day cash leaves the envelope. A withdrawal remembered at night loses its date, and a lost date breaks the left column: the last "left" figure only means something if every row before it landed on paper the day it happened. Ten minutes late is fine; a week late is a different ledger.
  3. (Opinion.) Set up the month's envelopes the day your cash arrives. An envelope written after the first spend starts short, and the shortfall reads as overspending when it is really setup lag. Write the categories and amounts before the month's first purchase, and the circles measure the month instead of the delay.
  4. (Opinion.) Reprint rather than squeeze a thirteenth log row. A squeezed row breaks the column arithmetic that makes the log readable at a glance, and the fix costs one sheet. Keep the printed stack where the envelopes live, so the reprint happens before the squeeze, not after.

What this page is

A write-in record sheet for a cash-envelope month: six blank envelope boxes, a tally of four circles per envelope, and a 10-row log of what left each one. It organizes spending records on paper; it does not tell anyone how much to place in an envelope, which categories to pick, or whether cash fits a household better than a card. Nothing on this page is financial advice. What the sheets do is keep the whole month's envelope activity in one visible place, so the next withdrawal is one you write down on purpose.
