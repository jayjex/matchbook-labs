Content Creator Admin Checklist (Free Printable): Weekly and Monthly Loops

Two printable pages that run the boring half of a creator business: an 8-item weekly admin loop, a 6-item monthly loop, an idea backlog where every idea gets a format and a status circle, and a partnership log so no pitch dies of forgetfulness. Print both pages, pin them above the desk, run the weekly list once a week and the monthly list once a month. The content itself lives elsewhere; this is the paperwork around it.

How to use this page: pick one weekday as admin day and run the weekly loop top to bottom, ticking each box as you go. The Notes column is for the one thing each task surfaces: who is late on an invoice, which post ships Tuesday, who is waiting on a reply. On the first admin day of each month, run the monthly loop instead. Page two stays out all month: add ideas to the backlog as they come, move status circles, log every pitch. When a sheet fills up, print a fresh one.

Eight items on purpose. A loop that takes half an hour happens every week; a loop that takes an afternoon happens twice and then quietly dies. The Notes column is where the loop pays off: "invoice: Sam, day 12" beats "check invoices sometime" every time.

A partnership is any agreement to make or share something together: swaps, guest slots, collabs, sponsored work. Write the deal in one line while you still remember its shape, and update the status column after every contact. Six rows is enough; more than that and the follow-ups themselves become a backlog.

Four opinions from a loop that stuck

Opinions from running admin on paper, not survey data. Take what fits.

  1. Admin day gets one weekday, forever. A floating admin day is no admin day: "sometime this week" loses to every deadline that shows up. Pick one weekday, keep the same slot in the calendar, and let the loop be boring. Boring is why it happens 52 times a year.
  2. Keep the weekly and monthly lists apart. The moment the monthly items join the weekly list, one week a month turns into a three-hour slog, and that week teaches you to dread the whole ritual. Two lists, two rhythms, one page each.
  3. An idea without a format is a wish. "The rent data thing" sits in a notes app for months because nobody ever decided what it becomes. The Format column forces the decision while the idea is fresh, and a dead idea with a format is easier to delete than a vague one is to decode.
  4. "No reply" is a status, not an insult. Log the pitch, give it two weeks, send one follow-up, then write "ghosted" in the status column and move on. The log exists so chasing happens on paper instead of in your head at midnight.
