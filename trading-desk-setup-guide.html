Trading Desk Setup Guide (2026)

A trading desk is four surfaces pretending to be one. This page splits it into zones, gives you a six-line checklist to run before each session, four opinions from setting up desks, and a printable kit that covers the reference zone. Organization only; the note at the end says what this page is and is not.

The four zones

ZoneWhat lives thereMechanic
Screen zoneMonitor, keyboard, mouse, nothing elseThe only zone that needs power. Cables and coffee stay out of the writing space, so the other three zones keep a flat surface
Notes zoneOne pad of paper, one penWhere written plans and written numbers live. Paper, not an app, because a page you can see is a page you will reread
Reference zonePrinted sheets taped or laminated at eye lineContent you glance at mid-task. On the wall next to the monitor, never in a folder on the desktop
Log zoneA record sheet or notebook, always in the same spotFilled in after the session, dated, kept. A fixed home for the log is what makes entries comparable week to week

One surface, one job. When a zone starts collecting things from another zone, that is the signal to reset the desk.

Before you start: six checks

Write-in checklist. It checks that things are written down, not what they should say.

  • Desk cleared, all four zones have their own space
  • Plan written on paper in the notes zone, in your own words
  • Risk line written in ink; the number is yours to choose, this list only checks that it is on paper before anything opens
  • Reference sheets taped at eye line, not stacked in a drawer
  • Log sheet open in the log zone with today's date on it
  • Notifications off, second device out of arm's reach

Four opinions

  1. (Opinion.) One screen is enough to start. The second monitor mostly adds surfaces to watch. Everything on this page assumes a single screen and paper doing the rest; add hardware only when a real task keeps failing for lack of it.
  2. (Opinion.) Paper first, apps later. A written plan and a written log survive a dead battery and a closed tab. Once the paper habit holds for a month, move what genuinely needs software.
  3. (Opinion.) Tape sheets where you actually look, not where they look tidy. The kit's own README says the sheets are "designed to be glanced at, not read" and names four to tape up first. The wall beside the monitor wins over a folder of PDFs nobody opens.
  4. (Opinion.) Reset the desk after every session. Notes zone cleared, log zone filled and dated, reference zone untouched. A desk that starts clean makes the six checks above take a minute.
