4 opinions on getting a spring checklist finished

  1. One room per day. (Opinion.) Twelve rows on one sheet reads like one big day, and one big day is how spring lists die. A row circled per day fills the sheet in under two weeks, and the done circles make the pace visible. If a room takes two rows, that room gets two days.
  2. Rooms first, tasks second. (Opinion, borrowed from how the pack's checklist page groups its 30 tasks by room.) A task list mixes kitchen jobs and closet jobs into one long column, and you end up walking the whole house to finish three lines. Write the room in the first column and stay in it until its rows are circled.
  3. Use the skip list instead of quietly abandoning a room. (Opinion.) Guest rooms and storage rooms can sit a spring out. Writing them in the box turns "didn't get to it" into "decided against it", and next spring you start from a decision instead of a guilt pile.
  4. Move the jobs that repeat every season onto a monthly grid. (Opinion, from the pack's own monthly deep-clean plan.) The pack assigns each room a week - kitchen week 1, bathroom week 2, bedrooms and living areas week 3, laundry week 4 - with 2 or 3 write-in jobs per cell, so the deep cleans add to the routine instead of replacing it. Windows and closets fit in week 3 without a separate list.
