Note to the landlord

Three lines, neutral tone. Fill the blanks and send it the same day as the walk. Keep it to what you saw and when - leave out blame, requests, and anything about deductions.

1. Hi ________________ - I walked through the apartment today, before moving anything in, and noted the condition of each room.

2. Photos are attached with timestamps on; items marked F were already like that when I collected the keys.

3. Happy to walk through it together if that is easier. Thanks, ________________

4 tips for running the walkthrough

Opinions from moves done both ways - with a form and without one. The without-one version is why the with-one version exists.

1. Walk it before the truck arrives

An empty room shows its floors, walls, and windows. Do the full pass the moment you get the keys, before boxes and furniture pile against the baseboards. The one day the apartment is completely bare is move-in day; after that, you are photographing around your own stuff for a year.

2. Shoot a slow video pan in each room

Stand in the doorway, pan left to right, then hit the close-ups of anything you marked F or P. Photos prove details; the pan proves where the details are. Thirty seconds per room, and the phone's timestamp does the record-keeping for you.

3. Email the note, do not just mention it

A spoken "yeah, the blinds were already bent" disappears. The same sentence in an email, dated and sitting in your sent folder, does not. Send the three-line note above with photos attached, and keep the thread until you are settled in - a searchable copy beats a paper copy stuffed in a folder.

4. Test the boring things

Every tap, the flush, the breaker box, both window locks, the stove's four burners. They take ten minutes and they are the items nobody photographs until they fail. Mark anything that sputters, hums, or does nothing on the form - that is exactly what the notes column is for.
