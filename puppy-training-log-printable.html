The first weeks with a puppy run on small records: which skill you practiced, for how many minutes, how it went, when potty trips happened, where accidents happened. Memory is bad at all of that; paper is good at it. This printable puppy training log holds the records on two printed pages. Page 1 is a 12-row session sheet: date, skill, minutes, and a went circle. Page 2 is the week on one table, a 4-row accident log, and four opinion tips.

The sheet is an organizer, not a course. It records what happened so the pattern shows up on paper, like the hour accidents keep landing in. When you want the full 8-week structure behind these two pages, that is the paid product linked at the bottom.

4 opinion tips for a puppy training log

Opinions, labeled as such. They held up in our house; take or leave:

  1. Keep sessions short. (Opinion.) Two good minutes, three times a day, beats one long cranky one. Twelve rows fit a week of short sessions comfortably.
  2. End every session on something puppy can already do. (Opinion.) The last rep is the one you both carry into the next session. Starting from a win costs less than digging out of a flop.
  3. Write accidents down the same day, with the time. (Opinion.) Cleanup fades from memory by evening; the time column survives. Four dated rows turn scattered messes into a readable pattern.
  4. Print spare copies before you need them. (Opinion.) A log you cannot write on tonight is a log you stop keeping. Print the pair twice while the printer is already on.

What this page is

A record-keeping sheet. It organizes dates, times, minutes, and notes about your own puppy's days. It is not training advice and it predicts nothing: there is no claim here about what any puppy will learn, how fast, or which method works. It names no breeds, evaluates no temperament, and makes no veterinary or medical claims; anything about health or behavior that worries you belongs to your vet. The sheet keeps the entries in one place so the pattern is visible to you.
