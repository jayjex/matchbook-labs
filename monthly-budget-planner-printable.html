4 opinions on keeping this sheet alive

  1. Round every written amount up to the nearest whole number. (Opinion, and the only thing on this page closest to a rule.) Hand-summing exact cents is where sheet-keeping dies somewhere around week two. A number you can add standing up gets added; a number with two decimals waits for a calculator that never comes. The exact figures can live somewhere that computes them - the paper is for the shape of the month.
  2. Fill the fixed-expense table before the month starts, not during. (Opinion.) The fixed rows are already decided - the lease was signed, the plan was chosen - so leaving them blank means writing history twice. Eight filled rows on day one is half the sheet done before anything can go wrong, and the variable lines stop feeling like the whole month.
  3. Keep both pages in one place, all month. (Opinion.) A planner in a drawer gets re-created from memory in week three, and the re-creation is always kinder than the original. Fridge, clipboard, inside the notebook you already carry - one place, every time, is the entire system.
  4. Do the category recap at month end, from receipts, in one sitting. (Opinion.) A recap updated daily turns into a diary and gets abandoned with the diary. Done once at month end, the five lines answer a different question: which categories keep showing up, and which were a one-off. That answer is what the next month's sheet gets built from - which is what the note-to-next-month box is for.
