Emergency Contact List (Free Printable): 10 Contacts and Where the Papers Live

The two things a household looks for first are a phone number and a piece of paper, and both tend to live in the one drawer nobody labeled. This free printable puts them in order. Page 1 is an emergency contact list with ten write-in rows: name, relation, phone. Page 2 is a locator for the papers: five lines naming what each document is and where it lives, plus one backup note. Print both, fill them in during one calm hour, and put them where the household can reach them. It is the starter of a free emergency binder checklist; the full binder grows from here.

How to use this page: print both pages at 100% scale (the site chrome drops out on paper). Page 1: fill the contact rows in call order, whoever picks up goes in row 1, and write the relation (daughter, neighbor, workplace) so a caller knows who they reached. Page 2: one line per paper, what it is and where it lives, then the backup note for where copies sit. Tape page 1 where everyone looks and keep page 2 with the papers. When a number or a folder changes, cross out and rewrite; the sheet expects it. Nothing here is dated by year, so the sheets keep working until the household changes.

Page 1: the contact list, 10 rows

Four opinions on keeping an emergency contact list

Opinions from keeping sheets like this, take or leave:

  1. Paper beats phone-only. (Opinion.) A contact list that lives in one phone needs that phone charged, unlocked, and in the room. Paper on the fridge works for the sitter, the kid home first, and the neighbor holding a spare key. Save the same numbers into phones afterwards; the paper copy is the one that stays put.
  2. One calm hour does most of it. (Opinion.) The binder's own guide says it straight: one calm hour covers the contacts and as many document rows as the papers have surfaced, and the rest waits. A half-filled sheet this weekend beats a perfect sheet that never gets started.
  3. Last 4 digits and hints, never full numbers. (Opinion.) The binder runs on last 4 digits and hints on purpose, because sheets get seen by more people than their owner. Keep full account numbers and passwords in the password manager; the sheet only needs to say which account and where the real record lives.
  4. Reprint the sheet when it fills up. (Opinion.) Cross out and rewrite when one line changes. When the sheet itself runs out of room, print a fresh one: each page is a standalone sheet, and a clean reprint beats a cramped margin every time.
