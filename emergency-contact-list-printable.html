Emergency Contact List (Free Printable): One Sheet for Seniors and the People Who Help Them

When a phone is buried in a purse or the house is without power, the fridge and the wallet still work. This free printable puts the whole call list on one sheet of paper: eight family and friend slots, the doctor and pharmacy numbers, the person to call first, three neighbors, and a cut-out card for the wallet. Everything is blank and write-in, so the sheet works for any household.

How to use this page: print the sheet, fill in the names and numbers by hand, and put it where people look (most households pick the fridge). Cut out the wallet card at the bottom and slip it behind a license or transit pass. When a number changes, cross out one line, write the new number, and date it. This page holds phone numbers only; it is a household sheet, not a medical record.

Four tips for keeping the list current

Opinion, from one household sheet to another:

  1. One copy on the fridge, one in the wallet. The fridge copy is for anyone in the house; the wallet card (below) travels. Both get written in the same sitting, so they never disagree.
  2. Strike and rewrite, don't erase. A crossed-out number with the new one beside it keeps the change visible to everyone who uses the sheet. Erasing hides the change from the person who filled the sheet in the first place.
  3. Check the sheet on two dates a year. Attach the check to something already remembered: the daylight clock change works, birthdays work better. Two minutes per check: does each number still connect?
  4. Retire numbers that stopped answering. A dead number on an emergency sheet is a call that goes nowhere. If someone has moved on, cross the row out and write the next person in below it.
