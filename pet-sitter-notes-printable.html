4 opinions on leaving instructions a sitter will follow

  1. Leave two contacts, not one. (Opinion.) The owner's phone is the first number a sitter dials, and the first one that can be in a meeting or on a plane. A backup contact with the line "if nobody answers, do this" turns a dead hour of phone tag into one decision. The sheet's emergency box holds both for a reason.
  2. Fill the sheet sitting next to the owner, never from memory. (Opinion, straight from the pack's how-to: every empty box is a question to ask, not a guess to make.) Ten minutes of owner answers beats a page of sitter assumptions, and the ticking habit in the ask-the-owner list keeps the questions from being skipped.
  3. Write feeding exactly as the owner says it. (Opinion, from the pack's own note under the schedule: "write the schedule as the owner says it, not as any packet says it.") The sheet has no dosages on it on purpose - if the feeding row is unclear, the fix is a call to the owner, not a guess at the bag.
  4. Print a fresh sheet per booking and keep the filled ones. (Opinion, again from the pack's how-to, which says to keep one book per year and archive the filled ones.) A filled sheet is a record of what was actually said and what actually happened on the walks, which is exactly what you want in hand if a "you never told me that" conversation shows up later.
