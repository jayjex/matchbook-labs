A weekly rhythm to start from

Example pattern, opinion, overwrite it. Five rows because most people can keep five commitments; the blanks are yours to fill.

DaySlotYour angle (write in)
MondayTip
WednesdayBehind the scenes
ThursdayAnswer a real question you got this week
FridaySomething light
WeekendOff, or backlog prep only

Tip days work best when the tip is one you paid for with a mistake. Behind-the-scenes days need no new ideas, only honesty about what you did. The point of a rhythm is that Tuesday-you never has to invent anything from a blank page; Monday-you already decided the shape.

Four opinions on using the grid

  1. Batch one format per day. Fill every Monday cell with the same format and the whole day becomes production of one thing instead of context-switching between five. The grid makes the repetition visible on purpose.
  2. Write the hook first. A weak opening line makes the post weak no matter how good the asset is. Fill in the topic line as a hook, in the words you would actually say, before you make anything.
  3. Keep four status words on paper. Idea, draft, scheduled, published. Tick through them in the cell margin as work moves. The same flow that keeps a spreadsheet honest keeps a pen honest.
  4. Fifteen minutes a week, hooks only. Once a week, fill next week's topic lines and stop there. Drafting gets much faster when the hook already exists, and fifteen minutes is short enough that you will actually do it.

From paper to spreadsheet

The printable grid above is the planning step. When a month deserves tracking instead of pinning, the paper idea continues in a Content Calendar built for Google Sheets and Excel: thirteen tabs, one per month from January to December, one row per post. Each month tab has the same columns in this order: Date, Platform, Status, Format, Hook, Caption, Link, Notes.

Dropdowns keep the entries consistent, which is the spreadsheet's main advantage over handwriting. Platform dropdown values: Twitter/X, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, YouTube, Blog. Status runs idea, draft, scheduled, published, matching the four words from opinion three. Format covers Post, Thread, Reel, Carousel and more. January ships with 10 sample rows marked "SAMPLE row - replace with your own", and the Hook column sits before the Caption column on purpose, for the reason in opinion two.

Requirements are plain: Excel 2010 or newer, or a free Google account with Google Sheets. No macros, no add-ons, no external links.
