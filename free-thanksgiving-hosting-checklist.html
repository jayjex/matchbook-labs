The 18-item prep checklist: 3 days before, 1 day before, morning of

Eighteen items, six per phase. Every one is a decision you do not want to make with a house full of guests:

Thanksgiving hosting checklist

18 boxes · work top to bottom · add your own lines at the bottom

3 days before

  • Chase the missing RSVPs: mark the guest table below, then text the silent ones a yes-or-no question
  • Assign every dish you are not cooking; name the dish when you ask, so nobody volunteers "a little something"
  • Write the final menu and mark what can be made the day before
  • Shop the non-perishables: pantry staples, canned goods, baking supplies, drinks
  • Count chairs, plates, glasses, and forks against the final headcount; borrow the gap now, not at 4pm
  • Start the fridge thaw if you bought a frozen bird: about one day per 4 pounds

1 day before

  • Shop fresh: produce, dairy, bread, flowers
  • Make the make-ahead dishes; pies, casseroles, and cranberry sauce all keep overnight
  • Prep the vegetables and hold them in containers of water or covered bowls
  • Set the full table: linens, plates, glasses, centerpiece, seating cards
  • Chill the drinks and clear a shelf so the fridge door opens without an avalanche
  • Walk the guest route once: entry, coats, bathroom; fix what a stranger would trip over

Thanksgiving morning

  • Take make-ahead dishes out early so they reheat or serve at room temp
  • Start the bird, timed backward from dinner (worked example below)
  • Put out the grazing bowls: nuts, olives, something crunchy, placed away from the kitchen
  • Hand out door duty: one person greets, takes coats, pours drinks
  • Give the bathroom and entry a 10-minute reset
  • Stage the leftovers: containers labeled and stacked where guests can see them

5 anti-stress rules for the host

  1. Delegate one named job per adult guest. Door, drinks, dishes, or dessert. "Let me know if you need anything" has never once been taken up; a named job gets done.
  2. Make-ahead beats made-that-morning. Any dish that survives a night in the fridge moves to yesterday, and the morning list shrinks with it.
  3. Cook fewer dishes, cook them well. Six dishes that run out beat twelve where three come back untouched. Cut the menu before you cut quality.
  4. One list, one place. Menu, shopping, and seating live on paper in one spot. The version in your head loses to whoever asks you a question mid-mash.
  5. Plan the leftovers before you cook. Containers labeled and on display mean guests pack food without being asked, and you get your fridge back by Friday.

Common questions

When should I start this checklist?

Three days out, which is how the phases are split. If you cook for a dozen or more, push the non-perishable shop a week early; stores near big family tables empty out fast the week of.

How do I get guests to actually RSVP?

Give a deadline, ask in one channel, and make it a yes-or-no question. Silence past the deadline gets a direct text: "we're saving you two seats unless we hear otherwise." The bringing-a-dish ask rides along in the same message.

What happens when someone brings an extra person?

Set two slack seats when you count chairs on day 3, and pick one side dish that stretches in a pot. That is the whole defense, and it works.
