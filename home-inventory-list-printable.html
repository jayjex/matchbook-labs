Home Inventory List (Free): a Printable Household Inventory Template

This home inventory list prints on one page: room, item, serial or ID, estimated value, and a checkbox for when the photo is taken. Walk the house once, room by room, and the household inventory sits on paper instead of in your head. Every field is blank so you fill in your own rooms, items, and numbers.

How to use this page: print it, pick a room, and fill one row per item worth writing down. Tick the photo box only after the picture exists, then write where the list and the photos are kept in the box below the table. This is an organizational tool only, not legal advice: it records what a household owns and where the records sit, and it does not tell you what any policy covers.

The inventory table: 10 rows, room by room

One row per item, one room at a time. The room column keeps each row attached to a place, which is what makes the list easy to update later: new blender, kitchen row. Serial numbers go in for anything that has a plate or a sticker; everything else gets a short description good enough to point at.

Where the inventory is documented

A list nobody can find works like no list at all. Fill these four lines in once, the same day you print the sheet, and the answer to "where are the records" stops depending on memory.

4 tips for a home inventory that stays useful

Four habits from households whose sheets are still alive a year later. They are opinions, not rules.

  1. Photograph open drawers, not just rooms. A wide shot of a tidy bedroom proves less than a photo of the drawer with the camera in it. Open drawers, closets, and boxes before you shoot, and let one photo per item do the talking.
  2. One room per sitting. Whole-house inventory afternoons die somewhere around the hallway closet. A room a week finishes; a marathon rarely starts twice.
  3. Write the guess. Nobody remembers what the TV cost three years later, and "about $200, bought 2023" gives the row something to stand on. Round numbers are fine here; an empty value column is the only wrong answer.
  4. Re-shoot once a year. Pick a month you already remember and walk the house again with your phone. New purchases, moves, and renovations are the moments that quietly make old inventories wrong.

What this list does and does not do

An inventory makes the paperwork after a loss easier because the list and the photos already exist in one place, with numbers a person can point to. It does not change what any policy covers or pays. That answer is written in the policy itself, so read yours. No company names appear on this page. The sheet works with whichever paperwork your household already has.
