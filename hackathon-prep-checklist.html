Most hackathon prep lists tell you to sleep and drink water. This one is a working sheet instead: checkboxes, write-in lines, and three rules to keep open during the build. It stays generic on purpose. Pick your event from an organizer's page or a listing you trust, then let this page handle the week before, the weekend itself, and demo hour.

During the event — 3 rules worth taping to the wall

  1. Ship a vertical slice. One path through the product, working end to end, beats five half-built modules. Judges score what runs.
  2. Scope down, never up. Behind at the halfway point? Cut features, not sleep. A smaller finished thing beats a bigger broken one, and the decision gets harder every hour you delay it.
  3. Demo over docs. Record the working path on video while it works. Explain architecture only after the demo plays, and only if there's time.

4 opinions from people who've watched submissions land

These are opinions, not rules. Disagree freely.

  1. Sleep on schedule. All-nighters read as commitment at 2 a.m. and as sloppiness at demo time: wrong clicks, dead air, forgotten steps. A rested team presents better than a heroic one.
  2. Decide the demo before the code. Once you know the 30 seconds you'll show, you know exactly what to build. Building first and finding the demo later is how scope explosions start.
  3. Boring tech wins. The event weekend is the worst possible time to learn a framework. Build on the stack your team already knows, however unglamorous it looks in the README.
  4. Submit the ugly version. A rough project in before the cutoff beats a polished one that never lands. Submit early, polish if time remains, resubmit if the platform allows.
