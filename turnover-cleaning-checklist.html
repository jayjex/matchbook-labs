The turnover checklist

27 steps in four blocks plus a restock line list. Kitchen and bathrooms carry the most steps because that is where guest complaints start. Floors go last in every room. A full sheet usually takes one to two hours for a one-bedroom; write your own time next to the blocks until you know your pace.

Kitchen (7)

  • ☐ Dishes, glasses, and utensils washed or dishwasher run; cabinets checked for left-behinds
  • ☐ Counters, backsplash, and table wiped and dried
  • ☐ Sink scrubbed; faucet and drain polished, no standing water
  • ☐ Appliances wiped: stovetop, microwave inside and out, fridge shelves spot-checked
  • ☐ Coffee maker rinsed; carafe descaled on your own schedule
  • ☐ Trash emptied, liner replaced, bin wiped where needed
  • ☐ Floor swept and mopped, corners and under-table line included

Bathrooms (7)

  • ☐ Toilet cleaned: bowl, seat, outside, base, behind
  • ☐ Shower and tub scrubbed; glass squeegeed, streaks out
  • ☐ Vanity, sink, and faucet cleaned; mirror polished
  • ☐ Drains checked for hair and slow flow
  • ☐ Towels hung fresh: bath, hand, face per your setup
  • ☐ Exhaust fan and vent cover dusted
  • ☐ Floor swept and mopped, behind the toilet included

Bedrooms (7)

  • ☐ Linens stripped; bed made with clean sheets, corners taut
  • ☐ Mattress spot-checked; protectors changed on your own rotation
  • ☐ Pillows and duvet inspected, re-fluffed or replaced per your setup
  • ☐ Nightstands and dressers dusted; drawers left empty
  • ☐ Under-bed line checked with a flashlight
  • ☐ Closet wiped; hangers straightened
  • ☐ Floor vacuumed, edges and corners included

Living areas and exit (6)

  • ☐ Upholstery vacuumed or lint-rolled; cushions straightened
  • ☐ Hard surfaces dusted; remotes, switches, and handles wiped
  • ☐ Windows and mirrors spot-cleaned; sills wiped
  • ☐ All trash and recycling out; liners replaced everywhere
  • ☐ Restock list completed (below); caddy back in the closet
  • ☐ Walk-through with the sheet, lights off, doors locked

Restock line items

Tick a line only when the level matches your own minimum, not when the item merely exists. Anything short goes on the shopping line at the bottom.

  • ☐ Toilet paper, paper towels, tissues
  • ☐ Hand soap, dish soap, dishwasher pods
  • ☐ Trash liners, laundry bag or basket liner
  • ☐ Coffee, tea, sugar, creamer, filters
  • ☐ Clean linens and towels ready in the closet

Four opinions on tighter turnovers

These are opinions from working the sheet, not survey data. Take the ones that fit your unit and your helpers.

  1. Photograph every room after the clean. The photos take two minutes and settle disputes about condition, both from guests and from anyone who cleans for you. Date-stamp them and store them with the sheet or in a folder per turnover. After a few dozen, you also get a baseline: you will notice the scuff that was not there last month.
  2. Build one restock caddy per unit, not one per cleaner. A fixed caddy with set levels for paper, soap, coffee, and liners turns the restock list above into a two-minute check instead of a memory test. When a level drops below your minimum, the shopping line gets written the same day, not discovered mid-turnover.
  3. Clean top to bottom, back to front. Dust falls; you walk yourself out the door. Counters before floors, bedroom corners before the entry hall, bathrooms before you mop your way to the exit. The order matters more than speed: redoing a floor you already mopped is the slowest thing in the whole sheet.
  4. Walk the unit once more with the sheet, not with your eyes. The last walk-through is a checklist read, not a glance. Tick each block closed, then check the traps guests notice first: entry, bathroom glass, bed corners, bin liners. Cleanliness is the complaint guests write about most, and the last two minutes of the walk are where most of it gets caught.
