Office Cleaning Checklist (Free Printable, 2 Pages)

An office collects cleaning jobs quietly: a bin nobody owns, a kitchen everyone uses, a meeting room nobody notices until a client sits down. This free office cleaning checklist puts each area on one line. Page one is an 8-row write-in table: the area, the tasks it needs, a frequency circle (D daily, W weekly, M monthly) and a done circle. Page two covers the two spots that slip first: a shared-kitchen box with four lines and a restock box with three. Write-in only, no software, prints tonight on A4 or US Letter. It doubles as a workplace cleaning schedule template, free printable, one sheet per week.

How to use this page: print at 100% scale (the site chrome drops out on paper). Page one is the map: one row per area, the area and its owner written in, the tasks listed in plain words, and a frequency circle set once. Work down the rows each day the sheet is out and circle done when the row is finished for that frequency. Page two is the weekly side: four lines for shared-kitchen jobs and three lines for restock items, both filled in by you. Keep the sheet somewhere visible and print a fresh one whenever the lines fill. For a full week grid, the free weekly cleaning schedule template lays cleaning days out across Mon-Sun.

Page one: the area checklist, 8 rows

Eight rows, one per area. The area cell takes the name and, when the sheet is shared, the person who owns it: "Reception - Dana" reads faster than a separate owner column would. The tasks cell holds what the area needs, in the words your own walkthrough produces: bins, door glass, front desk. Frequency is one letter circled once, D for daily, W for weekly, M for monthly, and it stays circled until the area's needs change. The done column is a single circle: circle it when the row's tasks are finished, leave it alone until they are. Row 01 is a filled example; overwrite it with your own areas.

Page two: the shared kitchen box and the restock box

Two boxes, because most workplace checklists fail in the same two places. The shared-kitchen box holds four lines for the jobs that keep the room usable: write each job and the day it happens, one per line, in whatever words your office uses. The restock box holds three lines for supplies you check on the same weekly walk: write the item and the level that triggers a reorder. Both boxes are write-in on purpose. The kitchen jobs in your office and the supplies your rooms run through are yours to list; this sheet schedules the work, it does not instruct any method, and anything you already use stays governed by its own product label.

Four opinions on running an office cleaning checklist

Opinions from building record-keeping pages like this one, not survey data. Take or leave:

  1. (Opinion.) One owner per area. A row without a name waits; a row with a name gets circled. Ownership can rotate, but only when the row itself says so, never by assumption.
  2. (Opinion.) Circle the frequency once and review it quarterly. D/W/M is a setting, not a weekly decision; a scheduled review changes it deliberately, and everything in between just runs.
  3. (Opinion.) Walk the sheet at the same time every week. A fixed end-of-week pass finds the uncircled done boxes before the weekend, and it finds them in the same order each time, which is what makes misses visible.
  4. (Opinion.) Write the restock lines during the same walk. Counting supplies while you are already in the room turns buying into one stop, and it beats a note that lives on someone's monitor until the item runs out.
