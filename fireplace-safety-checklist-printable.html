Free Fireplace Safety Checklist (Printable, 1 Page)

One printable page for the heating season. This free fireplace safety checklist covers four areas: chimney and appliance service, detector tests, the hearth zone, and household habits. You also get a 12-month smoke and CO test grid, a seasonal deep-check box, and a blank emergency numbers card. Every check that depends on your specific appliance points to the manufacturer's manual, because clearances and service intervals are set there, not on this page.

This is a routine sheet, not a safety guide. It keeps track of what you did and when. It does not tell you what is safe for your specific fireplace, stove, or heater. Follow your appliance manual and local rules for clearances, service intervals, and anything else the manual covers. If you are unsure about a condition, the company that services your appliance can answer it.

Hearth zone (4)

What belongs near your hearth is a manual question first. Every stove and fireplace lists its own requirements; keep clear per the manual, then check the zone with these four.

  • ☐ Zone around the appliance kept clear per the manual's own stated requirements — not this page's numbers
  • ☐ Nothing stored on, against, or hanging over the appliance or its surfaces, including décor that migrated there
  • ☐ Tools, screens, and doors in working order as the manual describes them
  • ☐ Floor path to the appliance clear, and anything kids or pets knock over relocated out of the zone

Household habits (4)

Neutral routines that make the rest of the sheet easier to keep. Adjust to your household.

  • ☐ One person owns the monthly test: same day each month, grid filled in before anything else
  • ☐ Matches, lighters, and fireplace tools live in one fixed spot, returned after each use
  • ☐ Ash and fuel handling done the way your manual describes, with the container the manual recommends
  • ☐ Everyone in the house knows where the emergency numbers card below is posted

Four routine tips

Four opinions from keeping this sheet going, not survey data. Take what fits.

  1. Same day every month, or it drifts. Rent day, the first Sunday, the 1st. A test attached to a date happens; a test attached to "sometime this month" slides into next month and stays there.
  2. The grid is the record. When someone asks when the detectors were last tested, the answer is a filled circle, not a memory. Twelve boxes per year per detector, and a blank page at the end of the year is its own signal.
  3. Write the service dates in pen the day they happen. "Last serviced" is the question every sweep asks first. A written date makes the next booking a thirty-second call instead of a search through email.
  4. Print two copies: one for the wall near the appliance, one spare. The wall copy is where the grid gets filled. The spare covers next January, or the copy that meets a spilled drink.
