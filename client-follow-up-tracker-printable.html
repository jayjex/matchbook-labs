Client Follow Up Tracker (Free Printable)

Freelance clients rarely close the door; the thread just goes quiet. A proposal nobody circled back on, a check-in promised after a launch, a lead met once and never contacted again. This page gives those threads one place to land, on paper. Print two pages free: a 10-row client list with last-touch dates and next-touch circles, then an 8-row follow-up log with a channel column and a weekly goal line. Write-in only, no spreadsheet needed, prints tonight.

How to use this page: print at 100% scale (site chrome drops out on paper). Page one is the client list: one row per client, alias and project, the date you last touched them, a circle for when the next touch happens (this week or later), and a status letter. Page two is the follow-up log: one row per actual touch, with the date, the alias, the channel you used, and a short note. Print a fresh pair each week or let the log run across weeks, your call.

Page one: the client list, 10 rows

Ten rows covers the clients a one-person operation is actively keeping warm, and a page you can see whole beats a database you have to open. Each row takes an alias you pick, two or three letters, plus the project the client is attached to. The last-touch column is the honest one: the date you last actually contacted them, not the date you last thought about it. Then two circles for the next touch (this week, or later) and four letters for status: A Active, P Paused, C Completed, L Lead. Page two: the follow-up log, 8 rows

The list says where every relationship stands; the log says what you did about it. One row per touch: the date, the alias, the channel (email, call, or whatever you actually used), and a note short enough to fit the row. The note is for the promise, not the transcript: what you said you would send, and when. The goal line at the bottom is the part that turns the page from a record into a habit, and it is one line on purpose. When a touch belongs to a client on page one, use the same alias on both pages, and the pair reads as one document.

Four opinions on following up

Opinions from building client-organization pages like this one. Take or leave:

  1. Touch five clients a day, and count touches, not replies. Five is our pick: small enough to actually happen on a workday, big enough that an eight-row log fills by midweek, so print two copies. Counting replies instead puts the number in the client's hands, and a slow week reads as failure even when you did your part.
  2. Circle next-touch while the conversation is still open. Decide this week or later at the end of the call, before you hang up, and the circle on page one writes itself. Deciding later from memory turns every next-touch into later, and the wk circles stay empty for exactly the clients you meant to email.
  3. Write the channel even when it feels obvious. Today you know the thread was an email. In three weeks, spoke is a rumor and a channel is a fact, and the note column only makes sense next to it. Two words per row buys the whole log back.
  4. Let status letters move, and let them move honestly. A client marked A with no log row in a month is Paused whether you admit it or not; the page just makes the gap visible. Recopying the list weekly is the moment to change letters, because a fresh sheet has no old circles to defend.

What this page is

A relationship-organization sheet. It records who you last talked to, when, through which channel, and what you said would happen next, so the state of your client list is whatever you wrote on it. Whether a client replies, hires you, or goes quiet is up to the client; the pages promise no outcome, they only keep the threads where you can see them. Nothing here is business, legal, or tax advice.
