Moving Abroad Cost Checklist (Free): the One-Page Expat Budget Sheet

The money part of a move abroad has a shape: a pile of one-time costs, then a slower stream of monthly ones, and nothing to compare either against until you arrive. This expat budget planner is one printable page with both streams side by side - twelve cost rows to fill in, a first-90-days box, and one line for the exchange rate you plan around. Every amount is yours to write in; the page has no numbers on purpose. Still on the paper trail? The documents checklist for moving abroad sits next to this one.

How to use this page: print it, write each cost in as your research turns it up, and date the sheet - prices move. A row with no answer yet stays empty, and the empty row is information. Fees, deposits, and what exists on your route at all vary by country - check official government sources, and treat this page as a place to organize what you find, not a source itself.

The cost checklist: 12 rows

One row per cost category. The one-time column takes costs you expect to pay once; the recurring column takes costs that repeat every month. Most rows fill only one column, and leaving the other blank is the honest answer until your research says otherwise.

One-time vs recurring, and why the split matters

The two columns are the whole trick. One-time costs hit before your first paycheck in the new country: flights, shipping, deposits, setup. Recurring ones decide whether month three is calm or a scramble. A move can absorb a big one-time total and still lose to its own monthly bills, which is why the first-90-days box counts months, not weeks.

The exchange rate line

One rate, one date, one named source. Rates move and forum quotes drift, so write the rate you will plan around:

Exchange rate used on this sheet: 1 ____________ = ____________ ____________
Source: ____________ (an official central bank or your bank's published rate)  ·  looked up on (date) ____________

4 habits from moves done the expensive way

  1. Add your own buffer to the one-time total - we would say 20%, but pick a number you can defend - the buffer is room for the fee nobody mentioned.
  2. Fill the recurring column for three months, not one. Ninety days is the shortest window that shows whether the plan survives real rent, real food prices, and month two.
  3. Write amounts in the destination's currency, with your home currency next to them where it matters. Conversions hide fees, and logging both keeps the fees visible.
  4. Chase every deposit in writing, starting with the first one you paid. A deposit that comes back is part of the budget; one you forget about is not.

No numbers on purpose

Costs differ per country, per city, and per route, and they change - this page cannot know what your move costs, so it carries no numbers at all. Fill every row from your own research and official sources: the destination's official government portals, your airline, your bank's published rate card. Nothing on this page is financial, legal, or immigration advice; it is a sheet for organizing what you find out.
