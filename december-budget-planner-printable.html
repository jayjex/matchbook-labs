4 opinions on running a December budget

  1. Set the per-person cap before writing a single gift row. A cap turns "how much should I spend on Mom" from an open question into a closed one. Pick one number per person, write it in the budget column, and let the row argue with the cap instead of the other way around. In my experience the cap wins, and the list gets shorter, and nobody remembers the difference by January.
  2. Keep food and gifts in separate boxes. Hosting costs get blamed on gift overspending every year, because both hit the same card in the same week. Two boxes on one sheet means the grocery run for the family dinner never silently eats the gift budget. If food wins one month, cut from food, out loud, on paper.
  3. Write spent numbers the same day you spend. A receipt in a coat pocket is a number you'll never recover. The spent column only works if it's filled while the bag is still in your hand. Two minutes at the door beats a half-remembered reconstruction on the 30th.
  4. Check the sheet once a week, same day every week. A budget checked daily gets abandoned by December 10th; one checked at the end gets checked never. One sitting, once a week, with the sheet in front of you: re-read every spent column, glance at the planned-vs-actual box, and adjust while there's still month left to adjust in.
