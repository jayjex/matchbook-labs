Four opinions on keeping a walking log

  1. (Opinion.) Keep one route boring on purpose. A loop you can walk half-asleep is what keeps the early slot alive on a bad week. Mix in a new street when the day has slack, and the log's route column will show the mix by itself: mostly the dependable loop, sometimes something new.
  2. (Opinion.) Fill the row the same day, in one line. "One line per walk, same day" is the rule the client book ships with, and it holds on a single sheet too. The row written at 8 am takes ten seconds; the batch reconstructed on Saturday takes ten minutes and still gets the times wrong.
  3. (Opinion.) Write the duration that really happened. The plan may have said 30 minutes; the row is for what the clock says. After two weeks the gap between planned and real is the most useful pattern on the page, because it shows which slot always runs short and should be rescheduled.
  4. (Opinion.) Log the short days too. The product's walk-log page says it in a green banner: rain and rest days are information for the owner, not missed work. A row that reads "10 min, cut short, thunder" explains the week; a blank row explains nothing.

What this page is

A care-organization log. It records walks: when, how long, where, and what the notes saw. It holds no guidance on health, feeding, or how far any dog should walk, and no claims about breeds; those live in the owner's written instructions, and medical questions go to the owner and their clinic. What the sheet does do is shorten the handover: the owner reads the week off page 2 in a minute instead of asking for it, and the sitter starts the next visit from facts instead of memory.
