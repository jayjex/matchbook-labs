Conference Packing List (Free Printable): a 2-Page Checklist Template for the Bag, the Badge, and the Day Before

Packing for a conference is a different job from packing for a trip: the bag is smaller and everything that matters fits on one list. Forget socks and the hotel gift shop can help; forget the charger, the badge, or the business cards and the day gets harder at exactly the wrong moment. This conference packing list is a free printable two-page set built for that job. Page one is a bag table with 8 write-in item rows, each with a packed circle and a notes column. Page two covers the small stuff that carries the day: a badge and business cards box with 3 lines, a day-before box with 3 lines, and 4 opinion tips, each labeled as an opinion. If you want the event side of the same problem, the hackathon prep checklist covers prep through demo day, the hackathon calendar template tracks deadlines, and the web3 event calendar guide compares ways to pick what to attend.

How to use this page: print both pages at 100% scale. On page one, one row per item: write the item, circle Packed when it is in the bag, and use Notes for whatever the row waits on, like a charger that is currently charging the phone it charges, or a card that is still at the printer. Nothing on the table is pre-filled, because the items that matter depend on your bag, your role at the event, and your floor plan; write your own rows. On page two, the badge box holds the pocket where the badge and cards live, the day-before box holds the things that only work if they happen the night before, and the tips are opinions you are free to argue with.

4 habits, all opinions

  1. Charge everything the night before. (Opinion.) Laptop, phone, spare battery, and whatever else dies by mid-morning. Charging in the morning competes with leaving, and the 40% you leave with does not survive the first session.
  2. Put the badge and the cards in the same pocket. (Opinion.) Two pockets means two things to forget. One pocket means the morning check is one gesture, and the badge box on this page gives it an address.
  3. Fill the table the week before, not the morning of. (Opinion.) A list written in a rush drops the boring rows, and the boring rows, chargers and cables and printed copies, are the ones that are hard to fix once the day starts.
  4. Leave half the bag empty on the way there. (Opinion.) Stickers, totes, and paper handouts pile up faster than you plan for, and row 8 is easier to deal with when the return trip has room in it.

Check what the event itself asks for. What belongs in the bag changes with the format: some conferences run on a laptop and a badge, some are online and the packing is a desk and a cable, and some organizers publish their own attendee guidance. The answer lives on the organizer's page, not on a printable. This sheet records what you decide to bring; it does not decide it.

These two pages are a packing sheet: item lines, packed circles, and boxes. They hold no event recommendations, no registration or ticket information, and no travel advice, and nothing here promises attendance, a spot, or a win at any event.
