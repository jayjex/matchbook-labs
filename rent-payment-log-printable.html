Rent Payment Log Printable

Rent is the same number every month, which is exactly why it is easy to lose track of the details around it: the day it actually left your account, whether it went by bank transfer or cash, what happened to the deposit. This page gives those details one place to land, on paper. Print two pages free: a 12-row rent log with the amount, the paid date, the payment method and a circle for paid, then a deposit record box, a lease-renewal note and a maintenance-request log. Write-in only, no software, prints tonight.

How to use this page: print at 100% scale (site chrome drops out on paper). Page one is the payment log: one row per month, twelve rows to a sheet. Page two holds the long-lived facts: the deposit you paid in, when your lease renews, and the repair requests you have made. Page one gets replaced every year; page two stays in the folder for the whole tenancy. Print a fresh log sheet when the rows run out and keep the old ones.

Page one: the rent log, 12 rows

Twelve rows is a year of rent, one row per month. Nothing is pre-filled: no sample amounts, no dates someone else picked. Write the month, the amount you actually paid, the date the payment went through, and how it moved: bank, cash, or other. The method column matters more than it looks. A rent history that reads "bank, bank, cash, bank" is easy to match against a bank statement later; a page that says nothing but check marks is not. Circle paid in the last column only when the money has actually left, not when you scheduled it. A scheduled transfer and a cleared payment are different facts, and this sheet keeps them separate.

Four opinions on running the log

Opinions from building paperwork pages like this one. Take or leave:

  1. Pick one rent day and pay on it every month. A fixed day makes the log boring, and boring is the point: a row per month with no gaps, written while you still remember which method it went by. The date you scheduled the payment and the date it cleared can differ; this sheet wants the cleared date.
  2. Log the payment the day it clears. Half a minute, once a month. Rebuilding a year of rent dates from a bank statement at move-out takes an evening, and cash payments never show up in the rebuild at all.
  3. Keep the method column honest. The generic labels exist so the sheet works no matter how the money moves: bank, cash, other. Writing cash as cash is what makes the row matchable to a receipt later. A log that flatters the method column is a log that cannot be checked.
  4. Keep the deposit box with your move-in photos. The deposit line and the inspection pictures tell one story, so file them together. When the deposit comes back, write the date and circle it the same day; the box is finished when the circle is filled.

Check your lease, not this page

What this sheet does not do is tell you what your deposit rules are, because that lives in your lease and in the rules where you rent, not in a printable. The kit it comes from says the same thing:

Rental rules differ by state, province and country. This kit is a paperwork organizer, not legal advice: check your local tenancy laws and your lease.

What this page is

A renter-organization page. It gives rent payments, one deposit, a renewal date and a handful of repair requests a place to be written down and kept together, for one tenancy, from keys to move-out. Nothing here is legal advice, deposit advice, or a claim about how any tenancy is supposed to work, and no line on these pages means anything beyond what you wrote in. For anything with consequences, read your lease and check your local rules.
