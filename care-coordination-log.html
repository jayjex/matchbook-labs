Caregiver Coordination Log (Free): Daily Care Notes, Med Schedule, and Care Team Contacts

When several people help with one parent, the hard part is the handoff: what changed today, who came by, which supply ran out. This free printable coordination log gives the family one place for all of it. Everything is write-in, so it works whether your parent takes one medication or ten, and nothing here is about an illness or a prognosis. It is a household notebook, not a medical record.

How to use this page: print the daily care page once per day or per week, keep the contact sheet on the fridge or in the notebook, and let whoever notices something write it down. Two minutes at the end of a visit beats a long form nobody fills. If a doctor, nurse, or aide gives instructions, follow what they say; this log is for your family's own coordination.

Daily care log: one page per day

One page, one day, five things on it. The date first, then a quick check-in on mood and energy by circling 1 through 5 (you can circle two numbers when the morning and evening looked different, which happens more than people expect). Then the notes that actually prevent the classic family miscommunication: what was eaten, what appointments happened or are coming, and five lines of free notes for whatever the day brought. A fall, a good phone call, a broken lamp: it all goes in the five lines.

Medication schedule: checkboxes your family fills in

This grid deliberately has no dose column and no med names printed. Copy the schedule from the pharmacy label, the med list from the doctor, or the pill organizer itself, and write those in the "med" column yourself. The grid only tracks whether a dose was handled, and the grid row for today gets ticked when it was. Labels change, and a printed name gone stale is worse than a blank one. When anything about a medication is unclear, the answer is the pharmacist or the prescribing office, not a guess.

Four tips for the family caregiver

Opinion, from one household notebook to another:

  1. Rotate the visits, don't stack them. Three people each showing up on Sunday and nobody on Wednesday is a pattern worth breaking. Spread the week out and put the schedule where everyone can see it, so each visit also covers the check-in for the last one.
  2. Keep one notebook, not five apps. A paper log gets picked up by whoever walks in, works during a power cut, and can be handed to a substitute caregiver exactly as it is. Apps die when only one person has the login.
  3. Write it the same day. A note written at 8pm is trusted by everyone; a note reconstructed three days later starts an argument. Two minutes, five lines, done.
  4. Plan for the substitute caregiver before you need one. The daily routine, the contact sheet, and where the key lives, written down once. When the regular person gets sick or stuck in traffic, the day still runs.
