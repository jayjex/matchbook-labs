Budget priorities, written by you

No suggested numbers on this one. The same checklist in every city, venue, and headcount produces wildly different totals, so any figure printed here would be a guess. Sort your own spending into three piles and write the lines in:

PriorityYour line (write it in)What tends to fit there
Must-haveThe things the day falls apart without: the venue, the food, the person capturing it
Nice-to-haveUpgrades you'd enjoy but can trade away: extra florals, a bigger dessert table
SkipThings guests barely register: favors nobody takes home, add-on print packages

When a new idea shows up mid-planning, ask which pile it belongs in before you look at what it costs. That ordering is what the priority table is for.

Four opinions on order of operations

  1. Book the venue before anything else. The date, the guest ceiling, and most caterer options all inherit from that one booking. In the planner's own countdown sheet, "Set date and book venue" is the first row for a reason.
  2. Draft the guest list before you fill in budget lines. Headcount moves nearly every other number, and a list you trim early saves renegotiating things later.
  3. Track deposits owed, not just agreed prices. The number that bites is the balance still due, which is why the vendor sheet in the planner carries a separate deposits-paid and balance-due column.
  4. Count plus-ones when you plan seats. A yes with a plus-one is two chairs, and a seating chart that ignores this runs out of tables at the worst time.
