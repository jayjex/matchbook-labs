Family Recipe Card Template

A family recipe card template does one job: give each dish a card of its own, with room for the two things recipes usually lose, the amounts and the person they came from. This recipe card template is free and printable on two pages: page one is the full card layout, page two is a spare card with a source line for who it came from. Both are write-in, nothing is pre-filled, and both print tonight on A4 or US Letter.

How to use this page: print at 100% scale (the site chrome drops out on paper). Page one is the card you fill in first, page two is the spare for the next dish. One recipe per card. When the cards pile up, the recipe card organization sheet and the single-page recipe template take over from there.

Page one: the full card

The layout runs top to bottom the way a cook reads it. Title line first, then serves and time, then ingredients down the left and steps down the right, eight ingredient lines and six step lines. The lines are blank on purpose: no sample dish, no measurements someone else chose. A card that holds your aunt's stew should look the same before you start as a card that holds a two-ingredient glaze.

Four opinions on filling the cards

Opinions from building recipe pages, take or leave:

  1. Opinion. One card, one recipe. A card that holds two dishes files badly, photocopies badly, and gets found half as fast. Two cards beat one crowded one every time.
  2. Opinion. Fill the source line even when everyone already knows the story. "Everyone" shrinks, and the line is the only place the name gets written down.
  3. Opinion. Eight ingredient lines and six steps are enough for most dishes. A recipe that runs longer should take two cards anyway; squeezing it onto one is how "a pinch" becomes unreadable.
  4. Opinion. Leave the year off the card. A card without a date works for thirty years; a card stamped 2026 starts to look expired long before the recipe does.

Straight from the recipe binder README

Every card is blank on purpose. Recipes live in your handwriting, so nothing here reprints a cookbook, a website, or anyone's family card, and no brand or family name is printed anywhere. What the binder adds is the part recipes usually lose: who it came from, the story on the card, and an index so the whole household can use it.

Handwrite the first card with the oldest cook in the room and let them tell the story lines out loud. Ask for the missing measurements while you can; a pinch, written down, outlives a pinch remembered.

This binder is a write-in organizational tool. It stores no recipes, gives no nutrition or food-safety guidance, and replaces nothing your doctor or your grandmother told you.
