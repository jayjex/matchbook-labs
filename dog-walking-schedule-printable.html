Dog Walking Schedule (Free Printable)

A one-page pet care routine chart: seven days by three walk slots (morning, afternoon, evening) with the walk length left blank to write in, one labeled example row, and a 31-day monthly tracker underneath. There is also a short daily care checklist, a notes box for the dog walker, and 4 tips for keeping the routine alive past week two. No email, no sign-up, print it as-is.

How to use the chart: write a time and a walk length in each slot, for example "7:10, 20 min". The times that work are the ones that survive your worst Tuesday, so schedule around school runs and meetings, not around the ideal day. Post the sheet where the leash lives, and fill the tracker circle the moment you get back; same-day marks are the ones that actually get recorded.

Before you start: this is generic household-routine advice, not veterinary advice. How far and how long to walk depends on age, breed, and health, so ask your vet what fits your dog. In hot weather, use the weather note below and keep walks short.

Reading the example row: each slot gets a clock time and a length, and the afternoon slot is deliberately the short one. A schedule with three 30-minute walks every day looks great on Sunday night and dies by Wednesday, so give one slot permission to be small. Days that get missed entirely usually had no small option to fall back on.

Walker notes

For the dog walker or the neighbor with the spare key

4 Tips for a Walk Routine That Sticks

  1. Same times, on purpose. A walk at 7:00 becomes an alarm at 7:00 within about a week, and that alarm goes off whether or not you are ready for it. Decide now if the times on your chart are times you can hold on a bad day, because the dog will hold you to them.
  2. Rotate three routes. The same loop every day turns into a march for both of you. Three routes on rotation keeps the sniffing interesting, and a new route is also the easiest "extra enrichment" you can give for free.
  3. Tight lead means stop. When the leash goes taut, plant your feet and wait; when it slackens, walk on. It is slower for the first two days and faster forever after, because the dog learns that a loose lead is what makes the walk move.
  4. Fill the tracker the same day. A circle drawn at 6:45 am takes five seconds. The backlog you plan to reconstruct on Saturday never gets reconstructed, and then the sheet stops being trusted, and then the sheet stops being used.

Hot day: press the back of your hand flat on the pavement for five seconds. If you cannot hold it there, the ground will burn paws; walk earlier, walk later, or keep it to grass and shorten the midday slot instead of skipping it.
