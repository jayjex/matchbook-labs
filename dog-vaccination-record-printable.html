4 opinion tips for keeping the record

Opinions, labeled as such. They held up in our house; take or leave:

  1. Photograph the paper record after each visit. (Opinion.) The clinic's paper is the original and the one most likely to disappear. A photo in your phone backs it up in five seconds, and the sheet stays the working copy you write on.
  2. Copy entries over the same day. (Opinion.) Clinic handwriting and same-day memory both fade, and they fade together. Copying the row that evening keeps dates and names exact instead of half-remembered.
  3. Take the next-due date only from the paper. (Opinion.) If the clinic's paper lists one, copy it; if it does not, leave the cell blank rather than guessing. A record you invented is worse than an empty cell.
  4. Keep the sheet in one known spot. (Opinion.) A record that lives sometimes in the drawer and sometimes on the counter stops being findable. One spot, always, beats a better spot that changes.

What this page is

A record-keeping sheet. It holds dates, names, weights, and one clinic line that you write in from your dog's own papers. It is not veterinary advice: there is no vaccination schedule here, no recommendation about which vaccines a dog should have or when, and no medical claim of any kind. The columns only make sense filled in from what your clinic gave you; anything about your dog's health belongs to your vet.
