Four opinions on prep day

  1. (Opinion.) Prep two proteins, not four. The container count is the constraint most kitchens actually feel: four parallel batches want more clean containers, more counter space, and more of one session than a first prep day usually has. Two batches that both get stored beat four that stop halfway. The 8-row table exists because a plan grows; start it at two rows and let the count climb on later sheets.
  2. (Opinion.) Count containers before you cook, not after. The first checklist box is container work on purpose. A batch with nowhere to go sits in the pot while you hunt cupboard space, and the session stalls at its most crowded moment. The count is a five-minute job the night before and the difference between a session that runs and one that improvises.
  3. (Opinion.) Split the list by store section, not by recipe. A list written recipe-by-recipe sends you back down the same aisle four times. Three columns, one pass each: produce, protein, pantry. This is also why the workbook's Grocery List sheet sorts by category instead of by dish; the printable and the spreadsheet fail the same way if the store decides your walking order.
  4. (Opinion.) End the session on the checklist, not on feel. "Until the kitchen looks done" is a moving target that extends itself. The last box is stored-batches-tick-their-rows, and it either happens or it does not. A session with a fixed end gets repeated next week; one that ends on exhaustion does not.

What this page is

A prep-organization sheet, and nothing past that. It plans one cooking session on paper: which recipes go in, how much of each, how many containers to wash, and a slot for where each batch lands when it is done. No nutrition guidance appears anywhere on it, no portion advice, no diet framing, and no storage rule or duration; the Storage column is a write-in that holds your decision, not the sheet's. What the sheet does do is make prep day a scheduled job with a countable output: 8 planned rows, 18 grocery lines, 5 boxes. When a row's numbers stay blank, that recipe was not actually planned, and the sheet tells you that before Sunday afternoon finds out.
