Sheet 1: Tier List, 5 Tiers × 6 Lines

A tier is a group of players you would draft at the same pick without blinking. Write one name per line, best player first, roughly in the order you want them. When a tier runs out mid-draft, that is your signal to reach one spot early or trade down. The labels stay generic (TIER 1 through TIER 5) so the sheet works for any format and any sport your league drafts.

Fill the Tiers the Night Before

Ranking 200 players takes hours and goes stale the moment news drops. Tiers take 20 minutes and survive. Pull up your league's player list, write names into the tier lines in the order you value them, and stop: the top line of TIER 1 is your clear best, and TIER 5 is depth you would rather not start. Cross names off the sheet as they get drafted so the next read of the page shows the live board, not the paper one from an hour ago.

Bring your own rankings: every player line is blank, so the sheets work with any league size format, any scoring system, and any site you draft on.

Four Tips for Using the Sheets

Wait on your favorite team

(Opinion.) You watch every snap that roster plays, so every name on it looks a tier better than the rest of the league sees it. Let another manager pay the fandom premium in round 3, and take your guy two rounds later when the price is honest.

Six lines is a cap, not a target

(Opinion.) A tier that needs more than six names is two tiers wearing one label. Split it, even if that means the table needs a second copy. A tier you can scan in three seconds is the tool; a tier you have to read is decoration.

Keepers before tiers

(Opinion.) Fill the keepers watch box first. Every keeper crosses a name off the tier list before the draft starts, and finding that out mid-draft wastes a line you already planned around.

Log picks during the round

(Opinion.) Three picks fly by between rounds in a fast draft. Writing during the round is the only way the log stays complete; catching up at the round break is where mistakes come from.
