Exam Study Schedule Template (Free Study Plan Printable, 2 Pages)

Two weeks out, an exam feels roomy. Five days out, three subjects are fighting over the same evening, and the fight eats the evening. This free exam study schedule template settles it on paper: page 1 holds the exam info and ten study blocks with a done circle per block, page 2 keeps a weak-topics box, a one-line note on breaks, and a six-item day-before checklist. Write-in only, no sign-up, prints at 100% scale on A4 or US Letter.

How to use this page: print both pages at 100% scale (the site chrome drops out on paper). Fill the exam-info box first: subject, date, and the topics the course actually lists. Then split the days you have left into blocks on the table, one row per block with a day, a time, and a topic. Shade the done circle when the block happens. Page 2 works between sessions. Reprint the set for the next exam; the shape stays the same.

The two-page study schedule

Nothing is pre-filled. The topic column stays blank because a schedule copied from someone else's exam belongs to someone else's exam. Ten rows per page keeps the sheet to one side of paper; a topic that needs two blocks takes two rows, and the done circle still fills one per block. Day and time are write-in on purpose: a block you chose for Tuesday at 7 survives contact with the actual Tuesday more often than a generic "study more" line ever has.

Page 2: weak topics, breaks, the day before

The weak-topics box is the honest page: four lines for the topics you would rather not meet on the day, written while the material is still open in front of you. Pulling blocks from this box first, while energy is still yours to spend, is the whole trick, and the sheet labels it an opinion rather than a law. The breaks note holds one line. The day-before checklist is pure logistics, six boxes, and the point of it is waking up with nothing left to decide except the topics.

Four opinions on study schedules

Opinions from keeping sheets like this, take or leave:

  1. Short blocks win. (Opinion.) A block you actually sit down for beats the four-hour block you keep rescheduling. The table carries ten rows because ten small blocks fill more real evenings than two giant ones do.
  2. Weak topics get the first block of the day. (Opinion.) The end of a study day is where good intentions run out. One weak-topic block while you are fresh is worth three scheduled for "later".
  3. Shade the done circles honestly, including the misses. (Opinion.) A week of fully shaded circles tells you nothing. The unshaded rows show where the plan and the week disagreed, and that disagreement is the information the next schedule needs.
  4. Rebuild the sheet once a week, same day every week. (Opinion.) Fifteen minutes with the weak-topics box open, ten fresh rows, old sheet to the recycling. The cadence matters more than which day you pick.
