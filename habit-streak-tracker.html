Missed a day: the 3-step reset

An empty box is a data point, and the reset below takes five minutes the next morning. The only expensive mistake is the silent one, where the sheet goes in a drawer and the habit quietly ends with it:

  1. Mark the box honestly. Leave it empty or write what happened. One honest gap costs one square, and the dates around it still tell you when and why.
  2. Choose continue or restart, once. Continue: the chain resets, you keep the same grid and fill tomorrow's box. Restart: print a fresh sheet, same habit line, new start date. Pick one in the morning, write the date, and skip the relitigation at 11pm.
  3. Reset the anchor, not the goal. Put back whatever was supposed to trigger the habit: shoes by the door, book on the pillow, jar on the counter. Then stop reviewing the miss. One gap is a square; three in a week is a pattern the reflection box will catch on Sunday.

4 rules that keep a streak alive

Opinions from running these grids, in order of how much they change the outcome:

  1. Anchor the habit to something already scheduled. "Ten pushups after I pour coffee" survives; "do pushups sometime today" dies in week one. The anchor carries the reminder, and the grid only records what the anchor produced.
  2. Set the bar low enough that a bad day still counts. A five-minute walk keeps the chain alive when the day goes sideways. If your minimum only fits a perfect day, the streak breaks on the first hard Tuesday, and most days have some hardness in them. Raise the bar after 30 boxes, not before.
  3. One habit per grid. A shared sheet hides which habit is carrying the streak and which is coasting. Give each habit its own row (the mini version below handles three) or its own page.
  4. Grow the longest-streak number, not the box total. A sheet with two long chains and a gap in the middle shows more than a perfect grid you abandoned on day 9. At the end of a month, compare longest streaks across sheets: that number, and how fast it recovers after a miss, is the actual progress metric.

Common questions

How do I fill a box?

Shade it solid, tick it, or write the number of minutes in it. The one convention worth keeping: full box means the habit happened by the rule on the top line, empty means it did not. Partial credit lives in the date line, not the box.

Should I track three habits on the mini or three sheets?

Start with one habit on the big grid. If by day 10 it runs without effort, move it to a mini row and promote a second habit to the full grid. The mini is also the ink-saving option: one page covers three habits for a month.

What if the habit only happens on weekdays?

Write the schedule into the habit line: "gym, Mon to Fri". Weekend boxes get marked in advance as rest days, usually with a dash, so they never read as a miss when you look at the grid.
