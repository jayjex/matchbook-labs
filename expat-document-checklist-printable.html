Expat Document Checklist (Free Printable): a 2-Page Set for the Papers of a Move

Moving abroad means keeping track of papers that live in different places: a passport in the safe, a permit application with a consulate, a birth certificate at your parents' house. This expat document checklist is a free printable set of two pages. Page one is 12 write-in rows where each document gets an expiry date, a copies circle, and a location you can name in two words. Page two covers the backup side: a 6-item digital checklist, a contacts table for the people who help, and 4 opinion tips, including the two-copies-in-two-bags habit. If you want status columns like apostille tracking on screen instead, the documents checklist for moving abroad is the organizer version; the money side of the move is on the cost checklist.

How to use this page: print both pages at 100% scale. On page one, one row per document: write the name, read the expiry date off the document itself and write it down, circle Checked once you have verified it, then circle done or not under Copies depending on whether a paper copy exists, and write where the original lives. On page two, tick each backup item only after you have opened it from a second device, and fill the contacts table with names, roles, and phone numbers while you still remember who does what. Requirements vary by country - check official government sources, and treat these pages as a place to organize what you find, not a source itself.

The two-page expat document checklist

Nothing on the sheets says which documents your route requires or how to get any of them. The rows hold names, dates, circles, and locations; your handwriting holds the rest. 4 habits, all opinions

  1. Two copies in two bags. (Opinion.) One paper set rides with you, one goes in a different bag. Originals stay in carry-on, never in checked luggage or shipping boxes.
  2. Test the backup before you fly. (Opinion.) Open the cloud folder from a device that is not yours. A backup you cannot log into is a wish, not a backup.
  3. Name a holder at home. (Opinion.) Leave one set with a person you trust and write down what they hold, because next year you will not remember whether the birth certificate is with family or in the safe.
  4. Rescan what changes. (Opinion.) New stamp, renewed passport, new permit: scan it again and delete the stale copy, so the folder never contradicts the paper.
