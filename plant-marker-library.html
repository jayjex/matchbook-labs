Print settings for flat plant markers

  • Bed adhesion. Wash the plate with dish soap, rinse, dry. Clean PEI holds these parts without glue or hairspray. Add a 3-5 mm brim only if a corner lifts on the first print; the long thin stakes are the likeliest candidates.
  • Layer height. 0.2 mm on a 0.4 mm nozzle. The recessed label bars are 0.8 mm deep, which is four layers, so 0.2 mm keeps the floor solid and the edges crisp. Drop to 0.12 mm if you want sharper engraving on the herb names.
  • Material. PLA is fine for a season of seedlings. PETG for markers that stay outside year-round: it handles sun and rain without turning brittle the way PLA does in UV.
  • Supports. Off. Every design prints flat on its back in one piece. If a slicer asks for supports, the model is oriented wrong.
  • Infill and walls. 15% and two walls are plenty for a stake that gets pushed into soil by hand. The markers weigh 9-13 g each in PLA, so a spool goes a long way.

How the writing surfaces differ

The two bar-style stakes (120 mm and 140 mm) carry recessed bars 0.8 mm deep: write with a paint pen, and when the crop changes, sand or scribble over the bar and rewrite. The step-top marker trades bars for five spacing ticks, so it labels and spaces a row at the same time. The herb pack engraves the plant name into the plastic 0.6 mm deep, which reads well but never changes, so it suits permanent beds. The Halloween set has character toppers instead of writing space; write on the stake below the figure.
