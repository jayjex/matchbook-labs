Draft-Day Prep Checklist — 8 Items

Sort these before the first pick and draft day runs itself. Print the list, tape it next to the board.

  • Confirm the pick order with the commissioner, including any keeper declarations that shift it
  • Print two boards: one pick tracker for the room, one for your own roster plan
  • Charge the laptop and phone the night before, pack a charger; long drafts drain both
  • Snacks and drinks on the table before round 1, so nobody vanishes mid-pick
  • Mark your cheat sheet: circle the reaches, strike the fades, note bye-week collisions
  • Check roster requirements: starters per position, bench spots, injured-reserve slots
  • Test the room's WiFi and set up a phone hotspot as backup
  • Agree on a time limit per pick and who calls the clock

4 Draft-Day Tips (Opinions)

Opinions, not rules. Learned running boards in rooms that argue about everything.

Read the pick order out loud twice

Snake order is where draft-day confusion comes from, and one read-through hides the team that picks last in round 1 and first in round 2. Read it twice, slowly. Ten seconds each saves ten minutes of "wait, whose pick is it?"

Write the name, then cross it

Crossing names from memory at pick 40 is how a taken player stays available on your board. Write when the pick is announced, cross when the next one starts. A board that lies about who is available is worse than no board.

Announce your pick so the room hears it

Half of draft-day dead time is two managers waiting for each other to notice a pick happened. Say the name out loud, then write it down. The tracker stays honest and the clock keeps moving.

Stand up after round 8

Rounds 9 through 12 are where tired managers draft for needs they invented an hour ago. A two-minute break, some water, back to the chair. That reset saves more value than any ranking tweak you could make in the same two minutes.
