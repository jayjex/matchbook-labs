How Many LMSW Practice Questions? A Free Planner, an Error Log, and Honest Advice

People search for free LMSW practice questions, collect a pile, and then stall, because the pile was never the hard part. Using the questions is. This page covers the two things that decide whether practice questions actually help: how many you do (our opinion, with a free write-in planner) and what you do after grading (a 6-row error log template). No quiz here; this is a guide you print and write on.

How many practice questions per day? Pick a number you can grade

In our opinion, the right daily amount is whatever you will finish and review the same day. Ten questions with a graded note on each miss teaches more than fifty questions graded never. The daily number is personal, so the planner below is a write-in: pick the amount before the week starts, then record what actually happened. If your planned number keeps beating your done number, shrink the plan instead of feeling behind.

Print one sheet per week. In our opinion, a small set every weekday plus one longer timed block on the weekend beats one binge that leaves no grading time. The note column is for patterns, not scores: "guessed on 3", "slow after question 5", "all misses in one topic".

Why rereading wrong answers is the whole point

In our opinion, the question you answered wrong is worth more than the twenty you answered right, and the value shows up on the second look. A miss means some rule, definition, or step order in your head does not match the scenario in front of you. Rereading the rationale and writing one line on the rule behind the miss fixes that gap while the question is still fresh. Rereading also catches the quiet half of the problem: the questions you guessed on and got right by luck. Those deserve the same treatment, because on test day the luck runs out and the rule is all that remains. Rules transfer between questions; memorized answers do not.

Pacing per question: a note you can actually use

However many questions you work, pace them the same generic way: run a block with a clock, divide your minutes by the number of questions, and write down your average per question. Then compare your average against the time a full-length sit would allow and decide whether you have a content problem or a speed problem. If the average keeps drifting up, practice the triage habit on purpose: answer, flag, move on, and return only if minutes remain. We keep the math generic on purpose, because exam length and timing policies vary by jurisdiction; your regulator's handbook has the current numbers for yours.

Four opinion tips on using practice questions

1. Simulate timing once a week

Once a week, sit a block of questions against a clock with no notes and no pauses. Timing is a separate skill from knowing the material, and it only shows up under a clock. In our opinion, one honest timed block per week beats daily untimed practice for finding out whether your pacing holds.

2. Write the rule, not the question

For every miss, write one line on the rule behind it rather than the question text. Rules transfer to the next scenario; a memorized question does not. If you cannot name the rule, that line becomes your next study session's first topic.

3. Track your results per section, not just overall

A strong overall percentage can hide one weak section, and averages do not tell you where to spend your remaining weeks. Group your log lines by section or topic and count them. In our opinion, the section with the most misses is where your next daily set should come from.

4. Rerun missed questions after a gap

Wait at least two weeks, long enough that memory of the item has faded, then rerun the questions you missed from the log alone. Missing the same question twice tells you the rule never stuck and deserves a proper reread. Many candidates get the most value from a third pass over exactly those repeat misses.

Frequently asked

How many LMSW practice questions should I do per day? In our opinion, whatever amount you will actually grade the same day. A daily set you finish and review beats a weekend pile you skim. Pick the number yourself, write it in a planner before the week starts, and keep one weekly timed block for stamina. If a daily set keeps going ungraded, shrink it until it does not.

Where can I find free LMSW practice questions? We keep a free sample set on this site, and your regulator's handbook names the official prep resources for your jurisdiction; those live outside this site. Libraries and social work programs often carry prep materials too. Whoever the source is, read the rationale for every miss rather than collecting more questions.

Do I really have to reread every wrong answer? In our experience, the wrong answers are where the studying happens. Reread every miss and every guess, and write one line on the rule behind each one. Rules transfer between questions; a memorized question does not. An error log turns that habit into twenty minutes a week instead of a vague intention.
