4 opinions on running this tracker

  1. One sheet per team, not per employee. (Opinion.) The kit's personnel-record PDF carries a PTO tracker per employee file, 12 months x 15 rows, which is the deep record. This sheet is the other cut: every pending request on one page. A manager who can see all 12 rows at once spots the collision before approving the third overlapping request, which is the reason the log exists.
  2. Alias on the paper, name in the file. (Opinion.) The README's own instruction for the personnel record: it holds personal data, so store it somewhere locked. A request log works in the open, taped by the door or on a clipboard, and the alias column is what makes that workable. R.D. on the wall means nothing to a visitor; to the team, it is the row they check before booking flights.
  3. Circle the answer the day it is given, whatever the answer is. (Opinion.) An approval that lives only in conversation gets re-argued in December. The circle takes a second, and a row with no circle reads as pending, which is also information. Denials go in the log too: a written no with dates aims the next request better.
  4. Recount page 2 from page 1 at month end, not from memory. (Opinion.) Used days that never make it from the log to the balance row turn the remaining column into a guess, and a guess answers the next request wrong. Ten minutes at month end keeps the remaining column a fact instead of a feeling.
