New Hire Onboarding Checklist (Free Printable)

A new hire onboarding checklist is the manager's half of a first week: who owns the desk and the accounts, what happens on day 1, who answers questions in week 1. These two printable pages hold all of it in one place. Page 1 runs from the day before the start date through the first Friday. Page 2 holds the buddy system, the 30-60-90 goal lines and a feedback box. Print it, write on it, reprint one sheet per hire. The full kit with the 45-step PDF and a fill-in handbook is $19 at the bottom.

How to use this page: print at 100% scale, both pages come out on two sheets. Fill the name and start-date lines, then work the sheet in order: pre-start before day 1, day-1 items on the day, week-1 rows as they close. Tick the box when a step is done and put the date next to it. The kit's own instruction for the finished page: keep this sheet in the employee's personnel record. Page 2 takes about ten minutes at the end of week 1.

4 opinions on running this checklist

  1. Name the buddy before the start date, and write the name down. (Opinion.) The kit puts "Onboarding buddy assigned and introduced" in its Week One band, which is also the step managers postpone the longest. A buddy named on the sheet has a face on day 1; a buddy picked in the hallway on Friday stays a maybe. The three lines on page 2 exist so the pick survives past the conversation.
  2. Keep the sheet where the manager already sits, not in a folder. (Opinion.) A checklist in a drawer dies there. The README's own instruction is to print the checklists and keep them in a binder, one onboarding set per employee. The sheet earns its ticks at arm's reach, and the date column only fills in if the paper is visible on the day the step closes.
  3. Let the new hire write their own 30-60-90 goal lines first. (Opinion.) The kit's 30-day band starts with "30/60/90-day goals written and agreed", and agreed takes both sides. A draft in the hire's words, corrected in a 1:1, lands better than goals handed down. The sheet has three blank lines, and whose handwriting fills them first decides whether the review meetings are conversations or announcements.
  4. Reprint a fresh sheet for every hire. (Opinion.) One sheet per hire keeps the dates honest and leaves you a stack of finished pages that shows how your onboarding actually runs, which is the only version of the process worth improving. The template is undated on purpose; the dates come from the hires, not from the paper.
