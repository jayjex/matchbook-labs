Four kitchen notes

These are habits, take or leave, and the kind that keep this chart in use instead of in a drawer:

  1. Tape it inside the cabinet door you open most. A chart on the fridge fades into the background within a week; inside the spice or mixing-bowl cabinet, it's in front of you exactly when both hands are full.
  2. Write your own conversions in the blank line. Every kitchen has one weird ratio it uses constantly, a doubled batch, a halved pot, a family-size pan. The printed standard rows are the fallback; the handwritten line is the part that makes the chart yours.
  3. Check the substitution table before the store run. Half a supermarket trip for a tablespoon of an ingredient you can make from two things already in the pantry is the trip this table exists to cancel.
  4. One chart for the whole kitchen, not one per drawer. Two copies means one of them is always the outdated one. Print once, keep it where the measuring cups live, and let it get splattered.
