LMSW Practice Test Schedule: the Last 4 Weeks

The month before the exam is a pacing problem more than a content problem. This page lays out those four weeks as a schedule: how many questions to answer each day, when to review, when to sit a full mock, a write-in error log to keep, and an honest answer to the question everybody asks: how many practice questions is enough?

These are planning figures, not requirements. If your exam appointment falls midweek, shift the mock days so each one sits two or three days before a review day. Confirm your appointment length and what you may bring in your regulator's handbook, then set the mock start time to match.

How many practice questions is enough?

Honest answer: nobody can hand you an official number, because no regulator handbook we have seen publishes a question quota. Treat any specific total someone quotes you as their personal anecdote, not a threshold. What you can do is run the planning math and then adjust it to how you score.

Low end of the table: 25 questions × 5 days × 4 weeks = 500.

High end: 50 × 5 × 4 = 1,000.

So the schedule above produces 500 to 1,000 questions in the final month. Add the lighter weeks before that and a candidate following our 8-week study plan lands somewhere between several hundred and about two thousand overall. Both ends of that range are normal.

Three things move you around that range more than any formula:

  • Your diagnostic. A weak spread across every domain in week one argues for the high end. Strong diagnostics with one soft domain argue for fewer questions, aimed at that domain.
  • How you review. Fifty questions a day with the rationales unread is worth less than twenty-five a day with every miss written into an error log. Volume only counts when review follows it, which is why the schedule caps daily volume instead of maximizing it.
  • Guesses and near-misses. Review the questions you got right on a coin flip with the same attention as your misses. Most people undercount these, and they hide more gaps than the obvious wrong answers do.

Error log template

The error log is the cheapest high-value tool in the last month. One row per miss, filled in the same day. Ten blank rows below; print this page or copy the columns into a notebook.

"Why I got it wrong" means the mechanism, not the symptom. "Rushed" and "tricky wording" are symptoms; "picked the first-order change before assessing" is a mechanism. Mechanisms repeat, so they are what the Saturday review is hunting for. Once a row teaches you a rule, write that rule on a separate list and reread the list, not the rows.

Three rules for mock days

These are our opinions, built from how the last month usually goes wrong:

  1. Timed, one sitting, no pauses. Splitting a mock across an afternoon trains nothing you will use. Endurance with a clock running is a separate skill from knowing the material, and mock days are where you rehearse it.
  2. Same duration and same start time as the real appointment. If your exam appointment is a four-hour morning block, sit the mock as a four-hour morning block. Matching the start time matters more than it sounds; alertness at 8 a.m. and 8 p.m. are different instruments.
  3. Review the same day, while the misses are fresh. Every miss and every lucky guess goes into the error log that evening. A mock reviewed three days later is a score; a mock reviewed the same day is a lesson.

Frequently asked

How many practice questions should I do for the LMSW exam? There is no official number, and anyone who quotes you one is guessing. The planning math: 25 to 50 questions a day, five days a week, across the last four weeks lands between 500 and 1,000, and a full two-month plan usually produces several hundred to about two thousand overall. Where you land depends on your diagnostic and how thoroughly you review.

What is a good practice test schedule for the last month? Weeks four and three out: 25 to 40 questions a day with a fixed review day, first full mock at the end of week three. Week two out: 40 to 50 a day, second mock midweek. Final week: taper to 25 to 30 a day, error-log review daily, third mock early in the week only if the second is fully reviewed.

How should I review a missed practice question? Write three things down: the question, the domain, and why you got it wrong in your own words. A one-line rule you write yourself beats a rationale you read twice. Reread the whole log weekly and hunt for patterns across domains.
