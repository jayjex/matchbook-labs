4 opinions from parties like this one (take or leave)

  1. Buy the candy early. Two weeks out, the shelf still has the bags you actually want; the week of, you are choosing from what other households left behind. The candy budget box exists so you can shop for it the same day you send invites.
  2. Five snack rows, bigger batches. A short table you cook in doubles beats a long one where everything runs out mid-party. The qty column is there to push you toward multiples of the winners.
  3. Print the game sheets with the grocery run. Paper and ink are the cheapest line on this list, and doing it a week out means a dry run if the printer argues. Day-of printing is how game time becomes setup time.
  4. Give the door a job. Whoever greets guests hands each one a game sheet and a pencil. People who get something to do in the first minute settle in faster than people told to make themselves at home.

Common questions

When should I start this checklist?

Two weeks out, which is where the sheet starts. The date, guest list and candy budget decisions come first because invites, shopping and the snack table all inherit from them. If you are reading this with one week to go, start at the middle band; the day-of band still works no matter when you start.
