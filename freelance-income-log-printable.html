Freelance Income Log Printable

Self employed income arrives in pieces: an invoice paid here, a platform payout there, the odd cash job that never touches a bank feed. This page gives the pieces one place to land, on paper. Print two pages free: a 12-row income log where you write the date, a client alias, the work, the amount, and circle paid, then a monthly totals table and a five-row invoice-sent tracker. Write-in only, no spreadsheet needed, prints tonight.

How to use this page: print at 100% scale (site chrome drops out on paper). Page one is the payment log: one row each time money lands, date first, then the client alias, the work you did, the amount, and a circle for paid. Page two closes the month: six totals rows, plus the invoice-sent tracker for the invoices that went out without money back yet. Print a fresh pair for each month.

Page one: the income log, 12 rows

Twelve rows covers an ordinary month of freelance payments for a lot of one-person operations, and a page you can finish beats a ledger you abandon. Nothing is pre-filled: no sample rows, no amounts someone else made up. The client column takes an alias you pick, two or three letters, so the sheet stays private if someone else sees it. Circle paid in the last column only when the money actually arrives. An invoice sent and a payment received are different facts, and this sheet keeps them in different places: circles on page one, the sent tracker on page two.

Four opinions on running the log

Opinions from building record-keeping pages like this one. Take or leave:

  1. Log the payment the day it lands. A same-day entry takes half a minute while you still remember which job it was for. Rebuilding a month of income from bank statements takes an evening, and cash jobs never show up in the rebuild at all.
  2. Pick the client alias before the first row. An alias chosen at row one stays consistent down the page. Aliases invented mid-month, when a real name suddenly feels too recognizable, leave totals you cannot read back.
  3. Circle paid only when money arrives, never when the invoice goes out. Sent and paid are different facts with a gap between them, and the invoice-sent box on page two exists to hold that gap where you can see it. Everything circled that is not also in the sent box came from a job you already billed.
  4. Total the month the day the sheet fills. A total written while the rows are fresh matches the page in front of you. A total left for later turns into an estimate, and an estimate in an income record tends to stay there.

What this page is

A record-keeping page. It gives payments a place to be written, totaled and matched against invoices, so a self employed person can see the month as it actually went. Nothing here is income advice, tax advice, or financial advice, and no number on these pages means anything beyond what you wrote in.
