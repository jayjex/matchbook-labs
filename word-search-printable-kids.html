How to play word search (3 steps):

1. Pick a word from the word bank and read it out loud. Look at its first letter, because that is what you are hunting for.

2. Slide your finger along each row, left to right, hunting for that first letter. Then go down each column, top to bottom. Every word in these puzzles hides one of those two ways.

3. Found it? Circle the whole word, then tick its checkbox in the word bank. Eight ticks means the puzzle is done.

3 tips for kids doing word searches

Opinions from handing these to kids, not a teaching study:

  1. Laminate the printed sheet and use a dry-erase marker. One sheet survives a whole week of redoing, and a five-year-old will want to redo it, because finding the same words again is a win, not a repeat. Wipe, tick the checkboxes again, done. Regular pencil on paper works too, but the laminate version gets picked up without being asked.
  2. Read the whole word bank out loud before hunting. Kids who scan the grid cold hunt for letters at random. Kids who heard "CAT, DOG, SUN" first hunt for a C, or a D, or an S, which is a plan. For pre-readers this matters most: hearing the word is what tells them which letters to look for.
  3. Tick the checkbox before circling the next word. Half-done word searches die because nobody remembers which words were found. The tick takes a second and keeps the puzzle resumable after dinner. It also teaches the habit that matters later in school: track what is finished.

Common questions

What age are these word searches for?

Ages 5 to 9. The easy part is deliberate: only two directions to check (left to right and top to bottom), 8 words per grid, and short words. A five-year-old can finish puzzle 1 in one sitting with the word bank read aloud first. By age 8 both puzzles go fast, so use the laminate tip and time the second run.

Are there diagonal or backwards words?

No. Every word in both puzzles runs either left to right or top to bottom, forwards only. Backwards and diagonal words are what make standard word searches brutal for young kids, so they are left out here on purpose.

How do I know every word is in the grid?

Both grids came out of a generator that re-scans the finished letters after filling in the blanks, and all 16 words were confirmed present before publishing. The answer grids above show exactly where each one sits.
