Review box - answer in pen, on the page, at the end of the 90 days

1. Which milestone shipped, and what did it cost in time that you did not plan for?

2. Which milestone slipped, and was the date wrong or the scope wrong?

3. What deserves to be milestone 1 of the next quarter?

The slipped-milestone question forces a choice between two honest answers: the date was unrealistic, or the scope grew. Both are useful. "It just didn't happen" is the only answer this box does not accept.

Four opinions on running a 90-day plan

Opinions, not rules - they come from printing these sheets and watching which habits keep the second quarter's sheet filled after the first one faded.

  1. A quarter beats a year (opinion). Twelve weeks is short enough that the finish line stays visible and long enough to ship something with weight. Annual plans drift because December is a rumor in January; a 90-day plan ends soon enough to hurt if it slips.
  2. Six milestones, not sixteen (opinion). The table stops at six rows because a 90-day plan that lists everything accomplishes the first three rows and abandons the rest. If a seventh milestone matters, something in the first six is decoration - find it.
  3. Put the last target date around day 80, not day 90 (opinion). The final ten days are where the overruns of the previous eighty land. A plan that ends exactly on day 90 has no slack, and slack is what makes the Done circles honest.
  4. Review at day 45, not just day 90 (opinion). The review box is printed for the end, but run the three questions once at the midpoint on scrap paper. A milestone that is going to slip announces itself by week six, and only a midpoint look catches it while there is still time to trade scope for the date.
