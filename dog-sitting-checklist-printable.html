4 opinions on handing a dog off to a sitter

  1. Leave the boring details in. (Opinion.) What time dinner lands, which door sticks, where the leash hangs. The quirks box exists for the interesting stuff; the routine rows exist for the boring specifics, and the boring specifics are what a sitter uses on every visit.
  2. Fill it in with the owner, never from memory. (Opinion, straight from the pack's how-to: "Every empty box on the profile is a question to ask, not a guess to make.") Ten minutes of owner answers beats a page of sitter assumptions, and the filled sheet is the record of what was said.
  3. Pack the bag the night before. (Opinion, from the pack's own supplies banner: "The bag packed the night before is the visit that starts on time. Restock the day you notice.") The supplies box on page two tells the sitter what to carry; restocking the day you notice keeps the box honest.
  4. Keep codes and PINs off the paper. (Opinion, from the pack's care boundaries, which say access notes stay practical: which gate, which door, where the key lives by name. "Codes and PINs stay off paper.") A sheet that gets left on a counter should never carry anything you would not leave on a counter.
