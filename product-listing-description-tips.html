Product Listing Description Tips: How to Write Descriptions That Sell

A description does one job: it answers the buyer's questions in the order they think of them, so the only step left is checkout. This page gives you the five parts every product listing description needs, a before-and-after rewrite of one handmade candle listing, and four opinions. The mechanics here are generic craft for any marketplace, including Etsy listings; nothing on this page touches platform rules, because the description itself is the part you control.

The anatomy of a product description

Five parts, in the order buyers read. A short listing can merge some of them, but skipping one usually shows up later as a question in your messages.

PartJobMechanics
HookName the buyer and the moment of use.First one or two lines: who holds this, where it sits, what it replaces. Specific beats clever.
BenefitsTranslate each feature into a change for the buyer.One benefit per line, scannable. A spec becomes a benefit when it answers "so what".
SpecsGive exact, checkable facts.Size, material, quantity, what is included, as bullets with real numbers. No rounding that hides anything.
UsageShow the buyer using or personalizing it.Care, assembly, customization steps, or one concrete scene. Keep steps in order and short.
TrustAnswer the worry before it is asked.Processing time, what happens if something arrives wrong, honest limits. State them plainly.

Order matters: the hook earns the scroll, benefits hold it, specs and usage close the gap for careful buyers, trust removes the last hesitation.

Before and after: one candle listing, same facts

Both versions below describe the same handmade candle and both stay truthful. The difference is order and framing, nothing else. Example text written for this page.

Before — spec-led

8oz soy wax candle. Handmade. Burns approximately 40 hours. Glass jar with lid. Makes a great gift.

After — benefit-led

One slow Sunday, covered: this 8oz soy candle burns for about 40 hours, so a single jar lasts most of a season of evenings. It is poured by hand in small batches, and the glass jar comes with a lid, so you can snuff it, shelve it, and keep the scent in. Burn time, wax, and jar size are listed below; the wick arrives trimmed and ready to light.

What moved: the 40-hour burn time was already in the before version, buried in line three. In the after version it becomes the opening claim, tied to something the buyer recognizes (a whole season of evenings). The lid went from a feature fragment to a small story about storing the candle. Zero new facts were added: a rewrite moves what you already have instead of inventing more.

Four opinions on writing descriptions

Opinions, not rules. Take or leave.

  1. Lead with the benefit, not the product name. Opinion: the buyer already sees the product name in the title and photo, so the first description line should answer what their day looks like with it. Repeating the name wastes the most-read line you have.
  2. Write the description before the title. Opinion: a finished description hands you the words buyers actually use, and the title keywords fall out of it. Titles written first tend to hunt for words the description never earned.
  3. Answer objections in the order buyers hit them. Opinion: pull the five most common questions from your past messages and place each answer where it would naturally come up. A description built this way reads like a reply, which is what a nervous buyer wants.
  4. Read the draft on a phone before publishing. Opinion: most buyers meet your listing in a narrow column, and a 300-word unbroken paragraph dies there. Short paragraphs and bullets survive the scroll.
