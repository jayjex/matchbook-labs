Four tips for running the checklist

Opinions from printing and filling sheets like this one, not findings from anywhere - your evenings and your rows will differ.

  1. (Opinion.) Start at the same time every night. The time column exists so the start time is written down, not remembered. Pick a time that fits how your evenings actually go, write it in the first row, and let the rest of the rows fall after it; a routine with a written start time is easier to restart after a messy week than one that lives in your head.
  2. (Opinion.) Write tasks a tired person can finish. The rows are for the end of the day, so small verbs win: "set out tomorrow's clothes" fills a done circle, "get organized" can't. If a row keeps going unfilled, that's the sheet telling you the task is too big for 9pm - shrink it or move it to the morning.
  3. (Opinion.) Fill the tomorrow box last, even when you're sure you'll remember. The top task feels unforgettable at 10pm and looks like a stranger's handwriting at 7am. One line on paper settles it, and the second line - the thing you can set up tonight - is where the checklist earns its keep: tomorrow starts with setup already done.
  4. (Opinion.) Keep the sheet where the evening happens. On the counter, taped inside a cupboard door, under the lamp you actually turn off last. A checklist stored in a drawer is a checklist you skip; the done circles are also the honest part - an unfilled circle on paper beats a perfect night nobody wrote down.
