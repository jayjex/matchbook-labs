Reading the Monday example: the clock times matter less than the order. Potty on waking, food at a set time, potty right after food, nap, repeat. An 8-week-old puppy spends most of the day asleep, so the schedule runs around naps, not the other way.

Why one week, not one day

A single day of good routine proves nothing; the week is where it sticks. The skills themselves get trained on the 8-week puppy training schedule, one focus skill per week. When you write all seven days, weekends get scheduled too, and weekends are where most puppy routines fall apart. If Saturday looks different from Tuesday on your filled-in chart, decide on purpose whether it should.

Set meal times do double duty: they make the after-meal potty trip predictable, and food keeps its value as a training reward. For portion sizes, food brands, and any diet change, ask your vet.

5 Routine Mistakes That Wreck the Week

  1. Food down all day. Free-feeding ruins two things at once: you can never predict when the potty trip is due, and food stops working as a training reward because the puppy is never hungry. Meals go down for a set time and come back up; the clock runs the kitchen.
  2. Crate as punishment. Send a puppy to the crate for chewing your shoe and the crate becomes the place where bad things happen, so now you are fighting to get the puppy inside at bedtime. Naps and meals belong in the crate, timeouts do not.
  3. Potty trips on your schedule, not the puppy's. The puppy needs to go after waking, after eating, and after play bursts, whether or not you remembered. Predictable accident times mean the schedule needs one more trip, not that the puppy failed.
  4. Weekend cramming. A 30-minute Saturday session does not do what three 3-minute sessions a day do. Puppies learn from frequency, and one long session mostly teaches them to be tired of you.
  5. Every family member runs their own routine. One person says "go potty," another says "hurry up," a third takes the puppy out a different door. Pick one phrase, one door, one schedule, write it down, and hold the whole house to it.

Crate Breaks, in Short

A crate break is planned quiet time in the crate between play or training sessions, and it is how a puppy learns to settle alone instead of rehearsing chaos. The setup that works:

  • Feed every meal in the crate with the door open for the first week, so the crate means food before it means alone.
  • Keep daytime breaks short for a young puppy, around an hour at first, and end every break with a potty trip outside.
  • Some whining at the start is normal for a few days. Open the door on a pause in the noise, or you teach the puppy that crying works.
  • The crate is never a timeout spot, and it is not a place to leave a puppy all day. It is a nap spot with a door.

If your puppy panics in the crate for more than a few days, stop and mention it to your vet or a positive-reinforcement trainer; forcing it makes the next week harder.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should a puppy's daily schedule look like?

The same anchors every day: potty on waking, meals at set times, potty after every meal and play session, short play or training between naps, a walk in the evening, and a last potty trip before bed. At 8 weeks old most of the day is naps, so run the schedule around the naps rather than the clock.

How many times a day should a puppy eat?

Common guidance is 3 to 4 meals a day from 8 to 12 weeks, 3 meals from 3 to 6 months, and 2 meals from 6 months on. Portions and food choices depend on your puppy, so ask your vet.

How long can an 8-week-old puppy stay in a crate during the day?

Keep daytime breaks short, around an hour at first, and end each one with a potty trip. Overnight is different: an 8-week-old usually needs one trip out during the night, and that gets longer on its own over the following weeks.

When can my puppy go for real walks?

Ask your vet which outings are safe before the vaccine series is finished; that answer changes by area. In the meantime, leash practice in the hallway or your own yard counts, and the Monday example keeps it to 5 minutes on purpose.
