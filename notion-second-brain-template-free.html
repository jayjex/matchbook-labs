The Structure: 4 Databases, One Page

Every item in your workspace lives in exactly one of four places. That's the entire system; the rest of this page is making it stick. You'll see variations of this structure everywhere, sold under different method names. The bucket names below are generic on purpose: rename them to whatever you'll actually type.

DatabaseWhat goes inWhen-empty rule
InboxEverything unsorted: an idea, a link, a bill to file, a book someone mentioned. Capture first, decide later.Empty means you processed it, which is the goal. If it stays empty for days, you're capturing somewhere else; fix the capture habit, not the inbox.
ProjectsAnything with an end: finish the portfolio, renew the passport, plan the trip. Each row gets a next step and a due date, or it isn't a project yet.Empty means everything you started is finished. Start one thing this week or enjoy the calm; don't invent fake projects to fill the view.
AreasWhat you maintain with no finish line: money, health, home, work, the people you keep in touch with. One row per ongoing responsibility.Empty means you've been running on Projects alone. That works for a month and rots after; name what you maintain and it stops surprising you.
ArchiveDone projects, dropped ideas, old everything. Nothing here needs action, ever. Search finds it when you want it.Empty means you delete instead of archiving. That works until the first "wait, where did that go" moment; the archive is the delete button that doesn't scare you.

Why four works: capture has one door (Inbox), doing has one door (Projects), maintaining has one door (Areas), and letting go has one door (Archive). The moment a fifth door appears, you start deciding where things go instead of putting them somewhere.

Build It: an 8-Step Starter Checklist

One sitting, about 30 minutes on the free Notion plan. Check these off in order:

  • Create a page called Second Brain. One page, near the top of the sidebar, nothing nested inside it yet.
  • Create the four full-page databases: Inbox, Projects, Areas, Archive.
  • Add the same two properties to Projects and Areas: Status (Select: Active, Waiting, Done) and Next step (Text).
  • Add one more property to Projects: Due (Date).
  • Put all four on the Second Brain page as linked views, Inbox at the top, in that order.
  • Set the views: Inbox as a list, Projects as a board grouped by Status, Areas as a list, Archive as a list sorted by newest first.
  • Seed it with three real things: one open project with a next step, one area you actually maintain, one thing you want to read later in the Inbox.
  • Stop here. No icons, no covers, no template gallery, no fifth database until a real week proves you need one.

The Weekly Review: 4 Steps, 15 Minutes

The structure survives on one appointment: 15 minutes, same day every week, Sunday works for most people. Four steps:

  1. Empty the Inbox. Every item moves to Projects, Areas, or Archive, or gets deleted. Nothing stays in the Inbox after review; it's a counter, not a shelf.
  2. Walk Projects. Each row gets a next step written in, or moves to Archive if it's done or dead. A project without a next step is a wish; give it one or archive it.
  3. Glance Areas. One line per area: still fine, or needs a task? Add the task if something nags. Areas don't need more than that.
  4. Do nothing to Archive. It asks nothing of you. That's the point of it.

Week 6 looks like week 1 as long as the review happens. The weeks it doesn't happen are the weeks the Inbox fills and the system quietly dies, so guard the appointment, not the databases.

Four Opinions Worth Keeping

Labeled as opinions, from building and abandoning a few of these:

  • One inbox, not five. Notion's web clipper, mobile quick-capture, and email-to-page will each happily become a second inbox. Every extra inbox doubles the places you have to empty on Sunday. Point every capture at the one Inbox page, or type it in by hand.
  • If a property needs a paragraph to explain, delete it. Status and Next step carry Projects; Due carries dates. Priority scores, effort estimates, and relation webs come back later if a real week demands them. They rarely do.
  • Archive faster than feels right. A second brain rots from keeping too much, not too little. Search finds anything you archived; guilt finds nothing you hoarded in Projects.
  • Build it in one sitting and change nothing for 30 days. The week-3 redesign is how these systems die. The structure above survives because there's nothing in it to redesign; resist the urge to improve a system you've used four times.
