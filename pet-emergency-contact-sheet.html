Four opinions on filling it in

  1. (Opinion.) Fill it in with the owner, never from memory. The client book’s setup page says to fill the profile "with the owner sitting next to you," and the same rule runs a single sheet. A recalled phone number is a guess with confidence; a read-out-loud number is a record.
  2. (Opinion.) Tape one copy on the fridge. Paper in a folder protects the paper. Paper on the fridge gets found by whoever walks in the door, which is the whole point of leaving the sheet where a substitute sitter can see it without asking anyone.
  3. (Opinion.) The hours column matters as much as the phone number. A clinic row without hours is half a row: on a Saturday the reader can’t tell whether a call goes to the clinic or straight back to the owner. Write both, and the sheet survives being read in a hurry.
  4. (Opinion.) Update it the day something changes. The product’s own note: "New number, new door, new pet: update the row the day you hear it, then fix the profile spread to match." A contact sheet with an old number is quieter than no sheet at all, because nobody double-checks the one that looks complete.

What this page is

A care-organization sheet. It holds owner and vet contact details, feeding instructions in the owner’s words, and walk notes, so the next person who walks in starts from facts instead of a text thread. It holds no guidance on health or feeding, and no claims about breeds; those live with the owner, and medical questions go to the owner and their clinic. The sheet shortens the handoff in both directions: the sitter reads it in a minute, and the owner doesn’t re-answer the same five questions for every new booking.
