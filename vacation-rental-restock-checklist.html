Page one: the restock checklist, 12 rows

Twelve rows is enough for the consumables a turn actually burns: linens, towels, paper goods, soap refills, and the pantry items your guests open first. The par column stays blank on purpose. Par is a number only you can set, because it depends on how many guests a turn brings and how often you restock. Write it in pen and leave it alone until the property changes. On restock day, count the shelf and circle one of the two circles per row: in stock when the count holds at or above par, reorder when it does not. A row with no circle is an item nobody counted, and the empty circles are the part of the sheet that works.

Page two: the between-stays checklist

Six boxes, one pass, in the order a walk moves through the place: linens first, then trash, then the surfaces hands land on, then the restock basket topped up from page one, then paper goods back to par, then the door and keys on the way out. The boxes are mechanics, not a cleaning course: what to swap, what to carry out, what to top up. No brand names, no product picks; you already know what your property uses. The par-level notes box at the bottom holds two lines for the thing every sheet needs somewhere: what par stands at after this pass, and what changed.

Four opinions on setting par

Opinions from building restock and record-keeping pages like this one. Take or leave:

  1. (Opinion.) Set par at two turns, not one. One turn of stock in service and one turn on the shelf: a checkout empties the service set, the shelf set covers the next arrival, and the store run happens after the guest leaves instead of before they land.
  2. (Opinion.) Write par in pen and the count in circles. Par only moves when the property changes, so a number that changes every restock day is a par nobody set. The circles do the moving; the column stays honest.
  3. (Opinion.) Reorder from the circles, not from memory. The rows circled reorder on the last sheet are the shopping list, copied out on the same day. A list rebuilt from memory at the store misses the one thing that actually ran out.
  4. (Opinion.) Restock the day of checkout, while the six boxes are still fresh in your hands. The between-stays pass already touches every place supplies live, so page one costs three extra minutes then and a separate trip any other day.
