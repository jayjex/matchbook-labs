Free Self Audit Checklist Template (Two Pages)

A self audit is the boring kind of audit: you write down what you have, when you last touched it, and whether you still want it. Most people have never seen that list in one place. These two printable pages hold it. Page 1 is a ten-row account table - name, when the password last changed, a two-factor circle, a note - plus eight subscription rows, each with a keep-or-cancel circle. Page 2 is a data-exposure box, a quarterly-review box, and nothing else. Print it, write on it, reprint it next quarter. Below the sheet sits a $16 checklist for a different kind of audit entirely - smart-contract code, not your accounts.

How to use this page: print at 100% scale - both pages come out on two sheets. Start page 1 with the accounts you sign into weekly, then let the forgotten ones surface as you go. No passwords go on paper: the "last password changed" column is a date, and the note column is for whatever you'd otherwise have to remember. Circle Y or N in the 2FA column as you check each account. Page 2 takes five minutes and belongs at the end, once the list on page 1 has told you the truth about your subscriptions.

4 opinions on running this audit

  1. Cancel one subscription every quarter, minimum. (Opinion.) Eight rows fill fast because signups are easy and cancellations are work. Picking one row per quarter and circling CANCEL keeps the total honest without turning the sheet into a monthly austerity exercise. If all eight earn their keep, circle KEEP on all eight - the point is a decision per row, not a quota of cuts.
  2. Fill the account table from a list, not from memory. (Opinion.) Whatever inventory you already keep - a password manager's export, your email's signup folder, a browser's saved-sites list - walk it row by row. Memory gives you the ten accounts you like; the list gives you the twenty-three you have. The forgotten ones are the reason this page exists.
  3. Do the 2FA circles in one sitting, before anything else. (Opinion.) The circle is the fastest column and the one people skip. Ten Y-or-N circles take five minutes and leave you with a count you can act on. Dates in the password column can wait for a second sitting; circles can't, because a half-filled circle column reads as a done column by next quarter.
  4. Reprint fresh each quarter and keep the old sheets. (Opinion.) Editing one sheet forever turns the audit into a diary. A fresh print each quarter, with the previous sheet filed next to it, shows the drift you'd never notice day to day: which row count moved, which cancel circle finally got circled, what the next-audit date actually was versus the one you wrote.
