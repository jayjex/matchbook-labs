The 3 checkpoint questions: day 30, 60, 90

Every 30 days, count your full boxes, then answer the same three questions in writing. Same questions each time, so the answers can be compared across the round:

  1. What stuck? Which days and habits held without a fight. These are the rules worth keeping untouched.
  2. What slipped, and what caused it? Name the day of the week, the time, or the situation, not the mood.
  3. Continue, adjust, or restart? Rules held: keep going. Rules fuzzy: tighten them and continue. Round fell apart: restart from day 1 with corrected rules, on the record.

Missed a day: the 5-step protocol

Most 90-day challenges die the morning after a miss, in the argument with yourself about whether it still counts. Settle that in advance: a miss is whatever your written rules say it is, and the protocol runs the next morning, in this order:

  1. Name it in one sentence. What happened, and which rule it broke. One sentence, not a paragraph of analysis.
  2. Check the rules, not the mood. If your rule sheet says a 20-minute workout counts and you did 15, that is a miss by your own definition. The sheet wins.
  3. Make the call once. Two options: count the miss and keep the round going, or restart the whole round at day 1. Pick one, write the date, no renegotiating at 11pm.
  4. Log it. Date, day reached, and the one thing you will change this time. A miss without a logged change is scheduled to repeat.
  5. Set up tomorrow and stop. Lay out what the next day needs the night before, fill today's box or leave it empty, and stop replaying it. The grid takes the next box as day 1 of the rest of the round.

Two misses in a row is the pattern worth watching: the first needs the protocol, the second usually means the rules were too vague to grade. Tighten the wording, not the effort.

Common questions

What goes on the habit line of each box?

One concrete line about the day: what you did, or what got missed. "5k run, in by 10pm" beats "good day". The point is a written record you can judge at the checkpoints without trusting memory.

Should I fill in all 90 dates up front?

Both work. All 90 up front makes the round feel real on day 1 and shows you where day 90 lands. Filling dates daily keeps the grid honest if you need to restart. If you restart often, fill as you go.

Do I need a new sheet for a second round?

Yes. Print a fresh grid, write new dates, and reuse what worked: the checkpoint answers from round one tell you which rules survived. The full pack includes the rule sheet and restart log pages that round two fills in.
