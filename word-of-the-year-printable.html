Three intentions tied to the word (one each, written out):

1. One decision the word will settle this year (the thing you keep going back and forth on).

2. One routine the word shapes, weekly or daily.

3. One thing the word says no to.

Monthly touchpoints

Once a month, two minutes: fill in the circle for that month and name one decision the word changed, or one it should have. Fill the circle even when the answer is "it didn't come up": a row of blank circles by June is data, and it is cheaper than finding out in December.

Mid-year check

Answer in writing around June or July. Switching words at mid-year is allowed and costs less than quietly abandoning one; the check exists to make that call honestly instead of by drift.

1. Is the word still the right one? If not, what is, and what changed?

2. Name one real decision the word changed. If nothing comes to mind in thirty seconds, that is the finding.

3. What does the second half of the year need from this word?

Four opinions on choosing

  • Pick a word, not a goal. "Lose 15 pounds" dies the week you gain two back. "Strong" survives a bad month and still applies on Monday. Goals snap; words bend.
  • Test the word in three sentences. Take three real decisions from last year and write how the word would have changed each. If the sentences sound like a fortune cookie, the word is too vague; if you can't get through one, it's someone else's word, not yours.
  • Take the word you resist a little. The comfortable word describes the year you were going to have anyway. The one that makes you slightly nervous usually points at the actual bottleneck.
  • One word. Not three, not a phrase, not a manifesto. If it takes a paragraph to explain, it won't survive a busy Tuesday. Short words get used.
