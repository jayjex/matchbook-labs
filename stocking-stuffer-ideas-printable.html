Notes

 
 
 

Two empty lines per box: one for who it's for, one for the price. The bought box only gets ticked when the thing is in the house, not when it's ordered. That rule is the difference between a checklist and a memory.

4 opinions on filling stockings without the leak

  1. Check the date before ten of anything edible lands in the cart. Edibles expire, and stuffer-sized snacks tend to sit in a drawer until spring. My opinion: buy the edible stuffers last, close to the day they'll be opened, and skip anything that needs to survive a full month of storage. A treat eaten in December is a gift; an expired one in a drawer is money thrown away.
  2. Buy the whole grid in one trip if you can. Every extra stop is where the budget leaks, because checkout lines are engineered to add one more small thing to the bag. One trip, grid in hand, nothing off-list. In my experience you come home with exactly the grid, and the grid was priced in advance, which is the whole point.
  3. Stockings get grid items only. Stuffers that miss the grid do not get bought. If a 25th idea shows up mid-December, it goes on the notes lines for next year instead of the cart. A list that caps itself is the only kind that survives the month.
  4. Small stuff stays small, and the big gifts live somewhere else. Anything over a few dollars belongs on a gift tracker with its own row and budget line, not in a stocking box. Counting the same purchase in both places is how a budget starts lying to you, and the planner rows above are for people, not for that overlap.
