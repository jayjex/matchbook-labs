Bill Payment Tracker, Free to Print

Ten bills, one page, nothing to install. This bill payment tracker is a monthly bill tracker you can print free: page 1 holds ten write-in rows, each with the bill's name, its due date, the amount, and a paid circle to fill the day money actually moves. Page 2 adds an autopay audit box for the bills that charge themselves and one line for late fees as your statements show them. The sheet records what you owe and what cleared; it pays nothing and reminds nobody.

How to use this page: print both pages at 100% scale (the site chrome drops out on paper). On page 1, list the bills that arrive in a normal month; most people fit theirs in ten rows, and a second copy covers the rest. On page 2, the autopay audit lines record what your bank or card charges on its own, and the late-fee line copies whatever your billers print. Reprint the whole set each month.

Four opinions on keeping a bill payment tracker

  1. (Opinion.) Mark paid the day the payment clears. Scheduled on Tuesday, charged on Thursday: the circle wants the second date. A tracker filled the day you schedule records an intention, and Thursday's balance has no respect for intentions.
  2. (Opinion.) Copy amounts from the statement, never from memory. The bill you remember for $60 is the bill that printed $63.80. The amount column exists to hold what the paper said, which is the only version worth comparing month over month.
  3. (Opinion.) Run the autopay audit against real statements, once a month. Autopay bills drift: a promo ends, a fee changes, an amount quietly stops matching. The four lines on page 2 take one pass with the statements in hand and catch the drift while it is still one month wide.
  4. (Opinion.) Write the late-fee line even when it is blank. A blank line you looked at is different from a line you never checked. Copy the zero or copy the fee; either way the sheet now shows what your statements actually said.

What this page is

A tracker, two pages, pen-first. It records the bills a month holds, the dates they come due, the amounts they print, the payments that cleared, the autopay charges that hit on their own, and the fees statements mention. It predicts nothing, pays nothing, signs nothing up, and recommends nothing. Nothing on this page is financial advice. What it does is put a month of bills on paper you can hold, so the next one starts from rows you filled in instead of dates you are recalling.

What the formulas do

  • Monthly income/expenses: SUMIFS over the Transactions amount columns, filtered by a date range built with DATE(2026,month,1) to DATE(2026,month+1,1) - 24 cells, no pivot tables
  • Net and YTD: subtraction and plain SUM; savings rate is net divided by income, blank when income is zero
  • Category actuals: SUMIFS matching the category text and the selected month (picked in a dropdown cell, B2)
  • Variance: budgeted minus actual, with an under/over status flag
  • Savings goals: % complete is saved divided by target (guarded against blanks and zero targets); the dashboard progress bar is a REPT text bar that works in Excel, Google Sheets and LibreOffice
  • Dropdowns: Transactions category reads the editable list on the Categories sheet; method is a fixed list; the month picker is a 1-12 dropdown
