Dog Walking Schedule Template (Free Printable)

A two-page walk plan you print and fill in. Page one is a 7-day week table with a walk time, a route, and a done circle for each day. Page two is a route bank box for the 4 walks you cycle through, plus one weather note line. It works as a log too: fill the circle when you get back and the sheet keeps the record for you. No email, no sign-up, print it as-is.

How to use it: at the start of the week, write a time and a route next to each day. Routes come from the route bank on page two, so you decide once, not seven times. After each walk, mark the done circle. At the end of the week the table shows what actually happened, which is the part a plan in your head never shows.

What this sheet is: a schedule for organizing walks, not a training or veterinary guide. It holds times, routes, and done marks, and it hands the whole plan to whoever walks the dog next. If your dog needs anything beyond a routine, the right person to ask is your vet.

4 Tips for Using the Sheet (Opinions, Not Rules)

  1. Same route, same time. This is opinion, not fact, but it is the one we would defend: a fixed loop at a fixed hour removes the two decisions that kill walk schedules, which are when and where. Once those are settled, the only remaining job is putting shoes on.
  2. Write routes you would walk on a bad day. A route bank full of 90-minute adventures looks good on paper and never gets opened. If half your bank is doable in rain, dark, or a packed Thursday, the sheet survives contact with real weeks.
  3. Mark the done circle on the way in, not that evening. Same-day marks take two seconds and get recorded. Reconstructed marks are guesses wearing a checkmark, and a sheet full of guesses stops being trusted within a week.
  4. Retire routes out loud. When a route stops working, cross it out in the bank and write its replacement the same day. A route bank that only ever grows turns into a to-do list; one that gets edited stays a menu.
