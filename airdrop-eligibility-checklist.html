Airdrop Eligibility Checklist

A one-page checklist for the farming work itself: which protocol you touched, what task you did, whether it is done, and the date it happened. This free printable crypto airdrop tasks list keeps that history in write-in rows, so when a project publishes its eligibility criteria you can check your own record instead of reconstructing it from memory. It organizes tasks. It predicts nothing and holds no price claims. For the wallet side of the same habit, the wallet activity log printable covers individual transactions.

How to use this page: print at 100% scale (the site chrome drops out on paper). Write the wallet alias on the line, one per sheet. Fill a row the moment a task is done: the protocol's name as you call it, the task in a few words, circle Done, write the date. The notes box takes anything that does not fit a row. Keep filled sheets private; they are your activity history.

Security reminders

1. Never share a seed phrase or private key. No support agent, admin, or "verification" step ever needs one. Anything that asks is a scam, whatever name or logo it carries.

2. Verify URLs yourself. Type the address or open your own bookmark. A link that arrived in a DM stays unclicked until you have checked where it leads.

3. Test with a small transaction first. A new address, bridge, or wallet gets a small test send before anything larger moves. The test costs minutes; the alternative can cost the balance.

Four opinions on keeping an eligibility checklist

  1. One wallet alias per sheet. (Opinion.) A checklist that spans two wallets stops matching anything: dates blur, tasks mix, and the record loses the one job it has. Write a single alias on the line, print a fresh sheet per wallet, and every row stays attributable.
  2. Write the date the day the task happened. (Opinion.) Published criteria usually carry a deadline, and a date reconstructed a month later is a guess. Same-day entries take seconds; a rebuilt history takes an evening and still argues with itself.
  3. Write tasks as actions, not wishes. (Opinion.) "Bridged test amount" or "completed quest round 2" is checkable against criteria later. "Get eligible" is an outcome no sheet can verify, and it pushes the row into hope where a record should be.
  4. Reprint instead of cramming row 13. (Opinion.) Twelve rows keep the page scannable and printable; a cramped sheet stops getting read. When the rows fill, archive the sheet and start a fresh one, the way a log book turns its pages.

What this page is

This is a task-organization sheet, not financial advice. Nothing on it predicts any airdrop, token price, or project plan, and nothing here tells you what to buy, sell, or hold. The sheet exists so your own past actions are checkable when a project publishes its criteria. No reward is promised by writing rows: most activity across most campaigns earns nothing, and a checklist that proves what you did is still worth more than a memory that guesses.
