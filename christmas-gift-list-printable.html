The stocking stuffer grid: 12 boxes, small stuff only

Stocking stuffers wreck gift budgets not because any one item costs much, but because they're bought last, in a hurry, at whatever store is still open. The grid works the other way: pick the twelve before you shop, write who each one is for and the price, and tick the circle when it's bought. If an item isn't on the grid, it doesn't go in the cart.

4 tips on starting the list early

  1. Start the list in October, finish it in your head all year. The best gift ideas arrive in September, overheard in passing, long before you sit down to plan. A list started early is mostly transcription; a list started December 20 is mostly panic buys at a markup.
  2. Write the idea down the same week you hear it. Gift ideas stored in memory have a shelf life of about three weeks. Nobody remembers "she mentioned that thing about her kitchen" in December. The idea column on the tracker is for capture, not for perfection; half a sentence beats a blank row.
  3. Buy in one or two trips, not ten. Every extra shopping trip is where the budget leaks, because you come home with one thing that wasn't on the list. Group the tracker's rows by store, buy in passes, and consider the list closed when the last bought circle is colored.
  4. Wrap the day it arrives. A pile of unwrapped gifts on December 23 is how wrapping turns into an all-nighter with tape and regret. The wrapped circle exists so you can see the pile shrinking; if the bought circle fills faster than the wrapped one for more than a week, stop buying and start wrapping.

How this list fits your gift budget

This tracker tracks people, ideas, and progress. It deliberately does not track money beyond one column: the budget number per row. That number comes from somewhere else. On the free Christmas budget planner page, you set a gifts total (the example there is $340 for a household of four) before you shop. The fit check between the two pages is one line of arithmetic: add the budget column on this list, and if the sum is bigger than your gifts total, cut a gift here rather than the total there.

Working the other direction: the tracker tells the budget what a realistic gifts line looks like. If you price twenty real gifts and they sum to $410, a $340 total is fiction, and it's better to learn that in October. Decide on paper, once, with the numbers in front of you. That's the entire method.
