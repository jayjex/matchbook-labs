How to solve these mazes (4 steps):

1. Find the entrance: the gap in the border at the top-left corner, above the start arrow.

2. Move cell to cell through openings. Up, down, left, or right. Diagonal jumps are not allowed.

3. A drawn line is a wall. Every wrong turn ends at a wall or back at a cell you already passed through.

4. Finish at the bottom-right corner, where the border opens below the finish arrow. Stuck? Read tip 2 below.

4 tips for printing mazes for kids

Opinions from watching kids work through pages like these, not a teaching study:

  1. (Opinion.) Print two copies of each maze. Copy one is for finger-tracing, copy two is for pencil. A kid who knows a retry is free commits to a turn faster, and the finger pass teaches the route before the pencil locks it in.
  2. (Opinion.) Teach backwards solving early. Start at the finish arrow and work up. It is the same single path, and it splits the problem: fewer branches lead away from the finish than away from the start on most grids. It also gives a stuck kid somewhere to go instead of erasing.
  3. (Opinion.) Skip the timer. Speed turns a puzzle into a test, and some kids quietly stop picking up puzzles after one timed round. If you want a scoreboard, count mazes finished, not seconds.
  4. (Opinion.) Solve the 10x10 next to them, not for them. Getting stuck in the same corner at the same time is real coaching: "I would go this way, but look, wall. Where else?" Pointing at the answer teaches nothing; getting unstuck together teaches the whole skill.
