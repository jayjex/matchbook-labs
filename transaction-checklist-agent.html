Buyer side: 12 steps

Working order, not a script. Skip what does not apply to the file; write the date next to each box you tick.

  • ☐ 01. Confirm budget in writing: pre-approval or proof of funds
  • ☐ 02. List needs, wants, and deal-breakers with the client
  • ☐ 03. Set the search area and the price ceiling
  • ☐ 04. Tour and log impressions the same day, while they're fresh
  • ☐ 05. Pull recent sold prices before writing the offer
  • ☐ 06. Set offer price, deposit, and timeline positions
  • ☐ 07. Submit, then plan the response: accept, counter, or walk
  • ☐ 08. Under contract: calendar every deadline before anything else
  • ☐ 09. Book the inspection; read the full report, not the summary
  • ☐ 10. Request repairs or credits where the evidence supports it
  • ☐ 11. Track the appraisal and the rate lock to the finish
  • ☐ 12. Walk-through, closing figures line by line, sign

Seller side: 12 steps

Same rules. Steps 6 through 8 are where most listing files stall, so give those dates first.

  • ☐ 01. Walk the property and list fixable issues
  • ☐ 02. Finish agreed pre-list repairs
  • ☐ 03. Gather the documents buyers will ask for
  • ☐ 04. Set the price strategy and the go-live date
  • ☐ 05. Prep the property for photos and showings
  • ☐ 06. Go live; log every showing request and feedback note
  • ☐ 07. Review traffic weekly against the plan
  • ☐ 08. Adjust price or presentation on evidence, not mood
  • ☐ 09. Compare every offer, including financing strength
  • ☐ 10. Under contract: calendar deadlines, send disclosures
  • ☐ 11. Coordinate inspection and appraisal access
  • ☐ 12. Walk-through ready, agreed items out, keys handed over

Four opinions on running the pipeline

  1. Update the row the day something happens. A pipeline with last week's stages is worse than none, because you'll trust it and call the wrong person first. Two minutes at day's end beats an hour of reconstructing on Friday.
  2. Next action gets a date or it doesn't go in. "Send comps" without a date is a wish. "Send comps by 09-11" is a row you can be late on, which is at least visible.
  3. Dead files move back, they don't get erased. A buyer who lost a bid comes back in the spring. Move the stage back to searching and keep the history; deleted rows delete your own context.
  4. The sign-in sheet has a 24-hour shelf life. Warm leads from an open house go stale faster than any other source. Transcribe the sheet into the pipeline the same evening while the faces still match the names.
