The five lines of a meeting summary prompt

A meeting summary prompt template needs five lines. Fill them once, save the file, and only the context line changes from meeting to meeting.

LineWhat it doesExample fill
RoleSets who the tool writes as."You are the project coordinator for a weekly 30-minute status meeting."
ContextSupplies facts the tool cannot guess."Attendees are [TEAM]; the meeting date is [DATE]; decisions matter more than discussion."
FormatNames the sections of the recap."Three sections: Decisions, Owners and deadlines, and Open questions."
LengthCaps the size of the output."One bullet per item, under 150 words total."
ExclusionsLists what stays out."Leave out anything marked [OFF_RECORD]."

Order matters less than completeness. A prompt missing context forces the tool to invent facts, and one missing the exclusions line hands you a recap that mixes decisions with discussion nobody agreed to circulate.

The fill-in template

Brackets mark what you change. Square brackets are placeholders, not code.

You are [ROLE].

Summarize the meeting notes I paste below into a recap.

Context: [ATTENDEES]; the meeting date is [DATE]; [WHAT_MATTERS_MOST_THIS_WEEK].

Format: [SECTIONS_YOU_WANT, e.g. Decisions, Owners and deadlines, Open questions].

Length: [CAP, e.g. one bullet per item, under 150 words total].

Exclusions: [WHAT_TO_LEAVE_OUT, e.g. anything marked OFF_RECORD].

A complete example, filled in

This is a working AI meeting notes prompt for a weekly status meeting. Paste your raw notes under it, in the same message.

You are the project coordinator for a weekly 30-minute status meeting.

Summarize the meeting notes I paste below into a recap.

Context: attendees are [TEAM]; decisions matter more than discussion; the meeting date is [DATE].

Format: three sections, Decisions, Owners and deadlines, and Open questions, each as bullet points.

Length: one bullet per item, under 150 words total.

Exclusions: leave out anything marked [OFF_RECORD], and keep wording from the notes where possible.

Every line does one job. Naming the format before the notes get pasted matters because the tool sorts while it reads instead of summarizing first and restructuring after. Naming the length keeps the recap inside what a busy team reads.

An output checklist for AI meeting notes

Run the recap through five checks. Each one targets a failure mode generic summaries share.

  • Decisions captured: every decision in the notes appears in the Decisions section.
  • Owners named: each action item has a person attached, never "someone".
  • Dates attached: every deadline reads as a date, not "next week".
  • Open questions separated: unresolved items sit in their own section, not folded into decisions.
  • Off-record stays out: anything marked private is absent.

A recap that fails a check goes back with the fix pointed at, not a rewritten prompt. "Keep the structure, add dates to the Owners section" reruns in seconds.

Four opinions on meeting-notes prompts

These are opinions from writing and reusing prompts across projects, not survey data and not claims about any specific tool.

  1. (Opinion) Name the format before you paste the notes. "Summarize my meeting" invites whatever shape the tool feels like that day. Three named sections get you a recap you can scan in ten seconds.
  2. (Opinion) Ask for decisions, not discussion. A recap mostly needs what was decided and who owns it. Saying "decisions matter more than discussion" moves the tool's attention to the right lines, and it is the cheapest two-line fix for bloated output.
  3. (Opinion) Cap the length at what you read. A 400-word recap after a 30-minute meeting goes unread. Under 150 words works for a weekly status meeting; if the cap keeps truncating decisions, your meeting needs two prompts, not a longer cap.
  4. (Opinion) Keep prompts that worked. Save the exact wording that produced a usable recap and change only the brackets next time. A saved template beats memory inside a week, which is the whole reason this page is built around fill-in lines.
