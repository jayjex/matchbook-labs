Freelance Payment Terms Explained (Free Guide)

Every invoice carries a line that says when the money should arrive. This guide covers the labels freelancers actually write there: due on receipt, Net 7, Net 15, Net 30, and 50% upfront. The five common terms, one line each

TermWhat it says on the invoice
Due on receiptPayment is owed the day the client receives the invoice.
Net 7Payment is owed 7 calendar days from the invoice date.
Net 15Payment is owed 15 calendar days from the invoice date.
Net 30Payment is owed 30 calendar days from the invoice date.
50% upfrontHalf the total is paid before work starts and the rest on delivery, with the balance due under the term you pick for the second half.

Whichever label you use, most invoices also carry two dates: the invoice date and the due date. Writing the actual due date next to the label removes the counting step for whoever pays you.

When an invoice is late. An invoice counts as late once its due date has passed with no payment recorded against it.

The usual first step is mechanical. Resend the same invoice with a one-line note that names the invoice number and amount.

Until the payment lands, the invoice stays on your outstanding list, which is exactly why a running log beats memory.

Four opinions on picking terms

These are opinions from sending and tracking invoices, not survey data and not guidance on what your contract allows.

  1. (Opinion) Pick fewer terms, not more. One or two windows across all clients are easier to track than five, because you only have to remember one due-date rule when a client asks, and your log stays comparable from invoice to invoice.
  2. (Opinion) Write the calendar due date next to the label. "Due Oct 14" tells the payer the day without counting 30 days from the invoice date, and it gives you a fixed date to check against your log instead of a calculation you redo every time.
  3. (Opinion) Match the window to how you invoice, not to the client's size. Due on receipt or Net 7 fits small, one-shot jobs where you want the loop closed fast; Net 30 fits monthly or milestone billing where invoices already arrive on a rhythm. Choosing by job shape keeps the decision simple, and the client gets the same answer every time.
  4. (Opinion) Log the invoice the day you send it. A row with a status dropdown of Draft, Sent, Overdue, or Paid makes the late one visible without checking your email, and the outstanding total updates itself instead of living in your head.
