The "why" column is the one people skip and then need in October, when the goal looks arbitrary. Write it while the reason is fresh.

Pick one word for the year

A single word works as a tiebreaker all year: when two goals compete for the same Saturday, the word decides. Write yours in the box. The five words below are examples of the kind that hold up, labeled as examples because yours should come from your own list of goals above.

My word for the year
Example wordThe situation it fits
SteadyPlans that tend to start hot and stop by February. Steady picks the boring repeatable option.
FewerCalendars and budgets with too much in them. Fewer means cutting commitments, not adding one more.

The Q1 checkpoint

End of March, answer these three in writing. Honest answers include "drop this goal", which counts as progress: the checkpoint exists to kill goals that were never going to happen.

1. Which goal actually moved, and what caused the movement?

2. Which first step never happened? Was the goal wrong, or the step too big?

3. What changes for Q2: the goal, the first step, or the habit?

Review quarterly, adjust, restart

Nothing on this page requires January. The grid works in March the same as in the first week of the year, and the point of the quarterly loop is that goals are drafts: review at the end of each quarter, keep what's working, adjust the goal or shrink the first step, restart the habit tracker. Four adjusted quarters beat one perfect list made on January 1st, because a list you revisit is the only kind that lasts a whole year.
