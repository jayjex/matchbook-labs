Receipt Organizer Printable

A receipt outlives its own memory: the amount survives, the reason fades, and by the time a number gets questioned the slip is one of fifty in a drawer. This page gives receipts one place to land, on paper. Print two pages free: a 12-row receipt log where you write the date, a merchant alias, the amount and a category in your own words, then circle filed when the slip is put away. Page one is the log: one row per receipt, written the day it lands in your pocket, date first, then a merchant alias, then the amount, then a category, then circle filed when the slip goes in the envelope. Page two closes the month: total each category into the table, then fill the envelope box so the month's paper has one address. Print a fresh pair for each month.

Page one: the receipt log, 12 rows

Twelve rows covers a normal month of kept receipts for a one-person shop, and a page you can finish beats a shoebox you avoid opening. Nothing is pre-filled: no sample slips, no amounts someone else made up. The merchant column takes an alias, because the slip itself already carries the full legal name, and a short name is enough to match a row to a slip on your own desk. The category cell is blank on purpose, so the sorting words are yours instead of a fixed list you have to translate. The filed circle does one job: a row with no circle is a receipt still loose somewhere, and one glance down the column shows how many.

Four opinions on running the log

Opinions from building record-keeping pages like this one. Take or leave:

  1. (Opinion.) File the receipt the same day you write the row. The row and the filing are one task split in two: done together it is a minute at the counter, done apart it is a pile that grows on its own schedule.
  2. (Opinion.) Alias the merchant on paper. The slip in the envelope carries the full legal name already, so the log only needs enough to match a row to a slip. Initials or a shortened name do that on your own desk.
  3. (Opinion.) Write the category as you log, not at month end. A category named at the counter costs two seconds. Sixty rows sorted from memory cost an evening and produce guesses that all read "store".
  4. (Opinion.) One envelope per month, and count it against the rows once a week. When the count and the rows disagree, the gap is one week wide and the missing slip is still traceable. Left a month, it is archaeology.
