Home Warranty Tracker, Free to Print

Ten warranties, two pages, pen first. This free printable home warranty tracker keeps the paperwork facts where you can find them: page 1 is a ten-row warranty list with the item, the date you bought it, when it expires, who provides it, and a claim circle to mark Y or N as claims happen. Page 1: the warranty table

Ten rows, one item each. The expires column carries the page: bought dates rarely matter twice, but an expiry date matters on the day it arrives, so copy it exactly as the paper prints it. The provider column takes the company name the paperwork uses, which is the name you would look up or write to. The claim column starts blank and stays blank until a claim happens, then gets its circle. A row where every cell is filled from the paper is a row you never have to second-guess.

Page 2: the two boxes

The renewal-soon box holds four lines, each pairing an item with the expiry date you copied over from page 1. When a date passes or a paper gets renewed, the line gets a new one; the box works as the short list you actually look at, with the full table as the backup behind it. The serial-numbers box holds three lines: item, serial, and where the paperwork lives. Serials get copied at purchase time, when the label is new and readable, and the "where noted" line is what turns the number into something findable. Four and three are just sizes; if the house holds more, print two copies.

Four opinions about keeping a home warranty tracker

  1. (Opinion.) Check the expiration dates on the last day of each quarter. The last day of every third month is a standing date to walk down the expires column and refresh the renewal-soon box. Quarterly beats "when something breaks", because a date spotted after it passes is history and a date spotted a month out can go on a calendar.
  2. (Opinion.) Copy dates from the paper, never from the sticker or your memory. The date on the fridge magnet and the date on the receipt disagree more often than anyone expects. On this sheet the receipt wins, and the expires column means what the paper means.
  3. (Opinion.) Let the claim circles build a history. Circle Y the day a claim goes in, even a small one. Ten rows of Y's and N's reread like a record; a column left blank "until it matters" reads like a guess.
  4. (Opinion.) Write where the paper lives next to the serial. A serial number without a location sends you back through every drawer in the house. Item, serial, drawer: the three-line box turns "it's in here somewhere" into two minutes.

What this sheet does not do

A record sheet, household paperwork on paper. It holds the items, dates, providers, claim history, and serials you copy onto it, and nothing else: no coverage decisions, no home valuation, no repair instructions, and no filing help. What any warranty covers is the text of that warranty; the sheet keeps track of where the text lives.
