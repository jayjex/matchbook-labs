Two sets of scrambled words, fourteen rows, one pencil. A generator script did the scrambling and then checked every row: the letters always rebuild into the answer, and no scramble ever spells the answer by accident. 4 tips that crack scrambles

What works on rows like these:

  • (Opinion.) Write the vowels in one row and the consonants under them. The skeleton usually names the word before you place a single letter. REBEZE stops fighting the moment two E's line up.
  • (Opinion.) Take the short rows first. CULE and IABLI fall in seconds, and a fast win settles you down before the eleven-letter row. Order costs nothing; use it.
  • (Opinion.) Spend the hint before you spend the answer block. A hint narrows the word without spelling it. The key ends the row, which is a worse trade when you were one swap away.
  • (Opinion.) Print two copies and race someone. Scrambles sharpen with a deadline, and the medium set runs close enough in difficulty to keep it fair.

How the scrambles were generated

A word scramble generator, a short Python script with a fixed seed, produced every scramble on this page. After each shuffle it ran four checks before the row could ship: the scrambled letters must be the same letters as the answer (so every row is solvable), the scramble must not spell the answer itself, no scramble may spell a different row's answer, and all fourteen scrambles must be distinct. The hints were checked too: none of them contains its answer word. The script graded the puzzles, not your guesses, so if a row ever refuses to resolve, the letters are the problem, not you.
