How to do a dot to dot (4 steps):

1. Find dot 1. That is always the start. The orange numbers run in order from there.

2. Draw a line from 1 to 2, then 2 to 3, and keep going to the last number. Saying the numbers out loud while drawing helps younger kids hold the order.

3. After the last dot, draw one more line back to dot 1. That closes the shape.

4. Color the finished picture. A faint dashed outline sits under the dots as a hint; ignore it if you want the surprise.

Ages 4 to 7: what to expect

At 4, the counting does the teaching and the picture is the payoff. Expect slow number hunting, lines that miss dots, and a star with wobbly corners; all of that is normal and none of it breaks the exercise. At 5 and 6, both puzzles usually finish in one quiet sitting, and the house door is the only spot that draws a second attempt. At 7, the challenge flips: ask them to draw the house in reverse order, from dot 9 back down to dot 1, and the same puzzle turns into a working-memory game. Ten dots is a small number on paper, but it sits right at the edge of what a 4-year-old tracks without losing count, which is exactly why dot to dots work at this age.

3 tips for dot to dot sessions

These are opinions from running printable puzzles with small kids, not a curriculum.

  1. Count out loud on the first pass. Quiet counting hides a skipped number until the picture comes out wrong and the kid is frustrated. Out loud, you catch the skip while the pencil is still one line behind.
  2. Cover the numbers you are not on yet. A sticky note over dots 5 onward turns the puzzle into one number at a time for a 4-year-old. Peel it forward as they go. By the star's second run they will not need it.
  3. Print two copies. The first copy absorbs the wobbles and the wrong turns without anyone caring. The second copy is the one that goes on the fridge, and it usually looks good enough to keep.
